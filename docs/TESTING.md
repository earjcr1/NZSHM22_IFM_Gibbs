# CRU workflow: testing guide

This guide covers the current code set: spike-and-slab (the default prior), one `FLOOR` setting (1e-10 by default; 1e-12 in late October 2026) serving as spike-and-slab's slab floor and the diagnostic cutoff, one measurement per slip-rate section (`SLIP_SECTIONS = 'auto'`), the MFD bins' uncertainties as the inputs give them (`MFD_UNCERTAINTY = 'as-given'`; `'two-measurements'` counts the bins as the two measurements they are projected from), positivity, every prediction above zero (`BAND = 'positive'`; the band, every prediction within a factor 10 of its target, `BAND = 10`, for a few days before), the conservative preset, with no balance term (`DOWN_THE_MIDDLE = False`), and running summaries in place of stored draws. θ = 1 (the alternative until October 2026), the square-root slab (`--slab sqrt`, the cross-check until the end of October 2026) and the other priors tried (the per-bin boxes, the earlier default, and the linear and halfway priors), all `gibbs.py` options for the command line, still run but are no longer part of these tests. Figures marked as estimates have not been measured. Figures marked "counting each subsection" were measured before one measurement per section became the default, with every slip-rate subsection its own measurement (now `SLIP_SECTIONS = 'none'`). The findings behind these checks are in `REPORT.md`, and the sampler design in `sampler-design.md`.

## Before any run

Run the self-tests once per machine and after any change to `gibbs.py`, and check the cleaning output against the reference values below whenever the inputs change.

- **Self-tests:** the notebook's `RUN_SELFTESTS` runs `python tools/gibbs.py --selftest current` the cases the notebook's default uses, with six verdicts, all `PASSED`: per-coordinate lower edges (the slab floor), per-coordinate upper edges (the soft top), spike-and-slab, the balance term (`DOWN_THE_MIDDLE`, both weightings), swap moves under a log-box prior, and the band (`BAND`; spike-and-slab, a log-box prior with swaps, the linear prior). After any change to `gibbs.py`, run the full `python gibbs.py --selftest` (about 3.5 minutes), with ten verdicts: the main suite, the retired hierarchical prior, per-coordinate lower edges, per-coordinate upper edges, spike-and-slab with each slab, the linear prior, the halfway prior, the balance term and the band. The main suite includes the swap and transfer moves; their helper now lives in `cru_common.py`, so the tests run with only the current scripts present. The retired scripts' self-tests are no longer part of the routine.
- **Randomized best fits:** `python tools/randomized_nnls.py --selftest` (about a second; the notebook's `RUN_SELFTESTS` runs it in place of `gibbs.py`'s when `RANDOMIZED_NNLS` is set) must say `PASSED`: for a full-rank problem with every rate well above 0, 400 fits scatter as the posterior under a flat prior would (variances within 9% of σ²(GᵀG)⁻¹), and the fits are the same in one process and in two.
- **Row groups:** with `BLOCKS = None`, the slip-rate and magnitude-bin rows are detected automatically; `check_bin_rows` and `check_slip_rows` must pass, and any group you name is checked against detection.
- **Slip-rate sections:** cleaning prints the sections it found (runs of copies of one slip rate), with notes on targets shared between sections and on neighbours with identical targets that share no rupture, and the fit check. On new inputs, compare the sections with the fault model; if they disagree, give a mapping file (`SLIP_SECTIONS = 'file.txt'`, lines of `original_row section`).
- **Collective scale:** the preliminaries' line must say the floor (1e-10 by default) is below the collective negligibility scale.
- **Row-block fit and MFD helpers:** `python tools/block_fit.py --selftest` (seconds) must pass after any change to it or to the problem helpers in `cru_common.py`. It checks the block measurements and the misfit split, and the MFD uncertainty: the bins counted in magnitude order (a removed bin leaving a gap), the M7.0–7.1 bin found, each setting's multiplier, the inputs' power recovered, what the bins say about the MFD as a whole, the bin rows divided and every other row unchanged, and bad settings refused.
- **MFD uncertainty:** cleaning prints the inputs' bin uncertainties (10.0% at M7.0–7.1, from 9.8% to 15.4%, growing as R_j^−0.250, exactly) and what the bins say about the MFD as a whole (±2.8% on the total rate and ±0.024 in b; with `'two-measurements'`, ×3.000, then ±8.3% and ±0.071). On new inputs, check the power and the M7.0–7.1 bin; a warning that rupture sizes do not rise with the bins' order means the magnitudes, and the bin a number refers to, may be wrong.

## Run lengths, timing and storage

The very conservative preset is the production setting: three replicates of 16 chains.

| Preset | Spike-and-slab | θ (comparison only) |
|---|---|---|
| Quick | 4 chains × 2,400 sweeps (burn 400, thin 8), 2 replicates | the same |
| Conservative | 8 × 30,000 (burn 3,000, thin 32), 2 replicates | 8 × 20,000 (burn 4,000, thin 16), 2 replicates |
| Very conservative | 16 × 120,000 (burn 10,000, thin 32), 3 replicates | 16 × 100,000 (burn 20,000, thin 16), 3 replicates |
| Very conservative on the M2 | 4 h 14 min at 211k (measured, late October 2026; about 4.6 h estimated) and 11–12 h at 5e5 (an estimate; about 10% more with the square-root slab); 1 h 20 min at 2e4 with the earlier lengths and kernel (measured) | 4.1 h at 2e4 (measured, before October 2026's faster kernel) |

Spike-and-slab's conservative and very conservative lengths were set in October 2026 for 2e5–5e5 ruptures, from the 211k conservative run (then 8 × 20,000, burn 4,000, thin 16). Its effective draws per recorded draw were within 5–20% of the 2e4 run's at the same preset: about 0.9–1.0 for the total rate, 0.8 for the 5th percentile of the rupture rates, 0.45–0.5 for χ² and 0.19–0.26 for the slowest fraction on. Its chains reached their stationary level within about 30 sweeps of the best-fit start. The fractions on cleared their check (each fraction's effective draws at least a fifth of the total rate's) only narrowly, at 0.21–0.26, and recording every 32 sweeps instead of 16 makes their draws nearly independent too. Projected for the new lengths: about 6,700 recorded draws per replicate at the conservative preset and 55,000 at the very conservative one, with effective draws for the total rate about 3 and 2.5 times their targets and for the rupture rates (5th percentile) about 5 and 4 times. θ's lengths are unchanged: from about 2e5 ruptures its boxes (n_j / ln 10 decades, up to about 26,000 at 211k) are deeper than its slice sampler can cross in any affordable run, since each update moves a rate by at most about 28 decades and the best fit's zeros start at the boxes' floors, so θ there needs a change to the sampler first.

The optional linear prior mixes much faster: its chains settle within 8 sweeps of the best fit, and draws 8 sweeps apart are nearly independent (the 5th percentile of the rupture rates' effective draws is about 0.8 per draw), but every rate moves in every sweep, so a sweep costs about three times spike-and-slab's. Its presets are shorter: quick as for the other priors, conservative 8 × 4,000 (burn 400, thin 8; about 3,600 draws per replicate), very conservative 16 × 16,000 (burn 400, thin 8; about 31,000), at 211,000 ruptures about 4.5 and 30 minutes per replicate (estimates from 67 and 112 ms per sweep). The optional halfway prior mixes as fast and has the same presets, but its sweeps, slice updates of every rate, cost about six times spike-and-slab's: about 9 and 70 minutes per conservative and very conservative replicate at 211,000 ruptures (estimates from 140 and 262 ms per sweep).

The notebook's `PRESETS` gives each preset's burn-in and thinning. Spike-and-slab's time was measured with `MFD_UNCERTAINTY = 'two-measurements'`, stored draws and 50 representatives: 60.5 min of sampling for the three replicates (18.5 min each, 0.011 s per sweep for 16 chains on 4 threads), then 20 min of checks and tables, 19.5 of them in the convergence and table cells, which read the stored draws (17.5 GB) when they exist. Its sweeps cost about a fifth of θ's in the quick runs. These times predate October 2026's faster bookkeeping and kernel (below), and without stored draws, the default since then, the checks and tables come from the running summaries.

- **Kernel at 211,000 ruptures** (the M2's performance cores, short runs): under spike-and-slab 25 ms per sweep for 8 chains on 4 threads and 43 ms for 16 (41 and 86 ms before October 2026), under θ 150 ms for 16 chains (338 ms). The linear prior takes 67 ms per sweep for 8 chains and 112 ms for 16, and the halfway prior 140 and 262 ms, recording every 8 sweeps. The square-root slab took 24 ms per sweep for 8 chains, against 21–22 ms for the log-uniform slab in alternating runs alongside it: an off rupture's slab value costs no logarithm there (it is drawn down from the top as e^z_top (1 − u f)²), but more of the draws land on the soft top's shoulder, which takes a rejection step. Since then each thread moves its chains through the ruptures together, summing each column's products for up to four of them in one pass; per-rupture constants are computed once per run; and under θ the slice sampler takes the stepping-out steps that a bound shows would pass without evaluating them (about 1 evaluation per update instead of about 61). Every chain's arithmetic and random numbers are unchanged: fixed-seed runs of every prior, with 3 to 16 chains on 1 to 4 threads, gave bit-identical output, and the self-tests the same report. Everything included, a short run with the conservative preset's 8 chains went from 47 s to 30 s. The conservative spike-and-slab run at 211k took 29.7 minutes of sampling for its two replicates before the change (about 19 expected now) and 1.5 minutes of checks and tables; a very conservative one, at its present lengths, was estimated at about 1.5 hours per replicate and 4.6 for three, and took 77–85 minutes per replicate (0.038 s per sweep for 16 chains on 4 threads, swap passes and bookkeeping included) and 4 h 14 min in all (late October 2026, `DOWN_THE_MIDDLE = 'zero-model'`, 100 representatives); 11–12 hours for three at 5e5 (an estimate). In quick runs at 2e4, with one chain per thread, the gain is small.
- **The band at 211,000 ruptures** (16 chains on 4 threads, 960 sweeps, swap passes and bookkeeping included): 0.091 s per sweep when every rupture's band limits were found at each update, 0.045 s since early November 2026, when an off rupture's are found only if it switches on (bit-identical output), against 0.049 s without the band. The band's start: 400 s, then 94 s with each rate in units of 1/‖G_i‖ and a stop once inside 10^0.95 of every target, both at χ² 93.4–93.5; once per problem.
- **Bookkeeping at 211,000 ruptures** (16 chains on 4 threads, measured in short runs): under spike-and-slab, redrawing the fractions on takes under 0.5 ms per sweep and the running summaries 110 ms per recorded draw (26 ms and 290 ms early in October 2026, when both worked on every rate; the summaries now use each chain's nonzero rates, and the kernel counts each bin's ruptures on, with bit-identical results), against 43 ms per sweep in the kernel; under θ, 180 ms per recorded draw (430 ms), against 150 ms per sweep. For a very conservative spike-and-slab replicate that is about 12 minutes of bookkeeping instead of about 70.
- **Draws (`gibbs.py` without `--no-samples`, from the command line; the notebook stores none):** 5.9 GB per run at 2e4; 68 GB at 211,000 ruptures. Check the free disk space first (`df -h`): at 211,000 ruptures a single very conservative replicate's draws take 65 GB.
- **Without draws:** every notebook check, table and rate vector come from the running summaries; only `compare_runs.py`, `compare_priors.py` and the top-contact check need stored draws.
- **Kept draws (`REPRESENTATIVES` above 0):** 1,000 whole draws per run, with or without `--no-samples`, 73 MB per run at 2e4 and about 2 GB at 5e5 (float32), plus their pooled copy in `rate_vectors.npz`.
- **Memory:** the running log-rate histogram takes 1,651 bins × 4 bytes per rupture, about 1.4 GB at 211,000 ruptures.

The runs are long because short tests suggested the slowest ruptures mixed poorly under both priors. In the very conservative θ run the 5th-percentile effective draws were four times the target, so χ²'s R̂, not the per-rupture target, sets the length.

## Convergence

The verdict (`check_convergence.py`, which the notebook runs; its report is `convergence_report.txt` in the prior's folder) says `PASS` when the targets below are met, reports the slow-mixing minority separately, and compares the replicates rupture by rupture.

| Target | Quick | Conservative | Very conservative |
|---|---|---|---|
| Effective draws, 95% of ruptures' log rates | 100 | 1,000 | 10,000 |
| Effective draws, total rate | 200 | 2,000 | 20,000 |
| R̂ of χ² and the total rate, below | 1.01 | 1.01 | 1.005 |

For spike-and-slab the verdict also needs R̂ below 1.01 for the number of ruptures on and every bin's fraction on, and at least a fifth of the total rate's effective draws for each fraction. A `CHECK` names the targets missed: run longer, and if χ²'s or the total's R̂ stays high, try dispersed starts.

- **Diagnostic cutoff:** per-rupture R̂ and effective draws use log rates with everything below 10^`FLOOR` (1e-10 by default) counted as equal. Runs scored at another cutoff are not directly comparable: scored at 1e-12 rather than 1e-10, the same θ draws flag about a quarter more ruptures as slow-mixing (259–288 instead of 195–243 in quick runs), while spike-and-slab's figures barely change (REPORT.md, "Floors and cutoff").
- **Noise baseline:** the worst R̂ that independent draws would give, about 1.0003 at the very conservative preset and 1.015 at the quick one. The table shows R̂ to four decimals so it is visible.
- **Slow-mixing minority:** ruptures with R̂ above 1.01, or above the baseline if higher, reported with their share of the total rate and flagged in the rupture table (`slow_mixing`). Expected under both priors: with one measurement per section, quick runs had 59–63 (0.3%) under spike-and-slab, carrying 16–18% of the total rate, 109–119 (0.6%) with the square-root slab, carrying 24–25%, and 195–243 (1.1–1.3%) under θ, carrying 23–24%. In the very conservative θ run (counting each subsection), 821–856 ruptures (4.5–4.7%) sat above the baseline and the worst R̂ was 1.35–1.38. Longer runs help them only slowly (doubling the sweeps would bring 1.36 to about 1.19). Under spike-and-slab at 211,000 ruptures they help more: the conservative run of the configuration adopted in late October 2026 had 26–36 per replicate above 1.01 (42 in all, carrying 7.5% of the total rate; the worst 1.11), and its very conservative run 5–6 (8 in all, 1.9%; the worst 1.022), whose chains, judged from its kept draws, agree on each rupture's whole distribution as perfectly mixed chains would (REPORT.md, "The reference run at 211,000 ruptures").
- **Replicate agreement:** standardized differences of each rupture's mean log rate should have sd near 1, about 0.27% beyond 3, and about the expected number beyond 4 (about 1.2 for 18,000 ruptures); the very conservative θ run gave 0.988–0.996, 0.22–0.25% and none. Ruptures that never rise above the cutoff in either run (spike-and-slab's always-off ruptures) are left out and counted.

Because each rupture's difference is scaled by its own Monte Carlo error, replicate agreement also tests whether the slow ruptures' stated errors hold.

## Checklist after each run

Work through these in order; the notebook prints them all, and each step also keeps its report next to its outputs: items 1–5 are in `convergence_report.txt` (and the log), item 6 in `block_fit_report.txt`, item 7 in `tables_report.txt` and `bins_posterior.csv`, and the rest in the figures' `.txt` tables and `select_report.txt`, all in the prior's folder.

1. **Log:** search the run's `workflow_log_<date>-<time>.txt` for "Warning", "Error" and "Traceback"; in Jupyter it also shows each cell's duration. With the band, each run's log (`<run>_log.txt`) says how many rows the start left outside it and whether the start inside it was found (the first run, with the penalized fit's steps, ending at 0 rows outside) or taken from `cru_clean_band<F>_start.npz` (later runs on the same problem).
2. **Verdict:** `PASS` at the conservative and very conservative presets; for a `CHECK`, follow its named reasons.
3. **Slow-mixing minority:** note its count and its share of the total rate. If it carries a material share of a product you will use, check those ruptures' `slow_mixing` flags and effective draws before relying on them.
4. **Replicate agreement:** as in Convergence.
5. **Fit check:** χ² against the value cleaning prints, 126 ± 16 on the corrected inputs (without the band; under the band, the default since early November 2026, χ² sits below it, at 211,000 ruptures 438 ± 20 against 590 ± 34, −4.4 sd, since the band is stricter than the data where a target is weak); in quick runs spike-and-slab sat about 1 sd above it, with either slab (as did θ). With `SLIP_SECTIONS = 'none'` it is 446 ± 80, and they sat 2.6–2.8 sd below it.
6. **Row-block fit** (after the fit check): each block's χ² per independent measurement near 1; in quick runs 1.04 for the slip-rate sections, 1.00 for the bins and 1.29 for the paleo rows (z +0.2, 0.0 and +1.3). The bins' misfit is nearly all spread, the model's freedom, so it says little about their uncertainties. The "beyond the freedom" line compares the blocks' residuals once the freedom is set aside (slip rates 1.05 ± 0.21, paleo 1.51 ± 0.45, overall 1.20 in quick runs): a block further from the overall than its ± would point at its uncertainties. The bins' width is `MFD_UNCERTAINTY`'s, and their misfit, nearly all spread, cannot test it; the report's MFD line says so.
7. **Bin totals:** a median about 2% over their targets (at most 10%), near the best fit's 1–5%. Much larger departures would point to pressure from the prior or tension in the data.
8. **Top contact** (needs stored draws; `compare_priors.py` prints it over every draw; measured counting each subsection): under θ about 45% of draws have a rupture above its target, by up to about two of the bin's uncertainties; under spike-and-slab about 80%, by up to about three. Much more would point to tension in the data.
9. **Rates by magnitude** (`fig_rate_magnitude.png`, with its table in `.txt`): the order check should say the median number of subsections per rupture rises with magnitude (on the corrected inputs from 3 in the lowest bin to 113 in the highest, rank correlation +1.00); otherwise check the bin order before reading magnitudes off the figure. Each orange dot (the bin's total of the means) sits against its target line: in quick runs about 2% above (at most 10%). The lower panel shows the same totals over their targets, with the band within one uncertainty and each total's posterior 16–84% range as a bar (from the runs' recorded predictions; none for the best fit's figure): in the 211,000-rupture reference run the target lies inside the bar for 12 of the 18 bins, and the six bins from M8.0 sit above theirs (by 15–33% at the median). The bottom band (means below 10^`FLOOR`, including exactly 0) held 32 ruptures under spike-and-slab and none under θ (quick runs at 1e-10); the shorter the run, the more stay there, because a rarely switched-on rupture's mean comes only from its visits. The table's second part counts each bin's ruptures by decade of mean rate, from below 10^`FLOOR` (including 0) to 1e-2 and above; each row sums to the bin's rupture count, and its first column matches the bottom band. In quick runs, over the columns from below 1e-10 up to 1e-4–1e-3: spike-and-slab 32, 118, 734, 4,363, 9,078, 3,671, 238 and 9 (none above 1e-3); θ 0, 0, 28, 3,354, 11,277, 3,340, 232 and 12; spike-and-slab with `'two-measurements'` 53, 168, 891, 4,331, 8,613, 3,907, 275 and 5. Longer runs empty the lowest columns: no very conservative run had a mean below 1e-8 (REPORT.md, "Summarizing the draws").
10. **Slip rates** (`fig_slip_rates.png`, with its table in `.txt`; in units of each section's measurement uncertainty; with kept draws, the default, each draw's section means are x's in place of the bars, and the same goes for the paleo and rates-by-magnitude figures): each section's mean prediction (a coloured dot) should lie near the 1:1 line, most within the grey band of one uncertainty, with its posterior 16–84% range as a bar. The bar is the model's spread, usually well under one measurement uncertainty, so a target outside it is no misfit in itself; in the 211,000-rupture reference run 37% of the targets lie inside their bars, and many weakly constrained sections' bars reach 0 (on the log scale, the bottom edge). In quick runs the mean model's χ² over the 388 slip-rate rows, as the likelihood weights them, was 51 under spike-and-slab and 53 under θ (72–75% of sections within one uncertainty, 97% within two); the best fit's is 17.0. This is the χ² of the posterior mean, below the mean over draws used in the fit check. 18 sections (72 subsections) are predicted below a quarter of their targets (the best fit: 6 sections), all with targets only 0.6–2.4 uncertainties above zero, so each stays within 2.3 uncertainties of its target; check them against the fault model. The best fit's own figure from cleaning (`fig_slip_rates_nnls.png` in `RUN_DIR/nnls/`) shows the same for the best nonnegative fit, for comparison.
11. **Paleo rates** (`fig_paleo_rates.png`, with its table in `.txt`; log-log, in units of each row's uncertainty, so a dot's distance from the 1:1 line is the ratio of prediction to target): most dots should lie within the bands, with the posterior 16–84% bars of their predictions reaching the 1:1 line. In quick runs the mean model's χ² over the 40 paleo rows was 34 under both priors (78% within one uncertainty, 92% within two; the best fit's 13.1), and most sites were predicted somewhat below their targets (median ratio 0.82–0.85), as in the best fit (0.94, 34 of 40 below). Furthest below were rows 860, 835, 853 and 861, about two uncertainties low. The best fit also leaves rows 860 and 861 at about half their targets, but nearly matches row 853, whose posterior median prediction is about 0: the ruptures that would reach it are off in most draws. Row 860 (and 863; 8916 and 8922 at 211k) is the paleo site on Hope: Conway, whose slip rate's stated uncertainty (0.2 mm/yr on 15.8, 3.2% as the inputs use it) is about five times tighter than any other section's, so every solution holds the section at its 12.64 mm/yr target; in a best fit with that uncertainty widened tenfold, the section rises to about 15.7 mm/yr and the site to about 0.6 of its target, while the bins, the total rate and every other section stay put (REPORT.md, open items). `fig_paleo_rates_nnls.png` in `RUN_DIR/nnls/` shows the best fit's own predictions, without bars.
12. **Rate vectors:** 18,648 rows, penalised ruptures 0, quantiles in order. Under spike-and-slab and θ most medians and modes are 0, so sum the means, never the medians or modes.
13. **Representative draws** (`select_report.txt`): the weighted means of the total rate and the bin totals within 2% of the exact means; the subsection totals' weighted means within 10%, and their weighted 16%, 50% and 84% points within 15% of those over every recorded draw, for 90% of subsections (and of bins); every representative's χ² and total rate inside the posterior's 1–99% range; no zero weight. These tolerances are proposals to revisit with the hazard team. The quick preset usually misses them; at the conservative preset and above, if 20 representatives miss and 40 meet them, use 40. In the first conservative test (spike-and-slab), both missed the bin and subsection tolerances by similar margins (REPORT.md, "Representative draws" under open items). With `'two-measurements'` the bins' posterior spread is about 30% per bin, three times the inputs', so 20 representatives match the bins' means less closely (the worst bin 16% off at the quick preset).
14. **Keep together:** the log, `workflow_config.json` and the result files of each result set.

## Sensitivity and regression checks

The strongest evidence that a result is data-determined is that it survives a change of prior; the strongest protection against breaking the code is a fixed-seed comparison.

- **The square-root slab against the default** (dropped at the end of October 2026: the request behind it had been misphrased). It was the main robustness check, run from the command line (`gibbs.py --slab sqrt`, with the notebook's other arguments). In quick runs the two agreed on the fit, the total rate, the bins and the moment rate, at 2e4 and at 211,000 ruptures, and at 2e4 on the subsection event rates (within about 7% for three-quarters of the subsections). At 211,000 ruptures the subsection event rates differ: under the square-root slab a median 0.62 of the default's (5–95%: 0.08–1.08; a longer run from zero agreed with a quick one from the best fit), so there they are partly the prior's (REPORT.md, "Fits and the prior's lower end"). θ = 1 played this role before it.
- **Slip-rate sections:** run the notebook again with `SLIP_SECTIONS = 'none'`, in its own `RUN_DIR`, to see what counting each subsection as its own measurement changes. It reproduces the earlier results exactly (for spike-and-slab χ² near 227 against 446 ± 80, where the default gives 140 against 126 ± 16). If the fault model's sections are available, a run with them as a mapping file checks the automatic detection.
- **MFD uncertainty:** run the notebook again with `MFD_UNCERTAINTY = 'two-measurements'`, in its own `RUN_DIR`, and, for a sense of scale, with a number (0.2 or 0.5, the M7.0–7.1 bin's relative uncertainty). In quick runs `'two-measurements'` raised the bins a median 8% above their targets (2% as given), the total rate by 5% and subsection totals by a median 4% (5–95%: −2% to +18%), and improved the paleo fit (χ² per measurement 1.22 against 1.29), with exceedance probabilities and the population within Monte Carlo noise; spike-and-slab and θ then disagree on how the four largest bins share their rate, though not on its sum. Single draws can then leave a whole bin empty: some bin fell below 1% of its target in 4.2–4.3% of the draws of a very conservative spike-and-slab run with `'two-measurements'`, and in none of the 16,000 draws of a conservative run as given, which matters wherever single draws are used, such as the representatives (REPORT.md, "MFD uncertainty").
- **Slab floor:** run the notebook again at another `FLOOR` (−8, say), in its own `RUN_DIR`. With one measurement per section, moving the slab floor from 1e-12 to 1e-10 changed every product by no more than Monte Carlo noise (subsection totals a median 0.1%, 5–95% −9% to +11%; exceedance at 1e-5 a median 0.02 in log₁₀) and only the number of ruptures switched on at undetectable rates, from about 497 to 379 per draw; counting each subsection, floors up to 1e-5 gave the same products, with about 53 fewer ruptures on per decade. With one setting, the second run's diagnostic cutoff moves too, so compare the products, not the diagnostics.
- **The band's factor:** run the notebook again with another `BAND` (3, say, or `None` for none), in its own `RUN_DIR`, and compare the fault-by-fault figures and the fit; at 211,000 ruptures a factor 10 changed the sections sharing ruptures with at most five others from 92% of draws without an event to none (REPORT.md, "The band").
- **Dispersed starts (`gibbs.py --init FILE.npy`, from the command line):** the most important untested item. Start one replicate from simulated-annealing solutions or perturbed best fits (the randomized best fits, say); its χ², totals and replicate agreement should match the replicates started from the best fit. A mismatch would mean separate modes, which the current replicate check cannot see.
- **Regression baseline after code changes:** keep one quick-preset spike-and-slab result set from fixed seeds. After any change, rerun with the same seeds and compare `rate_vectors.csv` and `bins_posterior.csv`; they should match byte for byte unless the change was meant to alter sampling. Then run `--selftest`.
- **Hazard at very low probabilities:** if hazard will be used at annual exceedance probabilities of 1e-5 or below, test the hazard itself at two slab floors. The prior sets the population of rates below detection, which none of the products above measures.

## Before 5e5

2. **Preliminaries on the new inputs:** row groups, slip-rate sections, best fit and the collective scale against the floor (1e-10 by default; with more ruptures per subsection the scale falls, and may come close to it). At 211k (2,696 × 204,839 after the penalty rows' removal, 9.3 million nonzeros) the collective scale was 4e-10, four times the floor. 301 soft zero-forcing rows survive that removal (REPORT.md, "Cleaning"); cleaning forces their ruptures to zero, leaving 2,395 × 204,538 with 65 paleo rows and a fit check of 590 ± 34. All of cleaning then takes 53 s on the M2, with at most 3 GB of memory: the best fit 25 s (1,443 outer iterations), the near-duplicate search 13 s (1.25 million pairs above 0.99) and the rest 15 s. The first 211k run took 22 minutes, before October 2026's sparse versions of the near-duplicate search (11 minutes then; it now compares each column only with the columns its cosine threshold allows, 6% of the pairs) and of the best fit (9.5 minutes then, on a dense copy of G; it now updates a QR factorization of the active columns). ## Known weak spots and next steps

The gaps that matter most are spike-and-slab's missing long run on the 2e4 inputs under the defaults (at 211,000 ruptures it is done), the untested dispersed starts and the slow minority under both priors.

- **Spike-and-slab, very conservative:** at 211,000 ruptures, run under the configuration adopted in late October 2026 (one measurement per section, the bins as given, `DOWN_THE_MIDDLE = 'zero-model'`, a slab floor of 1e-12; REPORT.md, "The reference run at 211,000 ruptures"): it passed in all three replicates in 4 h 14 min on the M2, with eight ruptures above R̂ 1.01 (the worst 1.022) carrying 1.9% of the total rate, replicate agreement as expected, and every product within Monte Carlo noise of the conservative run's. Not yet run on the corrected 2e4 inputs under the defaults; with `'two-measurements'` it took 1 h 20 min on the M2 and passed in all three replicates, with no more than one rupture above R̂ 1.01 (35–42 above the noise baseline, the worst 1.013) and replicate agreement as expected.
- **The existing very conservative θ run:** run `check_convergence.py theta/main_rep0 theta/main_rep1 theta/main_rep2 --preset very_conservative`, then `make_tables.py` on the same runs, from its `RUN_DIR` (or, in the notebook, re-run the configuration, helper, cleaning and loading cells, put `MAIN = RUNS = [f'theta/main_rep{r}' for r in range(3)]` in place of the runs cell, then run the convergence, results and figure cells). That gives the new verdict, the slow minority's share of the rate and the summary figure without re-sampling. Its 90%/99% counts will read "not available": its summaries predate them. It was run counting each subsection, so its χ² and products are for that setting.
- **Diagnostics and the cutoff:** compare slow-mixing counts only between runs scored at the same cutoff (Convergence above). The very conservative θ run kept no draws, so it can only be scored at the cutoff it was run with.
- **The slow minority:** a few percent of ruptures under θ, and fewer under spike-and-slab, switch on and off slowly at any affordable length. The remedy is moves that pass the active role between alternative ruptures, not longer runs.
- **Top contact without draws:** not recorded in the running summaries; adding a per-draw maximum of rate over target would make the check available at 5e5.
- **Slab-floor evidence:** one short run per floor on the 2e4 inputs; differences smaller than the seed noise (about ±10% on subsection totals) would go unseen.
- **Fit check:** with one measurement per section, spike-and-slab and θ pass it (about 1 sd above). It assumes each section's slip rate is uniform along it, rests on the provisional uncertainties, and counts the bins one each whatever their width; review these before treating it as a hard test.
- **MFD uncertainty level:** a modelling judgment the data cannot make, since the bins' misfit is the model's freedom. The default keeps the inputs' design value, under which the bins pin the MFD's total rate to ±2.8%, leaving its spread to the tree's N and b branches; `'two-measurements'` applies the design value to the MFD as a whole instead. A correlated covariance (the two measurements' within-branch uncertainty plus a model error per bin) would be the fuller treatment. Looser levels also let single draws leave whole bins empty: in very conservative spike-and-slab runs, some bin fell below 1% of its target in about 4% of the draws with `'two-measurements'` and in 81% at `MFD_UNCERTAINTY = 1`, where an empty bin costs at most about 1 in χ² and some bin sat at exactly 0 in 41% of the draws.
- **Slip-rate sections:** detected from identical targets and coefficients; a mapping file from the fault model would settle the 32 sections that share a target with another and the 6 identical-target neighbour pairs that share no rupture. A geodetic slip-rate model, varying along each fault, is left unweighted; if its errors are correlated along a fault, it would need a covariance.
- **Sparse sections:** with one measurement per section, both priors leave 18 sections (72 subsections) below a quarter of their targets; check them against the fault model.
- **Representative draws' tolerances:** the proposed subsection tolerances look out of reach for 20–40 representatives under spike-and-slab, whose subsection totals vary widely from draw to draw (a third with a coefficient of variation above 0.45); revisit them with the hazard team, or calibrate the weights on the subsection and bin totals directly.
- **Retired tools:** the HMC cross-check and the evidence tests are set aside (`cru_retired_scripts.zip`); restore them if either is wanted, or compute evidence by hand with a few tempered runs.
