# CRU 2e4 configuration: rupture rates under log-uniform priors

Development run in a claude.ai sandbox (1 CPU core, 4 GB RAM), October 2026. Inputs: `cru_A.txt` (G) and `cru_D.txt` (d), with σ = 1 for every row (the rows are already normalized by their uncertainties). Every number below comes from the scripts in this folder and can be regenerated with the commands at the end. Timings are for one core; the M2 runs chains in parallel.

The corrected inputs clean to 446 rows (the effective number of constraints) and 18,243 rupture rates; the earlier inputs, used for much of the analysis below, cleaned to 447 × 18,356. Every χ² is on the cleaned rows.

**How to read the numbers.** The defaults are spike-and-slab, one measurement per slip-rate section, and the MFD bins' uncertainties as the inputs give them; since early November 2026 they also include positivity, every prediction above zero (`BAND = 'positive'`; "Positivity", after "The band", which describes the band, every prediction within a factor 10 of its target, `BAND = 10`, the default for a few days before), with no balance term, a slab floor of 1e-10 and the conservative preset. The reference run at 211,000 ruptures ("The reference run at 211,000 ruptures") was made before that, with the balance term's per-type weights, a slab floor of 1e-12 and no band, and most quick-run numbers below without the band, at a slab floor of 1e-10, without the balance term or with its common weight. The Summary and "Slip-rate sections" give their results on the corrected inputs; "MFD uncertainty" gives what counting the bins as the two measurements they are projected from would change; the sections on spike-and-slab and θ explain the two priors and the tests behind them. Many of the analyses further down were made earlier, on the earlier inputs, under box priors and counting each slip-rate subsection as its own measurement; their numbers are as measured, and are marked where it matters.

**Default model.**

- **Prior:** spike-and-slab per magnitude bin (`--prior spikeslab`, `gibbs.py`'s default and the notebook's only prior). Each rupture is either off, with a rate of exactly 0, or on, with a log-uniform rate from a slab floor at 1e-10 (`FLOOR`; 1e-12 in late October 2026) up to a soft top at its bin's target rate R_j, whose width is the bin's own uncertainty. The fraction of ruptures switched on is estimated in each bin under a Beta(1, 1) prior, so the data, not a floor or a depth, decide how many ruptures are active, and the prior puts no pressure on the bin totals.
- **Cross-check:** none is run routinely. The square-root slab (`--slab sqrt`, a `gibbs.py` option for the command line: the same spike-and-slab with an on rupture's rate uniform in √rate, density m^−1/2 instead of 1/m, so small rates cost more and fewer ruptures are on) took that role in October 2026 and was dropped at the end of the month, the request behind it having been misphrased; its results stay on record ("Fits and the prior's lower end"). θ = 1 (`--prior binbox-theta:1:0`, a `gibbs.py` option: per-bin log-uniform boxes with depths chosen so that the prior puts no pressure on the bin totals) played the role before it and is kept for comparison only.
- **Likelihood:** Gaussian, exp(−χ²/2) with the given σ, for every prior, with every prediction kept above zero by default (`BAND = 'positive'`; `gibbs.py --positive`), a hard constraint, or instead within a factor 10 of its target (the band, `BAND = 10`; "The band"), and, from the command line, an MFD that never rises where its targets do not (`gibbs.py --mfd-monotonic`; the notebook's `MFD_MONOTONIC`, on by default, from early November 2026 until November 4th). Optionally a balance term (`DOWN_THE_MIDDLE`; `gibbs.py --down-the-middle`, on by default in late October 2026): each data type's relative shortfall, summed predictions against summed targets, costs A s² with one A for all types (`True`) or each type's own A, its zero-model misfit (`'zero-model'`) ("Balance between over- and underprediction"). Each run's χ² distribution, over the data rows alone, serves as a fit check. The χ² cap, an optional model class that kept only models with χ² at most a threshold, was removed in October 2026 ("Earlier priors").
- **MFD bins:** their uncertainties as the inputs give them (`preliminaries.py --mfd-uncertainty as-given`; `MFD_UNCERTAINTY = 'as-given'` in the notebook): 10% at M7.0–7.1, a design value, growing as R_j^−0.25 towards the rarer bins, each bin its own measurement. `'two-measurements'` counts the bins as the two measurements they are projected from, the rate of M ≥ 5 earthquakes and the b-value, multiplying every bin's uncertainty by √(bins / 2) = 3; a number sets the M7.0–7.1 bin's relative uncertainty ("MFD uncertainty").
- **Slip-rate rows:** one measurement per fault section. The inputs copy each section's slip rate to its k subsections; cleaning divides each section's rows by √k, so the measurement counts once (`preliminaries.py --slip-sections auto`; `SLIP_SECTIONS = 'auto'` in the notebook). Rows unlike their neighbours keep their weight.
- **Sampler:** Gibbs in log rate, exact, with exact on/off steps under spike-and-slab and exact swap moves between near-duplicate ruptures.

Box priors with a common floor, the per-bin boxes (`binbox-floor:-12`, the default until October 2026) and the single log-boxcar before them, still run in `gibbs.py`, for comparison only: their priors push the bin totals up, and with one measurement per section the per-bin boxes fail the fit check by a wide margin ("Earlier priors"). A linearly uniform prior is not used; a later section summarizes why.

- **Fit:** χ² = 140 ± 12, against 126 ± 16 expected if the stated uncertainties are right: 0.9 sd above. The square-root slab gives 138 ± 12, and θ = 1 gave 142 ± 12. The per-bin boxes, the default until October 2026, gave 547 ± 32, 26 sd above, because their prior pushed the bin totals up; after that result the default changed.
- **Total rate:** 0.0218 per year, against a bin-target total of 0.0214. Bin totals sit a median 1.9% above their targets (+0.4% to +7.3%), close to the best fit's +1.0% to +4.9%: the prior puts no pressure on them.
- **Population:** about 380 ruptures are switched on in each draw, most at rates the data cannot see; about 100 are above 1e-5 and 45 above 1e-4, and about 47 carry 90% of the total rate.
- **Slip rates:** the mean predictions of 72% of the 68 sections lie within one measurement uncertainty of their targets, and 97% within two. 18 low-slip sections (72 subsections) are predicted below a quarter of their targets, all with targets only 0.6–2.4 uncertainties above zero.
- **Block fit:** each row block fits about as its stated uncertainties say: χ² per independent measurement 1.04 for the 68 slip-rate sections, 1.00 for the 18 bins and 1.29 for the 40 paleo rows (+1.3 sd). The bins' misfit is almost all the model's freedom (17.1 of 18.1), so it cannot test their width.
- **Paleo rates:** the mean model's χ² over the 40 paleo rows is 34 (78% within one uncertainty, 92% within two). Most sites are predicted somewhat below their targets (median ratio 0.82), as in the best fit (0.94, with 34 of 40 below). Three sites are about two uncertainties low: rows 860 and 861, which the best fit also leaves at about half their targets, and row 853, which the best fit nearly matches but whose posterior median prediction is about 0, its ruptures off in most draws. θ gives the same.
- **MFD uncertainty:** the bins' uncertainty level is a design value (10% at M7.0–7.1), and counted as 18 independent measurements the bins pin the MFD's total rate to ±2.8%. Counted instead as the two measurements they are projected from (`MFD_UNCERTAINTY = 'two-measurements'`: every bin's uncertainty ×3), they let the slip-rate and paleo rows raise the bins a median 7.9% above their targets and the total rate by 5%, fit the paleo rows slightly better (χ² per measurement 1.22) and move subsection totals a median 4%, with exceedance probabilities and the population within Monte Carlo noise. Single draws, though, can then leave a whole bin empty: some bin fell below 1% of its target in 4.2–4.3% of the draws of a very conservative run, and in none of the 16,000 draws of a conservative run as given ("MFD uncertainty").

The data determine sums of rates and the upper part of the rate population, but not the rates of individual weak ruptures:
- **Data-determined:** subsection and bin totals, the number of ruptures above about 1e-5, and each rupture's posterior probability of exceeding 1e-5 or 1e-4. Under spike-and-slab they moved by no more than Monte Carlo noise for slab floors from 1e-12 to 1e-5, and spike-and-slab and θ = 1, two different treatments of the lower end, agree on them (exceedance probabilities within 0.02–0.16 in log₁₀, counting each subsection).
- **Prior-determined:** individual rates below the detection scale (about 3e-5 to 2e-4 for one rupture alone), including medians and lower quantiles. Most ruptures are off in most draws, so their medians and modes are 0, and whether a weak rupture is on reflects the prior on the fractions.
- **The largest prior sensitivity:** priors that push the bin totals up, the earlier box priors, give much denser solutions: about 490 ruptures above 1e-5 per draw instead of about 100, and exceedance probabilities at 1e-5 about 7 times higher (counting each subsection). Only the pressure-free priors pass the fit check.

At 211,000 ruptures two things change ("Fits and the prior's lower end"). The mean model's shortfall in the slip and paleo rates grows, because the posterior spreads each bin's rate over its ruptures' magnitudes as the bin's average does while the best fit takes the larger ones; no change to the slab or to how the fractions on are shared removes it, and the paleo rows are the block in tension. And the subsections' event rates depend on the slab: the square-root slab puts them at a median 0.62 of the default's, with the same fit.

The reference posterior at 211,000 ruptures is the very conservative run of the configuration adopted in late October 2026 (one measurement per slip-rate section, the bins as given, the balance term at each type's own weight, a slab floor of 1e-12). Its three replicates pass every convergence check, with χ² 554 ± 24 against 590 ± 34 and eight slowly mixing ruptures carrying 1.9% of the total rate; judged from its kept draws, its chains agree on each rupture's whole distribution as perfectly mixed chains would, and its products are the conservative run's to within Monte Carlo noise ("The reference run at 211,000 ruptures").

In early November 2026 the default kept every prediction within a factor 10 of its target for a few days (the band, `BAND = 10`), without the balance term and with a slab floor of 1e-10 ("The band"); since then it only keeps every prediction above zero (`BAND = 'positive'`, "Positivity"), with the conservative preset, and no full run of that is recorded here yet. Its very conservative run at 211,000 ruptures (early November 2026) passes the convergence checks, with χ² 438 ± 20 against the fit check's 590 ± 34 (−4.4 sd): the band is stricter than the data where a target is weak, so the fit check, which assumes the likelihood alone, no longer calibrates χ². The band switches on events on the faults that share few ruptures, which the reference run left mostly without, and makes the solutions about 2.5 times denser.

Gibbs in log rate is exact and needs no tuning. Under spike-and-slab a minority of ruptures switch on and off slowly (0.3% of them in quick runs, carrying 16–18% of the total rate), and the fractions on mix slowly in short runs, so its runs are long; its sweeps are cheap, because off ruptures rarely change any residuals. Log-space HMC agreed with Gibbs rupture by rupture (under the single box), and the self-tests check every prior against exact answers.

## Slip-rate sections: one measurement per section

A fault section's slip rate is one measurement. The inputs copy it, with its uncertainty, to each of the section's k subsections: the 388 slip-rate rows carry only 46 distinct values. Counted as independent, each section's slip rate entered the likelihood k times, as if its uncertainty were √k smaller (2.9 times on average). The default now counts each measurement once: cleaning divides each section's rows by √k (`preliminaries.py --slip-sections auto`; `SLIP_SECTIONS = 'auto'` in the notebook).

**What the weighting does.** For a section with target s and subsection predictions y_1 … y_k, in units of its uncertainty σ,

Σ_j (y_j − s)² / (k σ²) = (ȳ − s)² / σ² + Σ_j (y_j − ȳ)² / (k σ²).

The section's mean prediction ȳ is fitted to the measurement once, with the stated uncertainty, and the subsections' departures from their own mean are penalized as if each subsection's uncertainty were σ√k. Dividing the rows by √k is therefore the same as giving each subsection's copy the uncertainty σ√k: the measurement keeps its information about the section as a whole, the copies stop counting as k independent measurements, and the subsections are still asked, loosely, to share one slip rate.

**Finding the sections.** The inputs carry no section labels, so cleaning looks for runs of neighbouring rows that hold copies: identical targets (as normalized in the inputs) and identical coefficients for every rupture both rows contain. On the corrected inputs:
- 68 sections of 2–21 subsections (median 5), and no single subsections;
- all 320 neighbouring pairs with identical targets that share a rupture also have identical coefficients; of the 35 neighbouring pairs with different targets that share ruptures, 1 would pass the coefficient test, so both tests are needed;
- 6 pairs of neighbours with identical targets share no rupture, so they cannot be confirmed as copies and count as separate sections: the 62 runs of identical values become 68 sections;
- 32 sections share their target with another section (geologic slip rates are often round numbers); a mapping file would settle whether any of them belong together.

**Sections stop at each fault's end** (November 5th, 2026). A run of copies can cross from one parent fault to the next: the Turi segments, Lyell and Lyell South, Ruahine Central, North and South hold one slip rate on consecutive subsections. Counting such a run once makes √k do two jobs, the copies on one fault's subsections and a rate passed from one fault to another, and the second is the CFM correlation's (`CFM_TEMPLATE_RHO`, `CFM_RATE_LINKS`), which would then count it again. So, with the rupture set (`--rupture-set`, or the one in the inputs' folder, as `RUPTURE_SET = 'auto'` finds it), cleaning ends a section wherever the parent fault changes, whatever the settings: at 2e4 6 runs span two or more faults and the 68 sections become 75 (fit check 133 ± 16 instead of 126 ± 16); at 211,000 ruptures 37 runs do, and the 507 sections become 551 (634 ± 36 instead of 590 ± 34, under the CFM's slip rates). Without a rupture set the runs are as before, and cleaning says so. The problem records it (`sections_by_fault`). Runs before then counted the cross-fault runs once.

Rows unlike their neighbours keep their weight, so a geodetic slip-rate model, whose values vary from subsection to subsection, is used as given, each row its own measurement. On synthetic variants of the inputs, continuously varying targets gave 388 single subsections, as did equal slip rates with differing uncertainties; the geologic inputs give the 68 sections. `--slip-sections FILE` (`SLIP_SECTIONS = 'file.txt'`, in `DATA_DIR`) takes the sections from a file of `original_row section` lines instead, for example from the fault model: a missing or extra slip-rate row stops cleaning, and a section whose rows have different targets is noted. `--slip-sections none` (`SLIP_SECTIONS = 'none'`) keeps every subsection as its own measurement, the inputs as given, as a sensitivity test in its own `RUN_DIR`; it reproduces the earlier results byte for byte (the cleaned problem, the diagnostics, the rate vectors and every table). The cleaned problem records the row scales, each slip-rate row's section, the setting and the fit check (`row_scale`, `section`, `slip_sections`, `chi2_expect`).

**The fit check.** If the uncertainties are right, a model matching the truth has an expected χ² of Σ_s k_s w_s² + n_other, with variance 2 Σ_s (k_s w_s²)² + 2 n_other, where w_s is the weight of section s's rows and n_other the number of other rows: one shared error counted k_s times widens the spread. With w_s = 1/√k_s each section contributes 1 ± √2, giving 68 + 58 = 126 ± 16 on the corrected inputs. With `'none'` it is 446 ± 80 (446 ± 83 earlier, when the 62 runs of identical values were taken as the sections). The expectation assumes each section's slip rate is uniform along it; real variation along a section would add to it. Cleaning prints the fit check, and the notebook compares every run against it.

**The slip rates in log10 as well** (November 9th, 2026; `SLIP_MISFIT = 'split+log10'`, since November 10th part of `MISFIT`, below; `gibbs.py --slip-misfit`). A slip rate's split normal holds a weak target loosely below it: a section whose target is under about one sd will accept a prediction of zero, and one an order of magnitude too large costs little more where the upper side is long. With `'split+log10'` each slip-rate row is scored by half its split normal plus half a Gaussian in log10(prediction / target) with the same sd: on each side s = log10(1 + σ/target), σ that side's effective sd (the CFM's range, the doubling, the slip-rate factor, the rounding, and the template and link weights' 1/t, as the linear half has them), as `MFD_MISFIT = 'log10'` has the bins'. It is the geometric mean of the two densities, so each section still counts once; near the target the two halves agree, and away from it the log half grows with the number of decades off, (log10 off / s)²/2 on χ²'s scale, so zero is infinitely costly and 10× off costs 1/(2s²) (s = 0.05: 200; s = 0.26: 7.4). The sampler halves the slip rows' Gaussian by scaling them by 1/√2 and keeps the log half through the soft band's arrays, with a weight on each side of the target (each subsection 1/k, so a section's copies count once); the soft bands cannot be on with it. χ² as fitted counts both halves, and each section's share of the fit check is its misfit's mean and variance with its prediction drawn from its own curve at t = 1, the geometric mean of the linear density in rate and the log10 density in log rate (`cru_common.geo_moments`): at 2e4, 74.2 ± 12.1 against the Gaussian's 75.0 ± 12.2; at 211,000 ruptures (templates and links on, where s runs from 0.006 to 1.5 decades, median 0.26) 293.6 ± 21.1 against 298.9 ± 22.1. The best fit as fitted and the slip-rate figure use it too, and `gibbs.py --selftest` checks it against a grid integral with a lopsided row (`selftest_slipgeo`). At 2e4 the inputs' slip sds are tight (s from 0.003 to 0.13 decades, median 0.05), and in a short run there (CFM slip rates, paleo in years) the log half averaged 129 on χ²'s scale, χ² as fitted 364 against an expected 144 ± 17, and 0.1 slip-rate rows per draw were predicted 10× or more off their targets.

**One misfit setting** (November 10th, 2026; `MISFIT`, `'half'` by default, `'linear'` or `'log10'`; it replaced `PALEO_MISFIT`, `MFD_MISFIT` and `SLIP_MISFIT`). The user asked for the slip rates' treatment, half linear and half log with the same effective sd, for every data group, set in one place. `'linear'` scores the slip rates by their split normals (the inputs' Gaussians without `CFM_SLIPRATES`) and the MFD bins and the paleo rows by the inputs' Gaussians in rate. `'log10'` scores the slip rates and the MFD bins by Gaussians in log10(prediction / target) with s = log10(1 + σ/target) on each side, σ that side's effective sd (as `MFD_MISFIT = 'log10'` had the bins'), and the paleo rows by each site's recurrence-interval PDF in years (as `PALEO_MISFIT = 'years'` had them): for the paleo rows the log misfit is the years curve, the linear one the Gaussian in rate. `'half'` scores each group by half of each, the geometric mean of the two densities, so each measurement still counts once (`gibbs.py --slip-misfit split+log10`, `--mfd-misfit gaussian+log10`, `--paleo-misfit gaussian+years`; `cru_common.MISFIT_MODES`). In the sampler a group with a log weight b has its rows scaled by √(1 − b) in the Gaussian (by 1e-9 for b = 1) and its log misfit, times b, through the soft band's sided weights (slip, bins) or the paleo tables, kept exactly by Metropolis steps. χ² as fitted adds b × (log misfit − Gaussian) for each group, and each group's share of the fit check is its own misfit's mean and variance, drawn from the geometric mean, with weights 1 − b and b, of the two densities (`cru_common.geo_moments`, `paleo_misfit.site_moments_mixed`): with `'half'` at 2e4 (`SLIP_SD_MULTIPLIER = 2`), the slip sections 74.2 ± 12.1 (75.0 Gaussian), the bins 17.9 ± 6.0 (18), the paleo sites 43.5 ± 9.5 (40; 51.7 with the years curve alone), the whole about 136 ± 16 against cleaning's 133; at 211,000 ruptures (templates and links on) 293.6, 17.9 and 71.1 (65; 87.3 alone), about 383 ± 25 against 382. The best fit as fitted, `block_fit.py`, the three fit figures and the summary PDF score each group the same way, and the full self-test set checks the half and log modes of every group against grid integrals. In the quick preset at 2e4 with the defaults (`SLIP_SD_MULTIPLIER = 3`), χ² as fitted was 253 ± 15 against a fit check of 132 ± 16: the slip rates' log half averaged 215 (half of it counted), with about 4 slip-rate rows per draw predicted 10× or more off their targets; the NZSHM22 solution scores 1,735 over the slip-rate rows this way, nearly all of it from three rows it predicts at almost zero. Every prediction is now kept above zero whatever `MISFIT` (`gibbs.py --all-positive`: the slip-rate and paleo rows and the MFD bins), and `SLIP_BAND` was removed from the notebook (its factor band and `None` are gone; `gibbs.py --slip-band` and `--paleo-band` remain command-line options); `SLIP_BAND_SOFT` was removed too, as the log misfit does what it was for, with each row's own sd (`gibbs.py --slip-log-band` remains a command-line option). Runs are named `_misfit{MISFIT}`.

**Seeds** (November 6th, 2026; `SEED`). Every run before then gave replicate r the seed 101 + r, so two runs with the same settings were identical to the last bit (checked on two conservative runs at 211,000 ruptures: the same problem, best fits, χ² traces and final rates); the replicates within a run had different seeds, so the convergence report's comparison between them was a real one. Each replicate's seed is hashed from it and the replicate's number (numpy's `SeedSequence`), so runs started a second apart share no replicate, and `gibbs.py` spawns each chain's stream from it in turn. The configuration records the seed, and the notebook prints the replicates'. `tools/run_quick_checks.sh` pins `'legacy'`.

**χ² as fitted** (November 5th, 2026). Every run's χ² is the data misfit as its likelihood scores it: the slip rates by their split normals (with `CFM_SLIPRATES`), the MFD bins in log10 (with `MFD_MISFIT = 'log10'`), and the paleo rows by −2 ln of each site's recurrence-interval PDF (with `PALEO_MISFIT`), in place of their Gaussians. `gibbs.py` records it beside the Gaussian χ² (`misfit` and `chi2` in each run's file) and prints it every few sweeps; the convergence checks, the row-block fit, the representative draws, the fit figures and the summary PDF all use it. Its fit check is cleaning's, with each paleo site's share its own misfit's mean and variance when its rate is drawn from the site's own curve (`paleo_misfit.site_moments`) in place of a Gaussian row's 1 and 2: a median 1.29 per site at 2e4 (0.93–1.78) and 1.34 at 211,000 ruptures (0.74–1.90), so the 40 sites expect 51.7 ± 11.0, not 40 ± 8.9, and the 65 expect 87.3 ± 14.7, not 65 ± 11.4. A slip rate's split normal and a bin in log10 keep 1 and 2, being Gaussian on each side or in log10. With every misfit Gaussian the two χ² are the same. ## MFD uncertainty: how much the bins count

**The question.** The 18 bin rows hold the regional magnitude–frequency distribution (MFD) that the rupture rates must reproduce, M6.8–8.6 in steps of 0.1, each bin a target rate R_j with a relative uncertainty. They are not independent observations. They are a projection of two measurements, the rate of M ≥ 5 earthquakes and the b-value, whose own uncertainties the logic tree explores through alternative inputs. And their uncertainty level is a design value, not a measurement: the inputs give σ_j / R_j = 0.1 (R_j / R_7.0)^−0.25 exactly, 10.0% at M7.0–7.1, from 9.8% at the highest-rate bin (6.9–7.0) to 15.4% at 8.5–8.6. The 0.1 is set where cru_A and cru_D are built, a holdover of the UCERF3 practice of weighting the data types against each other, whereas the slip-rate and paleo rows are each normalized by their measured uncertainties. The power 0.25, also set there, splits the difference between two views: each bin checked against the earthquakes observed in it, whose Poisson counts grow tenfold per unit of magnitude for b = 1 (power 0.5), and every bin taken from the same N(M ≥ 5) and b (power 0). Cleaning keeps that shape and sets only the level.

**What the inputs' level implies.** Counted as 18 independent measurements, the bins pin the MFD as a whole far more tightly than one bin's uncertainty suggests: from the bin rows alone, the bins' total rate is known to ±2.8% and their slope to ±0.024 in b (a weighted least-squares line through the bin centres). The posterior followed: in quick runs its total rate was ±2.5%, its bins sat +0.4% to +7.3% above their targets, and their misfit was almost all the model's freedom (17.1 of 18.1; "Row-block fit" below), so the data could not say whether that width was right.

**The two-measurements rule** (`'two-measurements'`). If each bin's uncertainty is right as that bin's own, the bins together should carry the information of the two measurements they come from, not of 18. Write the bins' log rates as a projection of two parameters (log N, b) with covariance S, and each bin's variance as its share of it, σ_j² = J_j S J_jᵀ. Treated as independent, with every uncertainty multiplied by c, the bins carry information Jᵀ D⁻¹ J / c² about the two parameters, against S⁻¹ for the measurements themselves; matching the two on average (tr(S Jᵀ D⁻¹ J) / c² = 2, the number of measurements) gives c² = B / 2 for B bins, whatever the projection. So cleaning multiplies every bin's uncertainty by √(B / 2), 3 for the 18 bins, dividing its row of G and d by it. It is the composite-likelihood count that divides each slip-rate section's k copies by √k. The bins then have 30% at M7.0–7.1 (29.4% to 46.3% across the bins), and the bin rows alone give ±8.3% on the bins' total rate and ±0.071 on their slope: the inputs' design value now describes the MFD as a whole, about one bin's worth, rather than each of 18 independent bins. The targets R_j are unchanged, and the soft top's width, the bin's relative uncertainty, follows (0.112–0.165 decades, against 0.041–0.062).

**The settings** (`MFD_UNCERTAINTY` in the notebook; `preliminaries.py --mfd-uncertainty`):
- `'as-given'` (the default): the inputs' uncertainties, each bin its own measurement. The cleaned problem's matrix, targets and row scales are exactly those from before the setting existed, and quick runs reproduce the earlier numbers exactly.
- `'two-measurements'`: the rule above.
- a number u: the relative uncertainty (one sd) of the M7.0–7.1 bin, the other bins keeping the inputs' shape. 0.1 reproduces the inputs, and 0.3 is `'two-measurements'` for these 18 bins. The bins are counted up from 6.80–6.90 in steps of 0.1 in their original row order, as for the rates-by-magnitude figure, and that order is checked against rupture sizes; cleaning stops if no bin starts at M7.0.

Cleaning prints the inputs' uncertainties and the power they follow (R_j^−0.250 here, exactly), the multiplier, and what the bin rows then say about the MFD as a whole. The cleaned problem records the setting and the multiplier (`mfd_uncertainty`, `mfd_scale`), and its `row_scale` includes the multiplier. Another setting changes the problem both priors share, so it needs its own `RUN_DIR`; the notebook warns if a `RUN_DIR` holds another prior's runs made on a problem cleaned otherwise.

## Spike-and-slab (default)

Each rupture is either off, with a rate of exactly 0, or on, with a rate from a slab: log-uniform from a slab floor (1e-10 by default, the notebook's `FLOOR`; 1e-12 in late October 2026) up to a soft top at its bin's target rate R_j. The fraction of ruptures switched on is estimated separately in each bin, under a Beta(1, 1) prior (`--prior spikeslab:A:TOP:a:b`; `spikeslab` alone gives A = −10, a soft top and Beta(1, 1)).

This separates two jobs that a floor does for a log-uniform box. "Off" is an exact zero, so no floor is needed to define it, and the slab's lower end only has to lie below the scale at which all ruptures together become invisible to the data (about 2e-9 on the corrected inputs; "Floors and cutoff"). How many ruptures are active becomes an explicit quantity that the data estimate in each bin, rather than a side effect of a floor or a depth. The fractions are per bin because rupture counts vary 23-fold across bins (164 to 3,708): a single fraction would hand the crowded bins proportionally more active ruptures, which is the source of the box priors' pressure on the bin totals ("θ = 1").

**The targets and the top.** Each bin row is its constraint divided by the bin's uncertainty: coefficient 1/σ_j for every rupture in the bin, right-hand side R_j/σ_j. So R_j is d_j divided by the row's coefficient, whatever the uncertainty function, and d_j itself is one over the relative uncertainty. On the corrected inputs the targets run from 3.4e-4 to 2.1e-3 per year and sum to 0.0214. The inputs give them relative uncertainties of 9.8–15.4% (10% at M7.0–7.1), which the default keeps; `'two-measurements'` multiplies them by 3, to 29–46% ("MFD uncertainty"), which changes the bins' weight in χ², but not the targets. No rupture can carry more than its bin's true total, which the bin row gives as R_j with its uncertainty σ_j, so the slab (like θ's boxes) ends at R_j with a Gaussian shoulder in log rate of sd log₁₀(1 + σ_j/R_j), 0.041–0.062 decades under the default (0.112–0.165 with the bins counted as two measurements). Unlike a hard top at R_j, it does not truncate the plausible case of a bin whose true total is somewhat above R_j and dominated by one rupture; unlike a hard top at a multiple of R_j, it needs no arbitrary factor; and it follows the bins' uncertainties automatically if they are revised. `TOP` may instead be `hard` (at R_j) or a number F (hard, at F × R_j). Using the targets to place the top is a mild double use of the data, and dominant ruptures do press against it (in about 80% of draws, by up to about three of the bin's uncertainties, counting each subsection), which is why it is soft. An exact test (`selftest_edges`, part of `--selftest`) checks two rates with different shoulder widths, pushed above their tops by the data, against a grid integral on both backends (all |z| ≤ 1.1).

**Sampling.** Because a log-uniform prior is scale-invariant, writing m_i = R_j u_i turns every bin's range into the same one for u, below 1, with G's columns multiplied by their bin's R_j; the sampler runs on u and records rates. The update is exact and needs no numerical integrals. An off rupture keeps a slab value drawn from the slab itself (a pseudo-prior), and whether a rupture is on is a Gibbs step with probability π·L(m) / [π·L(m) + (1 − π)·L(0)], computed from quantities the update already has. An on rupture's rate is updated by the exact slice sampler in log rate. The fractions are redrawn exactly from their Beta conditionals after every sweep (`--hyper-every 1`, the default for this prior), and swap moves still apply between near-duplicates in the same bin, so they can exchange the active role. An exact test (`selftest_spikeslab`, part of `--selftest`) enumerates the on/off states of two rates in one bin, with the fraction integrated out in closed form, against grid integrals on both backends (all |z| ≤ 2.1). Its sweeps are several times cheaper than θ's: off ruptures rarely change any residuals, and swaps between two off ruptures are skipped.

## θ = 1: pressure-free per-bin boxes (the alternative until October 2026)

**Why box priors push.** With its top at R_j, a log-uniform box W_j decades deep makes the prior expect θ_j = n_j / (W_j ln 10) of the bin's ruptures per unit of ln(rate). For many ruptures, the prior distribution of the bin's total is then a generalized Dickman distribution: in units of R_j, its density below the top is proportional to s^(θ−1). Its slope at the target is θ_j − 1, so the prior shifts the bin total by roughly σ_j²(θ_j − 1), where σ_j is the bin row's relative uncertainty. Shallow boxes (θ_j > 1) push up, very deep ones (θ_j < 1) push down, and θ_j = 1 is flat below R_j. The density then falls gently above R_j, a resistance to overshoot of only 1–2%. Box priors with a common floor have θ_j of about 4 to 200, and runs of the single box (floors 1e-10 and 1e-12) and of per-bin boxes (10 and 12 decades deep) bore this out (earlier inputs, counting each subsection): the predicted pull (a median +26% to +39%) ranked the bins as the observed one did (+16% to +22%; rank correlation 0.83–0.87). The observed shift is about 60% of the prior-only prediction, because the slip-rate and paleo rows pull the totals back. What matters is θ, not where the top sits.

**Pressure-free depths.** W_j = n_j / ln 10 decades gives θ_j = 1: from 71 decades (the 164-rupture bin) to 1,610 (the 3,708-rupture bin) on the corrected inputs. A simulation for the median bin confirms the flat density below R_j (0.96–1.04 in relative terms) and the fall above it (0.93, 0.74, 0.62 at 1.1, 1.3 and 1.5 R_j). Within a factor of 2 of these depths (θ between 0.5 and 2) the pull stays near 1%. Rates of 10^-1,600 are no problem in log space; they are exactly zero in the predictions.

**Implementation.** `--prior binbox-theta:θ:SE[:TOP]` sets each bin's depth to n_j / (θ ln 10) decades below the same top as spike-and-slab's (θ = 1: `binbox-theta:1:0`). The C kernel and the NumPy fallback accept a lower edge per rupture. An exact test (`selftest_zlo`, part of `--selftest`) checks two rates with different lower edges against a grid integral, with soft and hard edges, on both backends (all |z| ≤ 2.3). Swaps are restricted to same-bin pairs. Transfer moves are not available.

At 2e4 (fit check 126 ± 16; the best fit's χ² 31) every variant gave the default's fit within noise: χ² 138–144, slip 1.00–1.04 and paleo 1.26–1.38 per measurement, misfits within about 0.1 of the default's (slip −0.53 / −0.55 / −0.25 / −0.46, paleo −0.57 / −0.59 / −0.74, against the best fit's −0.43 / −0.32 / 0.00 / −0.18 and −0.36 / −0.33 / −0.29), and 0.92–0.93 of the best fit's moment rate, the domain groups again raising the bins (+4%).

- **The variants.** The square-root slab (`--slab sqrt`): an on rupture's rate is uniform in √rate below the same top, density m^−1/2 instead of the log-uniform's 1/m, so its pull of each rate towards 0 is half as strong, while small rates cost more and fewer ruptures are on. The grouped fractions: one fraction on per magnitude bin and main tectonic domain (the rupture set's `Domain` of most of the rupture's subsections: 64 groups at 2e4, 254 at 211,000 ruptures) or main slip-rate section (468 and 2,991 groups), each under Beta(1, 1); or pooled, each group's fraction drawn from Beta(μ_j κ_j, (1 − μ_j) κ_j) around its bin's μ_j ~ Beta(1, 1), with the pooling strength κ_j log-uniform from 0.1 to 1e7, all estimated (exactly: μ_j and κ_j by slice sampling with the groups' fractions integrated out, then the fractions from their Beta conditionals; exact tests against enumeration passed).
- **None changes the fit.** The pooled models chose κ_j of about 10² (medians over the bins 10^2.1–10^2.7), close to one shared fraction per bin: the data themselves pick per-bin sharing. Independent fractions per bin and domain switch more ruptures on and raise the bins (+5% at 211,000 ruptures), with the slip rates a little closer and the paleo rates no closer; per bin and section, the bins rise 30% and the paleo rates are still about 0.9 uncertainties short.
- **Why: the bins count events, not moment.** Each bin spans 0.1 in magnitude, a factor of 1.41 in moment. The best fit puts each bin's rate on its larger ruptures (rate-weighted magnitude 0.020–0.021 above the bin's average over all its ruptures, at 2e4 and at 211,000 ruptures), while every posterior, whatever the prior variant, sits at the average (0.000–0.003 above). The data's pull towards the larger ruptures is spread thinly over thousands of interchangeable choices, against far more combinations near the bin's average. So at the same event rates the posterior delivers 8% less moment than the best fit at 2e4, and at 211,000 ruptures, where the best fit also raises its bins by 8%, 15% less. The slip and paleo rates come up short accordingly.
- **The best fit overfits.** Its χ² is 31 at 2e4 and 90 at 211,000 ruptures, against fit checks of 126 ± 16 and 590 ± 34: it fits the data far more closely than a model matching the truth would. The posterior's draws pass the fit check (140 and 545). By block, the slip rates are fitted within their uncertainties (0.76 per measurement at 211,000 ruptures), and the paleo rows are the block in tension, at 2.2 per measurement at 211,000 ruptures (1.3 at 2e4) under every variant. Closing that gap would take a different balance between the data (the paleo rows' uncertainties against the bins'), a modelling decision on the inputs rather than the prior.
- **At 211,000 ruptures the lower end matters.** The square-root slab fits as the default does but spreads the events differently. Its subsections' event rates (the summed rates of the ruptures through each) are a median 0.62 of the default's very conservative values (5–95%: 0.08–1.08; 0.26–1.10 for the three-quarters of subsections with the most events). A quick run from the best fit and a longer one from zero (8 chains × 12,000 sweeps) agree on this. Quick runs of the default scatter around its very conservative values with a median of 1.00 (5–95%: 0.57–1.39), and its very conservative replicates agree within ±7% (5–95%). At 2e4 the two slabs agree within about ±7% (5–95%) for the three-quarters of subsections with the most events. So at 211,000 ruptures the data leave much of how the events spread over the subsections to the prior. θ = 1's conservative run there puts them at 2.3 times the default's (5–95%: 0.87–6.0), but θ cannot mix at that size.
- **The square-root slab as the cross-check** (October 2026; dropped at the end of the month). It mixes as the log-uniform slab does (in the longer run, R̂ 1.011 for χ², 1.001 for the ruptures on and 1.013 for the slowest bin's fraction on) and costs about 10% more per sweep (24 ms against 21–22 ms for 8 chains at 211,000 ruptures). An off rupture's slab value is drawn down from the top, m = e^z_top (1 − u f)², with no logarithm. The quick smoke test at 2e4 gave χ² 138 ± 12, bins +1.4%, about 62 ruptures on per draw (55 above 1e-5) and 30 carrying 90% of the rate. An exact test (`selftest_spikeslab` with the tilt, part of the full `--selftest`) checks it against enumeration and grid integrals on both backends.
- **θ retired.** θ = 1 fitted no better (at 211,000 ruptures 0.81 per measurement for slip and 2.28 for paleo), shares the log-uniform's pull, and cannot cross its boxes from about 2e5 ruptures. It cannot take the square-root tilt either: its boxes hold every rupture, so the tilt would push all of a bin's rates up together, the flat prior's runaway in a milder form. The square-root slab took over its role as the cross-check; θ is kept for comparison only.
- **Not kept.** The grouped and pooled fractions on changed no fit, so they were not added to the code set.

## Floors and cutoff

One setting, `FLOOR` (1e-10 by default), marks the rate below which nothing matters. It is spike-and-slab's slab floor, the lower end of its on range (`spikeslab:{FLOOR}:soft` in the notebook; −10 is also `gibbs.py`'s default), and the cutoff below which rates count as equal in the convergence checks, the figures and the preliminaries' collective-scale check (the notebook passes `--diag-floor={FLOOR}` to `gibbs.py`, `preliminaries.py`, `check_convergence.py` and `plot_rate_magnitude.py`, whose defaults are −10). As a cutoff it never changes the sampling; under θ, whose depths come from θ, it is only the cutoff. The per-bin boxes, if run, take it as their floor.

Rates below 1e-10 are negligible:
- **Per rupture:** under a Poisson model, a rupture at 1e-10 per year has a 95% chance of never occurring in 500 million years, about the age of New Zealand's oldest surface rocks (the chance of at least one occurrence is 1 − e^(−0.05) = 4.9%).
- **Age of the Earth:** at 1e-10 per year the mean recurrence interval, 10 billion years, is more than twice the age of the Earth.
- **Collectively:** with every rupture at the same rate, no prediction moves by more than 0.1σ below 1.8e-9 for the corrected 2e4 inputs with one measurement per slip-rate section (6.9e-10 counting each subsection), so all 18,243 ruptures together at 1e-10 would move no prediction by more than about 0.006σ (0.015σ counting each subsection). Under spike-and-slab only a few hundred ruptures are on at a time, so the margin is wider still. The scale falls as more ruptures share the busiest subsection (here original row 156, crossed by 7,234 ruptures), so `preliminaries.py` reports it for each problem and says whether `FLOOR` lies below it; check it on the 5e5 inputs, where it may approach 1e-10.

**History.** The slab floor and the cutoff were 1e-10 when they were separate settings (`SLAB_FLOOR` and `DIAG_FLOOR`, early October 2026). They were lowered to 1e-12 to share one setting with the per-bin boxes' floor, which needed 1e-12 to pass the fit check of the time, and returned to 1e-10 when spike-and-slab became the default, since neither spike-and-slab nor θ needs the lower value. They moved to 1e-12 again in late October 2026, with the configuration adopted then (that of the reference run at 211,000 ruptures), and back to 1e-10 in early November 2026, with the band; with one measurement per section the two floors agree within Monte Carlo noise (below).

**The slab floor does not change the products.** With one measurement per section (quick preset, two replicates per floor), moving the slab floor from 1e-12 to 1e-10 changed every product by no more than Monte Carlo noise: subsection totals by a median 0.1% (5–95%: −9% to +11%; two replicates at the same floor differ by −9% to +20%), exceedance probabilities at 1e-5 by a median 0.02 in log₁₀ (5–95%: −0.20 to +0.13, against −0.29 to +0.18 between replicates), mean rates by a median 0.02 decades, and bin totals by at most 0.6%. Only the number of ruptures switched on at rates the data cannot see changed, from about 497 to 379 per draw, and the fractions on mixed slightly better (worst R̂ 1.03 against 1.04). Counting each subsection, raising the slab floor further, to 1e-8, 1e-7, 1e-6 or 1e-5, likewise left χ², the total rate, the bins, subsection totals, exceedance at 1e-5 and 1e-4 and the mean rates unchanged within the spread between seeds; only the number of ruptures on fell, by about 53 per decade, and a few near-empty subsections' medians became exactly 0. A slab floor of 1e-11 instead of 1e-10 (two seeds each, 8 chains × 10,000 sweeps) cost nothing per sweep and switched on about 50 more ruptures per draw at undetectable rates.

**The cutoff changes only the diagnostics.** θ's draws are identical whatever `FLOOR` is; only their scoring changes. With the cutoff at 1e-10, θ's quick runs flag 195–243 ruptures (1.1–1.3%) as slow-mixing, against 259–288 at 1e-12: θ's ruptures spend about 0.8% of their draws between 1e-12 and 1e-10 (counting each subsection), visits too rare to mix well in short runs and of no consequence, and the slow minority carries 23–24% of the total rate either way. Spike-and-slab's diagnostics barely depend on the cutoff. Compare slow-mixing counts only between runs scored at the same cutoff.

## Cleaning

The penalty entries in `cru_A.txt` are exactly 1e20 (not 1e21). `preliminaries.py` follows the two-step recipe and prints the count after each step. The description below is of the earlier inputs; the corrected inputs are summarized under "Note on the inputs" and in cleaning's report on them (`prelim_summary.txt`, at the top of each `RUN_DIR`).

Step 1 removes the 13 rows containing 1e20 entries (original rows 680, 682–686 and 688–694, all with d = 1 and no other entries) from G and d, together with the 292 parameters they apply to. That gives 741 × 18,356.

Step 2 removes all-zero rows and columns. There are no all-zero columns, but 294 rows are now all-zero. Rows 681 and 687 were empty in the raw matrix (d = 1). Rows 388–679 each held a single entry of 1e4 on one of the 292 removed parameters, with d = 0: a second, softer zero-forcing penalty, one row per removed parameter. Removing them leaves 447 × 18,356, with 1,074,239 nonzeros, all positive (minimum 41.6). The 447 remaining rows are the effective number of constraints.

**Soft zero-forcing rows** (step 1b, October 2026). The 1e4 rows with d = 0 are zero-forcing penalties too, softer ones (the weight is probably a legacy of how the inputs are built). In the 2e4 inputs every one of them is on a parameter a 1e20 row already removes, so step 2 removes them as all-zero rows. In the 211,000-rupture inputs, 301 of them (original rows 3649–7140) are on ruptures that no 1e20 row covers. As given they would cap those ruptures softly (a rate of 1e-4 costs 1 in χ²), and the fit check would count them as 301 measurements although they hold no data. Cleaning therefore treats every row after the slip-rate rows that has d = 0 and a single entry left as zero-forcing: its rupture is removed, with a rate of 0 by construction like the penalty rows' ruptures, and the row with it. `--keep-soft-penalties` keeps them as the inputs give them, and the fit check then leaves them out. At 211k the cleaned problem is 2,395 × 204,538 and the fit check 590 ± 34 (891 ± 42 with the 301 rows counted); the 2e4 results are unchanged.

The removed rows contributed only a constant χ² of 2, so keeping them would not change the posterior. This was checked directly with a 741-row version (`--keep-empty-rows`). With the same seed, the Gibbs sampler gave bit-identical draws, with every χ² exactly 2 higher. If the empty rows are ever kept, χ² comparisons must include the constant; the fit check recorded at cleaning includes it.

The rows fall into three blocks. Original rows 0–387 (388 rows, varying coefficients, d from 0.6 to 31.6) look like slip-rate rows. Rows 695–713 (19 rows, one constant coefficient per row, d from 6.5 to 10.2) look like magnitude–frequency bins; every rupture appears in exactly one of them. Rows 714–753 (40 rows, varying coefficients, d from 1.5 to 10.7) look like paleo-type rows. These labels are my reading of the structure; `--blocks` names them. Rename as appropriate.

No pair exceeds 0.9999, and no two columns are exactly proportional. On the corrected inputs with one measurement per section, 338,177 pairs exceed 0.99. The search finds every such pair without comparing every pair: two unit columns with cosine above 0.99 differ by less than √(2 × 0.01) = 0.141 in every entry, so a column's partners are all nonzero wherever it has an entry of at least 0.141, and each column is compared only with the columns of the sparsest such row (17% of all pairs at 2e4, 6% at 211,000 ruptures, where the search takes 13 s on the M2 against 11 minutes for every pair).

The best nonnegative fit, by Lawson–Hanson NNLS on the sparse G (it updates a QR factorization of the active columns as they enter and leave, sets the rates that block each step to exactly 0 so that rounding cannot stall it, and never forms GᵀG or a dense copy of G; under a second at 2e4 on the M2, 25 s at 211,000 ruptures), has χ²_min = 67.65 from 135 nonzero rates. The best fit's predictions are unique, but its rates need not be: at 211,000 ruptures this version and the dense one it replaced reach the same χ²_min to nine digits and the same predictions to 1e-12, with 729 of their 730 nonzero rates in common; the odd pair are two columns 0.9991 alike, which carry the same rate.

## Summarizing the draws

**χ².** Every χ² in this report is the mean over posterior draws, not the χ² of the posterior-mean model m̄. Because the predictions are linear in the rates, the two differ by exactly the total posterior variance of the predictions: E[χ²(m)] = χ²(m̄) + Σ_j Var(y_j).

| run (corrected inputs, one measurement per section, quick preset) | mean χ² over draws | χ² of the posterior-mean model | total predictive variance |
|---|---|---|---|
| spike-and-slab | 140.2 ± 11.7 | 86.1 | 54.1 |
| θ = 1 | 141.8 ± 11.8 | 88.4 | 53.4 |

With the MFD bins counted as two measurements the three were 137.0 ± 12.1, 80.4 and 56.7 under spike-and-slab, and 137.2 ± 11.9, 84.2 and 53.0 under θ. The mean over draws is the fit check. The posterior-mean model's χ² is the number to compare with single models such as NNLS (χ²_min = 31.25; 27.19 with the bins counted as two measurements) or simulated annealing; under spike-and-slab and θ, though, the posterior-mean model is a summary rather than a plausible sample, far less sparse than any draw. About half the excess of a typical draw over the best fit is predictive spread and half systematic.

**Means.** For anything that adds rates (totals, subsection and bin sums, hazard), use means in linear space, or better, compute the quantity for each draw and then summarize. Linear means add up exactly and are robust to the prior's lower end, because each rate's linear mean is dominated by its data-informed upper tail. Geometric means (means in log space) follow the lower end: under the single box they moved 1.85 decades for 4 decades of floor while linear means moved 0.06, and summed over ruptures they gave a total 25 times too small. Under spike-and-slab most draws of a rupture are exactly 0, so geometric means are not even defined. For a single rupture's distribution, use quantiles, which are the same in either space; for weak ruptures they are prior-dependent.

**Mean rates by decade.** The rates-by-magnitude table (`fig_rate_magnitude.txt`) also counts each bin's ruptures by the decade of their posterior mean rate. Under spike-and-slab a rupture's mean is the fraction of draws in which it is on times its mean rate when on. ## Samplers and convergence

**Gibbs in log rate** (`gibbs.py`, C kernel). Each rate is drawn exactly from its conditional by a one-dimensional slice sampler in log m, so hard and soft edges are handled exactly; under spike-and-slab an exact Gibbs step also switches each rupture on or off. One sweep costs one pass over the nonzeros of G. Each thread moves its chains through the ruptures together, reading each column once for up to four of them, and the slice sampler takes the stepping-out steps that a bound shows would pass without evaluating them; every chain's arithmetic is unchanged (bit-identical output), and at 211,000 ruptures the kernel became 1.7–2 times faster under spike-and-slab and 2.2 times under θ (October 2026). The likelihood is flat across the "off" plateau, so one update can carry a rate in or out of it, and ruptures switch freely between clearly on and clearly off; under the single box each did so about 0.30 times per recorded draw, giving about 980 effective draws per 1,000 for nearly every rupture.

**The band** (`--band 10`; the notebook's default for a few days in early November 2026, before positivity) restricts each update to the rates that keep every row of its column within a factor 10 of its target: the slice, or the truncated normal, is cut to that range; under spike-and-slab an off or on state the band forbids has probability 0; and a swap that would leave the band is rejected. Every draw stays inside it (each run records the count outside, `band_outside`, always 0). An off rupture can leave the band only by switching on, so under spike-and-slab its limits are found only when it does (since early November 2026): the same decisions and random numbers, bit-identical output, and a sweep with the band costs no more than one without (at 211,000 ruptures, 16 chains on 4 threads: 0.045 s against 0.091 s before, and 0.049 s without the band). The chains start from the best fit moved inside the band by a penalized least-squares fit, about 1.5 minutes at 211,000 ruptures, made once per problem and kept as `cru_clean_band10_start.npz` for the later runs and replicates. Exact tests check it under spike-and-slab, a log-box prior with swaps and the linear prior ("The band").

**Swap moves** (`--swap-cos 0.99`, always on in the notebook since October 2026) exchange the rates of near-duplicate pairs in the same bin (338,177 pairs with cosine above 0.99 on the corrected inputs with one measurement per section; 1,165,613 at 211,000 ruptures), one pass per recorded draw; they are exact, and let near-duplicates exchange the active role. Under spike-and-slab a pair does work only when one of its ruptures is on. A controlled test (October 2026; quick preset, inputs as given, balance term on, chains from zero, two replicates with and two without, same seeds) measured their effect. At 211,000 ruptures under spike-and-slab they cost no measurable time (44 s per run either way, about 3,500 accepted swaps per pass per chain) and gave the 43 ruptures on in at least 5% of draws a median 396 effective draws of 1,000 against 135 (2.3 times at 2e4, 373 against 150); the slow-mixing minority fell from 500–574 ruptures (32–33% of the rate) to 200–204 (19–20%), and the replicate-to-replicate spread of those ruptures' probabilities of a rate above 1e-5 from 0.060 to 0.017. Under θ every pair swaps on every pass (acceptance 1.000), which at 211,000 ruptures cost 59% more time per sweep (78 against 49 ms) for the same effective draws per second, though fewer slow-mixing ruptures (1,912–1,924 against 5,817–5,912). Neither prior's fit, total rate, bins or subsection totals moved beyond Monte Carlo noise, and the swap moves do not reduce the subsection totals' noise (7.4% and 7.6% between replicates at 211,000 ruptures), which comes from slower directions. **Transfer moves**, which shift rate between a rupture and up to four sub-ruptures reproducing its column, exist for the single box only (`build_transfers.py`, set aside in `cru_retired_scripts.zip`); they were exact and cheap but gave no measurable per-rupture gain at a 1e-12 floor.

**Convergence.** Even for perfectly independent draws the worst R̂ over 18,000 rates is above 1 (about 1.015 for 4 chains × 250 draws), so R̂ must be judged against that noise baseline, not against 1. Under spike-and-slab and θ a minority of ruptures switch on and off slowly and stay above it: in quick runs 0.3% of ruptures under spike-and-slab (carrying 16–18% of the total rate) and 1.1–1.3% under θ (23–24%), and in the very conservative θ run 4.5–4.7%, with the worst R̂ 1.35–1.38. Longer runs help them only slowly; moves that exchange the active role between alternative ruptures would. χ² and the total rate mix much better (R̂ near 1.01 at the quick preset), and spike-and-slab's number of ruptures on and fractions on are checked too.

**HMC cross-check** (`rhmc.py --param log`, set aside in `cru_retired_scripts.zip`). Under the single box at a floor of 1e-8 (plain likelihood), log-space HMC and Gibbs agreed rupture by rupture on mean log rate: standardized differences had mean +0.009 and sd 1.004, with 0.23% beyond 3 (0.27% expected), and the median absolute difference was 0.034 decades. HMC needed its mass from the Gibbs variances of log rate and its starting points from Gibbs end states; on one core, Gibbs delivered about 22 effective draws per rupture per second against 7–13 for HMC.

**Self-tests** (`gibbs.py --selftest`) compare against exact answers. They cover:
- a truncated normal 30 sd beyond its bound;
- two- and three-rate problems with walls under flat, exponential and log-uniform priors, with soft and hard edges;
- swap and transfer moves;
- per-rupture lower and upper box edges (θ's depths and the soft top);
- the spike-and-slab prior, by enumerating on/off states;
- the balance term's rows and bookkeeping;
- the band, under spike-and-slab, a log-box prior with swap moves and the linear prior;
- the retired hierarchical prior.

All pass on both the C and NumPy backends, and multi-threaded Gibbs is bit-identical to single-threaded Gibbs. Two exact speed-ups (November 5th, 2026), bit-identical in seven configurations: with the paleo misfit in years or the bins in log10 and no slip-rate soft band, the soft band's bound sums run over the column's weighted rows only, the others' coefficients being 0; and with the CFM's slip rates, each row's current split-misfit term is cached, so a column pass computes only the new ones. gibbs.py also runs OpenBLAS single-threaded, whose idle threads otherwise spin. In the default setup (the CFM's slip rates, the paleo misfit in years, positivity; 8 chains on 4 threads at 211,000 ruptures) a sweep takes 0.039–0.040 s against 0.045–0.047 s before, 14% less. The notebook runs `--selftest current`: only what its default uses, the per-rupture lower and upper edges, spike-and-slab, the balance term, swap moves under a log-box prior, and the band; the full suite (about 4 minutes) is for after any change to the sampler.

## Earlier priors

The workflow's earlier priors were log-uniform boxes ending at a common floor. They still run in `gibbs.py`, for comparison, but are not part of the workflow's tests. Their analyses, on the earlier inputs and counting each slip-rate subsection as its own measurement, established the robust products and showed why the floor could not be chosen from the data.

**Per-bin boxes** (`--prior binbox-floor:-12`, the default until October 2026). Each bin's ruptures had a log-uniform box from the same soft top at R_j down to a hard floor at 1e-12 common to all bins (`binbox:W` ends each box W decades below its R_j instead). They mixed well (about 980 effective draws per 1,000 for nearly every rupture) and fitted better than the single box before them (χ² 557 against 599 at 10 decades deep), but their depth, 8.5–9.3 decades below the targets, made θ_j ≫ 1, so the prior pushed the bin totals up. On the corrected inputs, counting each subsection, they gave χ² 663 ± 29 (2.6 sd above the fit check then, 446 ± 83), bins a median 23% above their targets (−86% to +85%), about 1,170 ruptures carrying 90% of the rate, and 80% of a typical bin's total carried by individually undetectable rates. Between floors of 1e-12 and 1e-10 their products moved little (subsection totals by a median 6%, exceedance at 1e-5 by 0.05 in log₁₀), but the floor or depth set the sparsity, and the data could not choose it. With one measurement per section they failed the fit check by 26 sd (547 ± 32 against 126 ± 16), after which the default changed to spike-and-slab.

**Tried again at 211,000 ruptures (late October 2026).** The per-bin boxes were briefly a notebook option again (`binbox-floor:{FLOOR}:0:soft`, from `FLOOR` up to the soft top), then taken out for simplicity. Conservative runs (8 chains × 5,000 sweeps, thin 8; `FLOOR = -12`, each subsection its own measurement, no balance term) on the 211,000-rupture inputs and on the SRunc1 inputs (slip-rate uncertainties halved) mixed well, both passing the convergence checks with their replicates agreeing, but fitted far worse than spike-and-slab with the same settings. χ² was 10,095 ± 144 and 10,153 ± 137 against the fit check's 2,395 ± 185 (spike-and-slab: 1,110 and 1,810). The bins sat a median 2.28 and 1.89 times their targets (0.03–3.32 and 0.02–2.46; spike-and-slab 1.05 and 1.19), rising with each bin's number of ruptures, and the total rates were 0.0696 and 0.0657 (targets 0.0372; spike-and-slab 0.0378 and 0.0417). With every rupture always on, 88–99% of each bin's and subsection's total sat in rates individually invisible to the data (the implied prior bin totals were 11 to 1,330 times the targets). That diffuse rate inflated the bins and put 113 and 69 slip-rate sections above twice their targets (3 under spike-and-slab), mostly sections with small targets relative to their uncertainties. Those weakly constrained sections follow the prior either way: spike-and-slab's sparsity leaves them near zero, and the boxes' pressure lifts them above their targets. A sweep cost about 7 times spike-and-slab's (0.17 s for 8 chains on 4 threads).

- **Edges:** hard edges instead of soft ones raised individual rates by about 0.55 decades and fitted worse (χ² 1,517 against 986 at a 1e-8 floor). A soft lower edge of sd 1 decade acts as about one extra decade of depth.

**The misfit cap** (removed in October 2026). This optional model class kept only models with χ² ≤ X (by default the fit check's expected value; 447 when these runs were made), sampled by coordinate hit-and-run on that set, so draws sat on its boundary and X was in effect the misfit chosen. It was a separate model class, not a filter: under the plain likelihood no draw of the single box at 1e-12 satisfied χ² ≤ 447 (599 ± 28). With the single box it removed the over-prediction while moving individual rates by only about 0.05 decades (total rate 0.0260 against 0.0268) and made sums less sensitive to the floor; tighter caps (X = 200 and 100) pulled the bins toward their targets but did not converge in 2,400 sweeps. The pressure-free priors pass the fit check under the plain likelihood, so the cap was no longer needed, and the Gaussian likelihood is now the only one.

**Hierarchical lognormal prior** (`--prior lognormal`, retired). log₁₀ m_i ~ Normal(μ_bin, s²), with one centre per magnitude bin and one shared within-bin spread under broad hyperpriors (s uniform on 0.05–5 decades, each μ_bin normal with mean −6 and sd 10 decades), sampled exactly, with centred and non-centred hyperparameter updates (ASIS), and passing its self-tests. It was not double-dipping: the bins come from the forward model, and the centres and spread were estimated jointly with the rates. A pilot (χ² 257, total 0.0231, spread about 1.55 decades) and a long run (4 chains × 36,000 sweeps: χ² 239, total 0.0230, bins 1.02× their targets, about 40 ruptures above 1e-4) found roughly the pressure-free answer, but the hyperparameters never converged: the spread's R̂ was 1.86, and it was still rising (from 2.10 to 2.33 decades between the halves of the kept draws) while the centres fell. Widening the spread while lowering the centres keeps each bin's total fixed, so this was a slide along a ridge, driven by the same pull toward sparsity; partial non-centring (`--pnc-pilot`) and a fixed spread (`--s-fixed`) did not help. Spike-and-slab replaced it as the way to let the data set the sparsity.

## Why not a linearly uniform prior

A prior uniform in each rate on [0, 1] encodes the expectation that rates are spread linearly, and in 18,356 dimensions it is far from neutral. Positivity turns the directions the data cannot see into a cone whose volume grows as roughly t^18,355 when all predictions scale by t. That volume overwhelms a likelihood with σ = 1.

| flat prior on [0, 1] | plain likelihood | uniform on χ² ≤ 447 |
|---|---|---|
| χ² | 10,274 ± 160 (log-HMC: 10,262 ± 156) | 446.3–447.0 (on the boundary) |
| predictions projected on the data | 1.709 ± 0.008 | 1.024 ± 0.002 |
| total rate | 0.0518 | 0.0267 |
| per-rupture marginals | exponential (median sd/mean 0.999) | exponential (median sd/mean 1.004) |

Gibbs reaches the flat-prior state within its first four sweeps from the best fit, and log-HMC reaches it independently. Per row, flat-prior predictions range from about 0.1 to 5.7 times the observed values. Its rate population is compressed into roughly 1e-8 to 1e-4, peaking near 1e-6, rather than spreading over many decades. Its subsection totals are 2.1× those of the single log-box at 1e-12 (median; 5–95%: 0.7–3.1×), and its rupture medians sit about 1.3 decades above those of the 1e-8 log-box.

**With each rate's top at its bin's target (October 2026).** `--prior linear` (a `gibbs.py` option, for the command line) makes each rupture's rate uniform from 0 to its bin's target rate R_j, drawn by the same exact truncated-normal updates, with no spike and no floor. The top caps each rupture, not the bin: a priori each bin's total is n_j R_j / 2, 82 to 1,854 times its target at 2e4 and 86 to 11,344 times at 211,000 ruptures, and the cone still overwhelms the data (quick preset, inputs as given, one measurement per slip-rate section):

So a soft top would change nothing: the inflation comes from the number of ruptures each bin adds up, which a cap on single ruptures cannot touch.

**Halfway between linear and log-uniform (October 2026).** `--prior halfway` (a `gibbs.py` option, for the command line) draws each rupture's rate from a 50/50 mix of a uniform range and a log-uniform range, both from 10^`FLOOR` up to its bin's target rate R_j with the soft top (`halfway:A:TOP:W` sets the floor, the top and the uniform part's share W), so each value has a 50% prior chance of coming from either; every rupture is always on. (A 50/50 bet on the whole population instead, every rate linear or every rate log-uniform, would be settled by the data: the linear prior's fit is worse by thousands of χ² units, so its posterior weight would be nil.) Its two ends are the linear prior above and the per-bin log-uniform boxes down to the same floor (`binbox-floor:-10`). It is sampled exactly in log rate, where the mix has density mA e^(z − z_top) + mB, each part normalized over the range and the top's shoulder. Halfway in prior probability is not halfway in the result: the halfway prior lands almost on its log-uniform end. Most rates sit far below R_j, where the log-uniform part's density (proportional to 1/m) exceeds the uniform part's (flat) by orders of magnitude, so the data assign almost every value to it. In the posterior, 1.5% of the values come from the uniform part at 2e4, carrying 28% of the total rate, and 0.4% at 211,000 ruptures, carrying 7%: the few largest rates. Both ends push the bin totals up, because every rupture is always on with a range reaching R_j: the log-uniform boxes by θ_j − 1 with θ_j = n_j / (W_j ln 10), which grows with each bin's rupture count ("θ = 1: pressure-free per-bin boxes"), hence far more at 211,000 ruptures. Spike-and-slab avoids that by estimating each bin's fraction on. The halfway prior mixes as fast as the linear prior (R̂ 1.00, and at the quick preset the 5th percentile of the rupture rates' effective draws is about 0.8 per draw), but a sweep costs about six times spike-and-slab's: 140 ms for 8 chains and 262 ms for 16 at 211,000 ruptures, recording every 8 sweeps. An exact test (`selftest_halfway`, part of `--selftest current`) checks two rates with different floors and soft tops against grid integrals, with the two parts normalized on the grid, independently of the kernel's closed forms; its answers move by several times its Monte Carlo error when the share changes from 0.5 to 0.3 or 0.7.

The flat-prior runs also served as the first sampler validation. Gibbs and log-space HMC agreed on all 18,356 posterior means: standardized differences had mean −0.020 and sd 1.000, with 0.23% beyond 3. Gibbs delivered about 100 effective draws per rupture per second against 10–15 for HMC. Reflective HMC in linear space failed outright. Each rate lies within a few percent of its conditional sd from the wall at zero, so trajectories made about 28,000 reflections per chain per 0.1 time units. Acceptance was zero at step sizes well below the stability limit, and reached 0.50 only at h = 3e-4. This is why every sampler here works in log rate.

## Randomized best fits (October 2026)

`randomized_nnls.py` (from the command line; the notebook's `RANDOMIZED_NNLS` option, which ran it in place of the sampler, was retired on November 3rd, 2026) runs best fits to noise-perturbed data: each the best nonnegative fit (NNLS, as cleaning computes it) to the data plus a fresh draw of their stated noise on the cleaned rows, so with one measurement per slip-rate section and the MFD bins at `MFD_UNCERTAINTY`'s level, as the sampler sees them. Its figures draw each target against the fits' median prediction, with their 16–84% and 2.5–97.5% ranges and the unperturbed best fit: the slip-rate sections, the magnitude bins and the paleo rows (`fig_rnnls_slip_rates.png`, `fig_rnnls_mfd.png` and `fig_rnnls_paleo_rates.png` in `RUN_DIR/rnnls/`, each with its table). `rate_vectors.csv` there gives the fits' mean rate per rupture and the share of fits in which each rupture is nonzero, and with the model's rupture set the notebook writes the fits' mean rates as an OpenSHA solution's (`rnnls/rates.csv`). The fits run in parallel processes, one thread each; fit k's noise comes from the k-th seed spawned from `--seed`, so the results do not depend on how many run at a time. A self-test (`randomized_nnls.py --selftest`) checks that for a full-rank problem whose rates all lie well above 0 the fits scatter as the posterior under a flat prior would (variances within 9% of σ²(GᵀG)⁻¹ over 400 fits, around the best fit), and that the fits do not depend on the number of processes.

**What it is.** The best fit's sampling distribution: how far the best fit moves when the noise in the data changes. For a linear model with Gaussian errors and no active constraint that is also the posterior under a flat prior; here it is not:
- **It never counts the models that fit equally well.** The data constrain at most as many combinations of rates as there are rows, and every fit has at most one nonzero rate per row. The noise does reshuffle which ruptures carry the rate (at 2e4, 40 fits used 1,579 different ruptures, with a median 21% of each fit's nonzero rates on ruptures the unperturbed best fit uses), but the solver picks each fit's ruptures, with no weighting among the alternatives.
- **It has no prior,** so where the data are silent the solver decides.
- **It misses where the posterior's probability lies:** among the far more numerous models that fit slightly less well ("Fits and the prior's lower end"). Each fit is an optimum, with the best fit's habits, each bin's larger ruptures among them.
- **Positivity biases it upward.** Noise that pushes a near-zero prediction down cannot be followed, since no rate goes below 0, while noise that pushes it up is; so the fits' totals sit above the best fit's, the more so the more rows have targets near 0 (at 211,000 ruptures, 331 of the 507 slip-rate sections' targets are under one uncertainty).

## Balance between over- and underprediction (October 2026; `DOWN_THE_MIDDLE`, off by default since early November 2026)

The question: penalize fits in which a data type's targets are mostly underpredicted (or mostly overpredicted), for the slip-rate sections, the MFD bins and the paleo rows separately. Counting over- against underpredictions is the sign test, a standard check of residuals that assumes independent errors; it is not known as a term in an inversion's objective. Putting it there makes a loss-based ("generalized") posterior, whose weight must be calibrated against the noise. A count also changes in steps, so NNLS cannot use it. And with the weight proposed for it, the zero model's misfit for the data type spread over its measurements, each unit of imbalance would cost 20, 76 and 18 in χ² for the slip sections, bins and paleo rows at 2e4 (3.5, 65 and 14 at 211,000 ruptures), so even a model matching the truth would pay over 100 from chance imbalance at 2e4.

What was tested instead is the smooth analogue: each data type's summed residual, in units of each measurement's uncertainty, penalized as w (Σ residuals)², which targets mean rather than median balance. It is one extra row of G and d per data type, so it works unchanged in the sampler and in NNLS, and the randomized best fits rebuild its targets from each fit's own perturbed data. Two weights: the proposed rule (the zero model's summed residual costs the zero model's misfit for the type: w = Σ d² / (Σ targets)²), which came to 3.4, 1.0 and 1.3 times (3.2, 1.0 and 1.3 at 211,000 ruptures) the calibrated weight, which counts each type's total as one more measurement (w = 1 / N_type). - **The weights.** The natural unit is one extra measurement per data type: under the stated noise a type's summed residual (or its count of over- minus underpredictions) is about ±√N by chance, so (Σ residuals)² / N (or (over − under)² / N) is a standardized statistic squared, weighing as much as one datum in χ²; that is the calibrated weight, and a model matching the truth pays about 1 per type. On that scale, though, a large type all one-sided costs far more than a small one. The equal-badness weight makes it equally bad for any type to be all underpredicted: it penalizes each type's relative shortfall, Σ residuals / Σ targets (−1 for the zero model in every type), with one common weight, the largest that keeps every type's term at or below one extra measurement, min over types of (Σ targets)² / N. The slip-rate sections set it at both sizes, so their term keeps the calibrated weight while the bins' gets 0.30 of it at 2e4 and 0.49 at 211,000 ruptures, and the paleo rows' 0.74 and 0.80. For counts the same rule gives a common weight of N for the smallest type, 18 for the bins, on each type's (over − under)² / N². Either way the term pulls only as far as the imbalance exceeds chance: at the posterior the slip sections' summed residual is about 10 of its chance spread at 211,000 ruptures, so even one measurement's weight pulls there.
- **The imbalance is the data's, not only the posterior's.** The best fit itself underpredicts 50 of the 68 slip-rate sections and 34 of the 40 paleo rows at 2e4 while overpredicting all 18 bins, and 428 of 507 sections and 56 of 65 paleo rows at 211,000 ruptures: sign-test z of 4 to 15, far beyond chance. The bins want less rate than the slip and paleo rows. Under spike-and-slab the paleo shortfall is the largest (z −6.6, a mean residual of −1.0 at 211,000 ruptures).
- **In the posterior the balance rows halve the mean shortfall.** With the proposed weight, slip from −0.47 to −0.24 and paleo from −0.63 to −0.33 at 2e4 (−0.58 to −0.29 and −1.00 to −0.48 at 211,000 ruptures), and the paleo rows' fit improves (χ² per measurement 1.29 to 1.14; 2.22 to 1.54). At 2e4 the total rate does not move; at 211,000 ruptures it rises 3%, with the bins 6% above their targets (χ² per bin 1.78 against 1.00). So the posterior meets the balance mostly by choosing better-placed or larger ruptures within each bin, the freedom "Fits and the prior's lower end" describes, and at 211,000 ruptures partly by raising the bins. The calibrated weight does about half as much, and the equal-badness weight about the same as the calibrated one (the slip sections, which set it, keep the calibrated weight; the bins and paleo rows get a little less).
- **Mean balance is not sign balance.** At 211,000 ruptures, 331 of the 507 sections have targets under one uncertainty; their predictions sit slightly below them, so the sections' signs stay unbalanced (−279) even as their mean residual halves. Balancing the signs would mean raising hundreds of low-slip sections just above their targets.
- **For the randomized best fits** the slip rates are already balanced in the mean; the balance rows mostly bring the bins down (from 18% to 12% over at 211,000 ruptures) and the paleo rows up.
- **As implemented** (`DOWN_THE_MIDDLE = True`, the notebook's default until late October 2026; `gibbs.py --down-the-middle`): the equal-badness weight. `cru_common.balance_rows` builds one row per data type, √A / T_t times the type's sum of rows (T_t its sum of targets), with target √A, so the row's residual is √A s_t; `gibbs.py` appends them to G and d, so the kernel is unchanged and every prior takes them, and the swap moves' column inner products include them (checked against the extended matrix to 4e-15 at 211,000 ruptures). The recorded χ² and predictions stay over the data rows, so the fit check, the row-block fit and the figures measure the fit to the data alone; the balance term (`balance`) and each type's relative shortfall (`balance_s`) are recorded on their own, and each run's log gives A, each type's share of a measurement and its mean shortfall. The best fit and the randomized best fits ignore it. An exact test (`selftest_balance`, part of `--selftest current`) checks the rows against a direct computation of the shortfalls, the zero model's cost A in every type, the weights, and the run's bookkeeping, under both weightings. Without the flag every prior's output is bit-identical to before. A quick 2e4 run with swap moves gave χ² 137 ± 11 over the data, a balance term of 15 on average, and shortfalls of −15% (slip-rate sections), +4% (bins) and −11% (paleo rows). `DOWN_THE_MIDDLE = 'zero-model'` (`--down-the-middle zero-model`; the notebook's default in late October 2026) gives each type its own A instead, its zero-model misfit, so s = −1 or +1 costs a type what the zero model costs it in χ²: the proposed weight of the table above, 1.6–3.4 times the equal-badness weight (A = 1382, 1375 and 717 for the slip-rate sections, bins and paleo rows at 2e4, against 408.5; 1787, 1162 and 883 at 211,000 ruptures, against 552.6). Without the option the equal-badness output is bit-identical to before (the run file records A once per type).
- **It weights the data, so it is a modelling decision.** The balance rows count each data type's total a second time: once more at the calibrated weight, 1–3.4 times more at the proposed one. That arbitrates the tension between the bins and the slip and paleo rows by a choice of weights, and the posterior's uncertainty in those totals is then understated by the same factor. As a check rather than a penalty, the sign counts and mean residuals per data type are cheap to report for any run.

## The band (early November 2026; `BAND = 10`, optional; positivity, `BAND = 'positive'`, the default; by row group since November 4th, 2026)

`gibbs.py --band F` (the notebook's `BAND = 10`, an order of magnitude either way; the default for a few days in early November 2026, before positivity) keeps the prediction of every data row with a positive target within a factor F of its target, from d/F to F d: each subsection's slip-rate row, each MFD bin and each paleo row (all 2,395 rows at 211,000 ruptures). It is a hard constraint, a prior on the rates that is 0 outside the band, for any prior and with or without the balance term. Each coordinate update draws from its conditional restricted to the rates that keep every row of its column inside the band; under spike-and-slab an "off" state the band forbids is never taken, nor an "on" one outside it; and a swap that would leave the band is rejected. Without it every run is bit-identical to before. An exact test (`selftest_band`, in the full self-test set) checks spike-and-slab, a log-box prior with swap moves and the linear prior's truncated normals against grid integrals restricted to the band, on both backends. The rows are banded per subsection, not per section mean: the √k that `SLIP_SECTIONS` divides a section's rows by cancels from the ratio of prediction to target, so every subsection must lie within a factor F of its section's slip rate, ends of sections included.

**The start.** At 211,000 ruptures the best fit leaves 121 subsection rows outside a factor 10 (predicted at or near 0); the NZSHM22 solution leaves 71, and each draw of the reference run about 1,100–1,250, all of them slip-rate rows. So the chains start from a least-squares fit with a penalty, growing in steps, on predictions outside a band a little narrower than the band (a factor F^0.9), which then lies inside it: χ² 93.5 from the best fit (an exact linear program found no start in 3 hours). Since early November 2026 the fit measures each rate in units of 1/‖G_i‖, which has the same minimum but converges 2.5 times faster (the column norms span 2e3 to 6e5), and stops as soon as every row lies within 10^0.95 of its target, since the start only has to be inside the band: about 1.5 minutes at 211,000 ruptures instead of 4–7, at the same χ². The fit is deterministic, so the first run on a problem keeps it beside the problem (`cru_clean_band10_start.npz`), and later runs with the same problem, band, start and bounds, the other replicates, take it from there; a fixed-seed run is bit-identical either way.

**What it changes** (211,000 ruptures; very conservative runs with the balance term's per-type weights and a slab floor of 1e-12, without and with the band): χ² 554 ± 24 → 447 ± 20 against the fit check's 590 ± 34 (−4.2 sd), the slip-rate sections 423 → 299, the bins 32 → 43 and the paleo rows 100 → 105; the bins a median 6% → 16% above their targets and the total rate 0.0379 → 0.0406 per year; ruptures on per draw 763 → 1,929 and the expected number above 1e-5 172 → 421; for the sections sharing ruptures with at most five others, draws without an event 92% → 0% and the posterior mean over the target 0.03 → 0.80; subsection event rates a median 1.6 times higher (5–95%: 0.7–33), mostly the isolated ones; the nine most isolated named faults' event rates from a median 1/8 of the NZSHM22 solution's to 1/1.2; and the slow-mixing minority from 5–6 ruptures to 24–26 (2% of the rate), with the run taking 9.4 hours instead of 4.1. The band is stricter than the data wherever a target is weak: a section whose target is under about one uncertainty would accept a prediction of 0, which the band forbids, so χ² falls below the fit check, which assumes the likelihood alone. With the band the balance term changes little (conservative runs: χ² 446, 434 and 439 with `'zero-model'`, `True` and `False`; the paleo rows fit worst without it, 122 against 105–107). The defaults since early November 2026 are the band, no balance term and a slab floor of 1e-10 (`BAND = 10`, `DOWN_THE_MIDDLE = False`, `FLOOR = -10`).

**Positivity, a gentler alternative** (early November 2026; `BAND = 'positive'`, `gibbs.py --positive`, spike-and-slab only). Every prediction with a positive target must be above zero in every draw, with no other limit: at least one of the ruptures in each subsection's slip-rate row, each MFD bin and each paleo row is on. Since the coefficients are nonnegative and an on rate is at least the slab floor, that is the same as a lower limit of half the smallest amount one switched-on rupture can add to the row (its coefficient times the floor), with no upper limit, which the band's machinery keeps: a rupture can always switch on, and the last one on in a row cannot switch off. A start that predicts 0 for some rows gets, for each, the rupture crossing the most such rows switched on at twice its slab floor, at once and with no change to the fit worth mentioning. The band's exact test includes it (two spike-and-slab rates whose small targets leave both off 42% of the time without it: P(on) within 2.3 sd of the exact answer on both backends). Unlike the band, it does not keep predictions near their targets: an isolated fault's prediction can be far below its target, as long as it is not 0.

**A soft band** (November 3rd, 2026; `BAND_SOFT`, `gibbs.py --log-band`; off by default, with positivity only). A Gaussian penalty on each positive-target data row's log ratio of prediction to target, ½ (log₁₀(prediction / target) / τ)², τ = `BAND_SOFT` decades at 1 sd (1: within an order of magnitude at 1 sd, two at 2 sd; 2: within two orders at 1 sd), each slip-rate subsection's term divided by the k subsections of its section, so a section counts once. There is no coefficient in front of it, as there is none in front of the Gaussian misfit: both enter the log posterior with the same ½, and its width is its weight (the penalty scales as 1/τ², so `BAND_SOFT = 2` weighs a quarter of `BAND_SOFT = 1`). Its rows' weights add up to 590, as many as the data's measurements in the fit check (507 sections, 18 bins, 65 paleo rows), but their influence is very uneven. It ignores each row's uncertainty, so it acts where the targets are weak (1,497 of the 2,312 slip-rate rows at 211,000 ruptures have a target under one uncertainty), where the data would accept a prediction near zero; where a target is strong the Gaussian likelihood already holds the prediction far closer than a decade. A prediction of exactly zero is infinitely penalized, which is why it needs positivity. It is not quadratic in the rates, so the kernel keeps it exactly by Metropolis steps: the switch on or off takes the penalty's change exactly in its log odds, a slab move (the slice sampler's step, reversible under the rest of the target) is accepted by its change, and swaps by theirs. For the many off ruptures, bounds on the change settle the decision: on each penalized row, with t = G_ji m / y (m the proposed rate, y the current prediction), log(1 + t) lies between t − t²/2 and t, so the change lies between m s₁ − m² s₃ and m s₁ + m² s₂, where s₁, s₂ and s₃ are sums over the rupture's column of per-row coefficients kept for each chain (2wq/y, w(1 + max(−q, 0))/y² and w max(q, 0)/y², q the row's log ratio). The sums come from the same pass over the column as the dot products, for four chains at once (`col_dots_lw`), and most "stays off" decisions need no logarithm (the odds of switching on are bounded by a polynomial in the bounds); the rest are computed exactly. The draws are the exact ones: since November 4th, 2026 (S1, below) this replaces a per-chain bounds pass (`lw_bounds`), with bit-identical output. Its exact test (two spike-and-slab rates with weak targets and a swap pair, against a grid integral; in the full self-test set) passes on both backends; without the option every run is bit-identical to before. In 1,200-sweep runs at 211,000 ruptures (4 chains, with the monotonic MFD and the summed slip rates), against positivity alone and the band (factor 10):

| | positivity alone | soft band, τ = 1 | band 10 |

So it moves the weak-target faults most of the way to where the band puts them (from about a sixth of their targets to about 0.6, the band about 0.75), with the slip-rate rows fitting nearly as well and the MFD bins paying less, while rows the data pull far down can still stay there; at about 2.4 times the time per sweep (before November 4th, 2026; about 1.5 times since, below: 0.037 s against 0.025 s per sweep with 8 chains on 4 threads) and half the effective draws, it cost about five times as much per effective draw (about three times since). The lost effective draws are not the Metropolis steps': in 640 sweeps at 211,000 ruptures they rejected 10% of slab moves.

**Its cost, profiled (November 4th, 2026).** On run `_1793693019`'s problem (positivity and the soft band; 8 chains, 640 sweeps, 4 threads), the sweeps took 97.5% of the run, the swap passes 0.8%, the fractions on 0.2% and the recording 1.6%. Off ruptures were 99.2% of the updates (about 1,600 of 204,538 on per draw; 1.4% of updates changed a rate, about 1,330 ruptures switching on and as many off per chain and sweep), and the old per-chain bounds pass took about 60% of the kernel's time, the column dot products 10% and the logarithms of each decision 6%; the bounds settled 99.95% of the off decisions. Fusing the bounds into the dot products' pass (S1) cut the time per sweep by 44–45%, with fixed-seed output bit-identical (every array, recorded and kept draw) on four setups: the soft band alone (0.066 → 0.037 s per sweep, 8 chains, 4 threads), with the summed slip rates (0.084 → 0.046), with the monotonic MFD, the summed slip rates and the plate rate (0.100 → 0.056), and positivity alone (unchanged code path). Per chain and sweep, before S1: the soft band 7.6 ms (16 chains, 4 threads), with the monotonic MFD 7.9, the summed slip rates 9.7, the plate rate 10.3 (their rows lengthen the columns); positivity alone 3.1 ms with 8 chains on 4 threads and 2.5 with 16 (the dot products serve four chains a pass, so two chains a thread waste half), and with the soft band 6.5 ms on 8 threads against 7.7 on 4.

**Skipping the column, tried and dropped (S2, November 4th, 2026).** Without the soft band, an off rupture's decision could be made without reading its column whenever a bound from the residual's norm (|G_i·r| ≤ ‖G_i‖ ‖r‖) already guarantees that it stays off: 73% of off decisions in the profile. Built with the slab draw and the uniform drawn first (the same draws in the same order), it gave bit-identical output with positivity, the band, and positivity with the monotonic MFD, slip sums and plate rate, and passed the full self-test set, but it was 7–10% slower (0.028 → 0.030 s per sweep with 8 chains, 0.045 → 0.050 with 16, on 4 threads): the column is read once for a thread's whole group of chains, so skipping one chain's dot product saves nothing while another chain needs it, and the bookkeeping costs more. It was not kept.

**A monotonic MFD** (early November 2026; `gibbs.py --mfd-monotonic`; the notebook's `MFD_MONOTONIC`, on by default, until November 4th, 2026, when it became a command-line option only, and again, as before and on by default, since November 10th at the user's request: without it the posterior mean's MFD already falls wherever its targets do, in run `_1793693019`, though 99% of that run's draws break the rule somewhere, and it added 5% to the time per sweep). In every draw, wherever a magnitude bin's target is not above the next lower bin's, the bin's total rate is not above that bin's either; where the target rises (with the NZSHM22 inputs only from M6.8–6.9 to M6.9–7.0, where the total MFD is shared with off-fault seismicity), the rise is capped instead (since November 3rd, 2026; before, the pair was left free): the upper bin's total is at most ρ times the lower's, with ρ fixed at the targets so that the cap sits 0.89 sd (`gibbs.py --mfd-rise-sd`, from the command line only) above the pair's target difference, the sd that of the difference from the two bins' uncertainties. The other pairs' walls (upper total at most the lower's) sit 0.30–1.04 sd above their own target differences, a median of 0.89, so the rising pair is held as the typical pair is: ρ = 1.427 for M6.8–6.9 at 211,000 ruptures, against a target ratio of 1.28 (the posterior's ratio was 1.08–1.78 without the cap, above 1.427 in 41% of the band run's draws). The cap is a ratio rather than a difference, so it limits the MFD's shape, not its level, and stays the same at any overall rate; a negative `--mfd-rise-sd` leaves rising pairs free. A pair across a bin removed by cleaning is left free. At 211,000 ruptures that is 16 pairs kept from rising, from M6.9–7.0 up, and one with its rise capped. It can be combined with the band or positivity, or used alone, under any prior but the retired hierarchical one. It is kept by constraint rows after the rows in the likelihood, one per pair, each ε × (ρ × lower bin's total − upper bin's total) with ε = 1e-9 (ρ = 1 except for a rising pair), held at or above 0 by the band's machinery; their effect on χ² is at most about 1e-20, far below rounding, so the likelihood is unchanged, and swaps, the on/off steps and both backends respect the constraint without any change to the kernel. A start that breaks it has each offending bin scaled down to ρ times its lower neighbour (the best fit at 211,000 ruptures does not break it). The band's exact test includes it (two rates alone in two bins, with targets that favour the upper one: P(on) and E[log10 m | on] within 1.2 sd of the exact answer on both backends, against 0.44 and 0.99 without it). In 960 sweeps at 211,000 ruptures with the band, without it 1,379 of the 480 draws' 7,680 pairs had the upper bin above the lower, and none with it, at no extra cost per sweep; a run without the option is bit-identical to before.

**By row group** (November 4th, 2026; `SLIP_BAND`, `SLIP_BAND_SOFT` (both out of the notebook since November 10th), `PALEO_BAND`, `PALEO_BAND_SOFT`; `gibbs.py --slip-band`, `--paleo-band`, `--slip-log-band`, `--paleo-log-band`; on November 5th `PALEO_BAND` and `PALEO_BAND_SOFT` left the notebook, as `PALEO_MISFIT = 'years'`, the default, keeps each paleo prediction above zero by itself, its misfit being infinite at 0; they remain `gibbs.py` options). The band, positivity and the soft band acted on every row with a positive target, the MFD bins among them. The bins' targets are strong and their misfit already holds them, so a band there counts them twice. The notebook's `BAND` and `BAND_SOFT` were therefore split into one hard limit (`'positive'`, a factor, or `None`) and one soft band (a width in decades, with that group's positivity) for each of the two groups the data leave loose: the subsections' slip-rate rows and the paleo rows. The MFD bins get none. The defaults, `'positive'` for both and no soft band, differ from the old default only in leaving the bins without a lower limit that never bound. `Target.set_groups` sets the limits row by row through the band's machinery (positivity as `set_positive` sets it, a factor as `set_band` does, possibly a different factor for each group), `band_start` and its cache take a factor per row, and `positive_start` switches ruptures on only for the rows with positivity. The older flags (`--positive`, `--band`, `--log-band`) still act on every positive-target row, for older runs and the bit-identity tests, and cannot be combined with the new ones. The paleo misfit's self-test (below) checks `set_groups` against `set_positive` and `set_band`.

## The paleo rows' misfit (November 4th, 2026; `PALEO_MISFIT`, `'years'` by default since November 5th, `'gaussian'` before; `gibbs.py --paleo-misfit`)

**What the inputs hold.** Each paleo row of `cru_A`/`cru_D` is the rupture set's `paleo_constraint_data.json` in a Gaussian form (checked exactly on all 65 rows and 845,083 coefficients). The target is `bestEstimate` divided by the one-sigma `stdDev`, which is half the width of the site's one-sigma range. Each coefficient is the UCERF3 probability that the rupture would be seen at the site, divided by the same sd. That probability is read from the JSON's table, bilinearly, at the rupture's magnitude and at the site subsection's position along the rupture as a fraction of its length from the nearer end; the slip-based displacement-magnitude function is not used. So a row compares the model's rate of detectable events at the site with the analysis's rate. The JSON gives more than the Gaussian uses: the rate's 15.865/84.135% points (`ONE_SIGMA`) and 2.275/97.725% points (`TWO_SIGMA`), and `bestEstimate`, which is 1 over the mode of the recurrence-interval PDF in years (confirmed in the upstream code; the analysis was done in years and its last step took 1 over everything).

**The ranges are lopsided.** Within one sigma, a site's range runs a median 1.9 times as far below its best estimate as above it (2.7 times in log rate); at two sigma the upper tail is the long one (in log rate, the 2σ point lies a median 1.81 times the 1σ distance out below and 2.81 times above). The Gaussian instead uses a symmetric sd, a median 0.35 of the best estimate, and caps a site's misfit: a prediction of zero costs it only about 8. Against their own asymmetric one-sigma ranges, the posterior means of the newer runs leave 32–40% of the sites below and 2–3% above (16% each way if well calibrated); the reference run with the balance term, 23% and 6%.

**The curves.** Each site gets a sinh-arcsinh curve in log rate u = ln y (four parameters; `paleo_misfit.py`). Its PDF of the interval T = 1/y in years has its mode at 1/`bestEstimate` exactly, and the four quantiles are fitted by least squares in u. At the 211,000-rupture inputs all 65 curves fit, with a worst quantile error per site of a median 0.0018 decades; two sites exceed 0.05 decades and are flagged, Hope Conway (0.055) and Wairau Zinke (0.052). Fitted to the four quantiles alone, the curves' modes in years agree with 1/`bestEstimate` within 0.02 decades at 85% of sites (median +0.001 decades), against 8–26% for the other centres tried (1 over the mean interval, the mode or mean of the rate, the mode in log rate): `bestEstimate` is the mode in years. A predicted rate y is scored by −2 ln p_T(1/y), measured from its minimum (0 at the best estimate), on χ²'s scale; half of it enters the log posterior. As a density in years, which is the likelihood when the analysis's prior was flat in years, it penalizes rates below the best estimate (longer intervals) more than rates above it: at a site's lower one-sigma point a median 2.46, at its upper one 0.42, against 1 each under the Gaussian. A density in log rate would sit between the two (`--paleo-misfit log`, command line only).

**How it is kept.** The paleo rows leave the Gaussian likelihood: they are scaled by ε = 10⁻⁹ in G and d, as constraint rows are, so their Gaussian share falls below 10⁻¹⁵ while the sampler still tracks their predictions. Each site's half misfit is tabulated in log rate (from 30 natural-log units below its best estimate to 14 above, step 0.01) with its slopes, for a cubic Hermite interpolant, continued linearly beyond the table, with κ, the largest |f''| of the interpolant. The kernel keeps it through the soft band's machinery: the paleo rows' change enters `lw_change` and `lw_swap` exactly, and for off ruptures their share of the bounds, f'(v) t ± (max(∓f', 0) + κ) t²/2 per row from f(v + L) − f(v) ∈ f'L ± κL²/2 with L = ln(1 + t) ∈ [t − t²/2, t], is added to the coefficients `col_dots_lw` sums, so the off decisions stay as cheap. With the soft band, the paleo rows' own soft band is not allowed (the misfit already scores each paleo prediction). The swap pairs' stored inner products lose the paleo rows' share. Each recorded draw records the paleo misfit (`paleo_misfit`) beside χ², which still counts the paleo rows' Gaussian misfit, for comparison with runs without it; the recorded predictions are unscaled first, so the figures and `block_fit.py` read them as before. Its exact test (two spike-and-slab rates, one in a weak Gaussian row and both in a paleo row scored by a lopsided curve, with swaps, against a grid integral of prior × likelihood × e^(−f)) passes on both backends: P(on) and E[log₁₀ m | on] within 0.3 sd of the exact answer in C and 1.8 sd with NumPy. Without the option every run is bit-identical to before.

**What it would have said about earlier runs.** At their posterior means, the paleo rows' misfit under the Gaussian, the same curves' density in log rate, and their density in years: the soft band alone (`_1793693019`) 92.6, 144.1 and 193.8; positivity alone (`_1793699115`) 94.9, 209.7 and 272.8; the reference run with the balance term 65.1, 175.0 and 207.2. Almost all of it comes from the sites below their best estimate (89% of them in the soft-band run, 192 of its 194). The balance term lifted the median ratio of prediction to target (0.87, against 0.66–0.69 without it), but left a few sites near zero, which the faithful misfit penalizes heavily.

**What it does** (short runs at 211,000 ruptures: 8 chains, 1,600 sweeps, burn-in 480, the same seed; the soft band on the slip-rate rows (τ = 1), positivity on both groups, the summed slip rates). With `PALEO_MISFIT = 'years'` against `'gaussian'`: the paleo misfit in years averaged 138.2 per draw against 451.4 (scoring the Gaussian run's draws with the same curves); the paleo rows' Gaussian χ² fell too, 105.6 against 131.8; the sites below their own one-sigma ranges went from 40% to 23%, those above from 3% to 8% (16% each way if well calibrated); and the posterior mean's median ratio of prediction to best estimate rose from 0.65 to 0.72. The cost falls on the slip-rate rows (χ² 272.2 against 260.5) and the MFD bins (53.4 against 44.5; the bins a median 1.202 of their targets against 1.164, the largest 1.278 against 1.237), and the total rate rose from 0.0417 to 0.0424 per year: the tension between the paleo rows and the bins remains, but the paleo rows now pull with their actual information. The time per sweep is unchanged (0.044 s against 0.045 s). The numbers come from short runs, so they are indicative.

## The MFD bins' misfit in log10 (November 5th, 2026; `MFD_MISFIT`, `'gaussian'` by default; `gibbs.py --mfd-misfit`)

**What the inputs hold.** Each MFD bin row of `cru_A`/`cru_D` is a Gaussian on the bin's total rate: the same coefficient, 1/σ_j, on every rupture in the bin, and the target R_j/σ_j, with σ_j between 9.9% and 18.9% of R_j at 211,000 ruptures (as the inputs give them; `MFD_UNCERTAINTY` sets their level). So a bin 20% above its target costs as much as one 20% below.

**The misfit in log10.** With `MFD_MISFIT = 'log10'` each bin's total y is scored instead by ((log10 y − log10 R_j) / s_j)², centred on R_j (the median of the implied log-normal), with s_j = log10(1 + σ_j / R_j), the width the soft top of the rupture prior already uses (0.04–0.08 decades). In ratio rather than difference: a bin 20% high costs a little less than one 20% low (1.9 against 2.3 sd at σ_j = 10%, where the Gaussian gives 2.0 either way). An empty bin would cost infinitely much, so each bin is kept above zero (positivity, a third group beside the slip-rate and paleo rows), which at the inputs' uncertainty level binds no draw (none has taken a bin below 0.53 of its target) but forbids the empty bins seen with `MFD_UNCERTAINTY = 'two-measurements'`.

**How it is kept.** The bins' rows leave the Gaussian likelihood, scaled by ε = 10⁻⁹ as the paleo rows are with `PALEO_MISFIT = 'years'`, and each bin's log misfit goes through the soft band's per-row weights, 1/(2 (s_j ln 10)²) on its log ratio, kept exactly by the same Metropolis steps; no new kernel code. The swap pairs' stored inner products lose the bins' share. Each recorded draw records the term (`mfd_misfit`, on χ²'s scale) beside χ², which still counts the bins' Gaussian, for comparison; `block_fit.py` scores the MFD block in log10 for such runs. The fit check still counts one measurement per bin: in its own units a log-normal residual is still χ²₁. Its exact test (two spike-and-slab rates, a Gaussian row and a bin row holding both, scored in log10, with positivity and swaps, against a grid integral) passes on both backends, within 1.4 sd of the exact answers; without the log term the same test fails by 39–272 sd. Without the option every run is bit-identical to before.

**What it does** (short runs at 211,000 ruptures: 8 chains, 1,600 sweeps, burn-in 480, the same seed; the CFM's slip rates, positivity on the slip-rate and paleo rows, `PALEO_MISFIT = 'years'`, no soft band and no summed slip rates). With `MFD_MISFIT = 'log10'` against `'gaussian'`: the bins rise a little, as the log form charges less above the targets, where they sit, to a median 1.116 of their targets against 1.101 (the largest 1.207 against 1.161, the smallest 1.022 against 1.000); the total rate is 0.0408 per year against 0.0400; the bins' Gaussian χ² 36.2 against 28.0, their log misfit 32.2 against 26.7. The rest barely moves: the slip rates' split misfit 490 against 489, the paleo misfit in years 118.4 against 120.3 (their Gaussian 90.8 against 92.6). The smallest bin total in any draw is 0.69 of its target (0.54 under the Gaussian), so positivity never bound. The time per sweep is the same (0.067 s against 0.066 s, 8 chains on 2 threads, beside another job). The runs are short, so the numbers are indicative.

## Slip rates from the CFM (November 4th, 2026; `CFM_SLIPRATES`, `True` by default since November 5th, `False` before; `preliminaries.py --cfm-sliprates`)

**What the inputs hold.** Each subsection's slip-rate row of `cru_A`/`cru_D` (raw row j is the rupture set's subsection with FaultID j) is a Gaussian: its coefficients are each rupture's average slip (`average_slips.csv`) over σ in m/yr, and its target is 0.8 × `SlipRate` / σ, with σ = 2 × `SlipRateStdDev` (checked on every row; the 8 subsections with an SD of 0 use σ = the rate itself). The inversion's 0.8 scales the rates, and `SlipRateStdDev` is round((SR_max − SR_min)/2, 2) from the NZ Community Fault Model, so σ, twice the half range, spans the whole range from SR_min to SR_max on both sides. `SlipRate` is the CFM's SR_pref (550 of 557 parent faults exactly, the other 7 within 0.005 mm/yr of rounding). The CFM's ranges are lopsided: the sd above the mode over the sd below is a median 1.00, but 0.50 to 3.00 across the 5–95% of faults, and 157 faults are beyond a factor 1.5 either way. The Gaussian loses that.

**The split normals.** With `CFM_SLIPRATES = True`, cleaning rebuilds each slip-rate row from its parent fault's line of the CFM (`NZ_CFM_v1_0.csv`, matched by name; `cfm_sliprates.py`) as a split normal in the slip rate itself, in mm/yr (linear, as the rates are measured in the field). SR_min and SR_max are read as 2 sd from SR_pref (the reading agreed with colleagues, November 5th, 2026; the inputs read them as 1 sd) and the result doubled, as the inputs double theirs (`--cfm-sigmas 2`, `SLIP_SD_MULTIPLIER = 2`), and the whole distribution is scaled by the slip-rate factor, 0.8, the assumed fraction of each fault's slip rate that the ruptures take up (`SLIP_RATE_FACTOR`, below): its mode is 0.8 × SR_pref, its sd below the mode 0.8 × (SR_pref − SR_min), above it 0.8 × (SR_max − SR_pref), so after the doubling SR_min and SR_max sit at 1 sd. Until November 5th SR_min and SR_max were read as 1 sd, as the inputs read theirs, giving sds of 1.6 × the widths (the numbers below for σ_ref and the short runs are from that reading, unless stated); the 2-sd reading gives the same sds as the 1-sd reading with `SLIP_SD_MULTIPLIER = 1`. A fixed factor on an uncertain rate scales its uncertainty too (were the fraction 0.01, an sd of several mm/yr on 1% of the Alpine Fault's rate would make no sense); the inputs scale only their targets, so their σ is 1/0.8 too wide by that measure, and σ_ref, the mean of the two sides, is a median 0.80 of the inputs' σ (5–95%: 0.75–0.90). The rest of the difference comes from the inputs' rounding of their SD to 0.01 mm/yr (Pukekohe, 0.02–0.05 mm/yr, has an SD of 0.02 rather than 0.015) and from the 12 faults with a side of zero width, which takes the other side's (the Turi faults, Parihaka, White Island 1 and 2, among them). A fault with SR_min = SR_pref = SR_max gets an sd equal to its mode, as the inputs do (3 faults: Ararata, St Stephens, Takapu). Each row is then rescaled to σ_ref: coefficients average slip / σ_ref, target mode / σ_ref. So the rows, χ², the soft band's targets and the summed slip rates all work in that unit, and the summed slip rates (`SLIP_SUM`) sum the modes with σ_ref as each sd (`plate_rate.cross_sections` reads both from the rows). The weights (σ_ref/σ_lo)² and (σ_ref/σ_hi)² are saved with the problem.

**SR_pref as the mode or the median** (`CFM_PREF`, `'mode'` by default; `preliminaries.py --cfm-pref`). By default SR_pref is each split normal's mode: the most likely slip rate, as the maximum-likelihood estimate of a rate from its best offset and best age is, and the peak of the likelihood the inversion uses. With the sides' sds unequal, the split normal's median and mean then lie on the long side (George to Jacksons, base sds 1.5 below and 3.4 above: a median of 28.4 and a mean of 28.7 mm/yr against SR_pref 27.2, only 31% of the probability below it). `CFM_PREF = 'median'` reads SR_pref as the median instead: each split normal is shifted, its sds kept, towards its short side until its median is 0.8 × SR_pref (George to Jacksons, at the default sds 2.40 and 5.44: its mode from 21.76 to 19.81 mm/yr, its median from 23.71 to 21.76; 153 faults move by more than a quarter of their σ_ref); the 18 faults whose mode would then fall at or below 0 (slow faults with long upper ranges, Akatore and Central Balleny among them) keep SR_pref as the mode. A frequentist reading of SR_pref, the rate under which the geological record is most likely, favours the mode; the median of a Monte Carlo propagation of offset and age is usually that same number, so the median option is for a sensitivity test or a different reading by the CFM's compilers.

**The CFM's rounding** (`CFM_ROUNDING`, `True` by default since November 9th, 2026, `False` before; `preliminaries.py --cfm-rounding`). A slip rate written as 1 mm/yr is less precise than one written as 1.1. With `CFM_ROUNDING = True`, each fault's SR_pref, SR_min and SR_max are taken as known only to half a rounding step q, uniformly (variance q²/12), and that is added in quadrature to each side's sd: the mode's rounding, (0.8 q)²/12, on both sides, and each width's, a difference of two rounded numbers, c² · 2q²/12 (c each side's sd per unit of width, 0.8 at the defaults). Neither the CSV nor the CFM's spreadsheet keeps the written precision (its cells are numbers in the General format, so a 1.0 is stored as 1), so q comes from the shortest form of each number, and a fault's three numbers are taken as set at one precision, the finest among them: a 2 beside a minimum of 1.5 and a maximum of 2.8 counts as 2.0. At 211,000 ruptures the sds widen by a median factor of 1.005; 76 faults widen by more than 10% and none by more than 12% (Aka Aka, 0 / 0.02 / 0.03 mm/yr); 43 faults have all three numbers written as integers, at q = 1 mm/yr. (Counting each number at its own step instead would widen 241 faults by more than 10% and 12 by more than a factor 2, penalizing values that merely happen to be round.) Rounding is only what the written precision loses; a coarse expert judgement should already be in the CFM's range. 78 preferred rates end in .5, and some may be half-unit steps (q = 0.5) rather than 0.1; the digits cannot tell.

**Template slip rates** (`CFM_TEMPLATE_RHO`, 1 by default since November 9th, 2026, `None` before; `preliminaries.py --cfm-template-rho`). Many CFM slip rates were not measured on their fault but set by a rule or borrowed from neighbours: the CFM's quality codes (Seebeck et al., section 3.3.16) grade the robustness of each slip-rate determination, from 1 (fault-specific trench or marine data) through 2 (some fault-specific data, with comparisons with nearby faults and regional budgets), 3 (geomorphic expression and/or comparisons with nearby faults) and 4 (ascribed from geodesy or seismicity) to 5 (not derived or inferred). At 211,000 ruptures the 557 faults have 291 distinct triples of SR_pref / SR_min / SR_max, 205 of them unique; a few recur often (0.05 / 0.01 / 0.1 for 46 faults, 0.2 / 0.1 / 0.3 for 19, 1 / 0.5 / 1.5 for 16). Repetition tracks the quality code and the method: 33% of the faults with a unique triple are of code 1, against 5% of those whose triple 15 or more share, and half of the latter's comments describe assigned or inferred rates (method 6 of Lamarche and Barnes, 2005, for many offshore faults), against a tenth of the unique ones'. Faults set from one template are not independent measurements: if the template is off, all of them are. With `CFM_TEMPLATE_RHO = ρ`, faults of codes 2–5 whose three numbers are identical form a group of n with correlated errors, and each one's rows are weighted 1/√(1 + (n − 1)ρ); at ρ = 1 a group counts as one measurement, as a section's subsections do (the two weightings nest: a fault of k subsections in a group of n has each row weighted 1/√k × 1/√n). The weighting goes into the rows' recorded scale, so the slip-rate figures and the summed slip rates keep each fault's own sd, and the fit check counts each group once. At 211,000 ruptures, at ρ = 1, 299 faults of codes 2–5 fall in 74 groups (the largest, 0.05 / 0.01 / 0.1, of 46 of them), 1,251 subsection rows are weighted below 1 (the smallest 0.15), and the 551 sections (since sections stop at each fault's end, below) count as 329 measurements: the fit check's expectation falls from 634 ± 36 to 412 ± 27, and with the borrowed rates below to 382 ± 26 (299 measurements). Code 1 faults are never grouped. The CFM's ranges already widen with the code (a median (max − min)/SR_pref of 0.77 at code 1, 0.91 at 2 and 1.33 at 3), so no extra widening by code was added; the narrow ranges of code 4 (a median 0.50, ascribed from geodesy or seismicity) would be the place for one.

**Borrowed slip rates** (`CFM_RATE_LINKS`, `True` by default, acting only with `CFM_TEMPLATE_RHO`; `preliminaries.py --cfm-rate-links`). A quality code of 1 does not guarantee a measurement on that fault: Lyell South's comment reads "SR from Lyell", Parihaka's "SR inferred from Turi", and five Alpine sections take their rates, or half of them, from the Caswell and Doubtful fan offsets. With the links on, faults of any code whose rate the CFM borrowed from another's are correlated with it too (`cfm_sliprates.rate_links`), by three rules: a comment that names the source (another fault of the rupture set right after "from", "as", "same as", "equal to", "that for", "that of" or "with", or right before "fault", "source", "SR" or "rate", in a sentence about a rate; scaled copies such as "3/4 of the Hope Hurunui SR" count, while sentences of sums, differences and budgets, where an independent term enters, do not); an identical triple on traces within 5 km of each other (the White Creek faults, the Turi segments); or an identical triple on two faults of one family name (Ariel Bank and Ariel Bank 2, 17 km apart). The links' connected faults form clusters. Merging clusters with template groups would chain unrelated templates together: Clear Hills – Waikopiro shares 0.2 / 0.1 / 0.3 with 14 faults and its comment names Oruawharo – Mangatoro, which shares 2 / 1 / 3 with 8 others. So instead each fault's rows are weighted 1/√(Σⱼ Cᵢⱼ) = 1/√(1 + mρ), with Cᵢⱼ = ρ for the m faults it shares a template or a cluster with. For templates alone this is the template weighting exactly; a fault in both is correlated with both groups without the two groups being merged. At 211,000 ruptures: 158 links (21 from comments, 112 identical triples within 5 km, 25 on faults of one name); 62 faults are correlated with more faults than their template's, 32 of them of code 1. Every link is listed, with its rule and evidence, in `cfm_rate_links.csv` beside the cleaned problem. The weights are reckoned between slip-rate sections, which since November 5th, 2026, never span two faults ("Finding the sections"), so √k counts the copies on one fault's subsections and ρ the copies passed between faults, each once; where a section does span correlated faults (a section file, or no rupture set), `cfm_sliprates.section_factors` counts the correlation between sections, so faults sharing a section are not counted as correlated again. Some links are doubtful and kept by choice: Clear Hills – Waikopiro's numbers are a tenth of Oruawharo's, and Teviot's no longer match the revised Old Man Fault's.

**The slip-rate factor** (`SLIP_RATE_FACTOR`, 0.8 by default; `preliminaries.py --slip-rate-factor`, with the rupture set). The fraction itself is a setting: every slip-rate distribution is scaled by it as a whole, target and sd alike, the CFM's split normals or, with `CFM_SLIPRATES = False`, the inputs' Gaussians. The inputs (NZSHM22) put their 0.8 on the targets only, sd = 2 × `SlipRateStdDev` in unscaled mm/yr; read as an implementation error upstream in the NZSHM22 code (the uncertainty scaling postdating the slip-rate factor, and not taking it in), it is corrected here: since November 5th, 2026, the inputs' sd is 2 × 0.8 × SD by default, 0.8 of theirs (in units of the sd, their targets and coefficients × 1.25). The 8 subsections (3 faults) with an SD of 0, whose sd is the target itself, are left as they are. Every run before then used the inputs as given, which `SLIP_SD_MULTIPLIER = 2.5` with the factor at 0.8 reproduces bit for bit (2.5 × 0.8 = 2), without needing the rupture set; `tools/run_quick_checks.sh` pins it, so TESTING.md's values still apply.

**The doubling** (`SLIP_SD_MULTIPLIER`, 3 by default since November 9th, 2026, 2 before; `preliminaries.py --slip-sd-multiplier`, with the rupture set). The default became 3 after a colleague's new work on the slip-rate uncertainties; the scripts' own default, without the option, stays 2, the inputs' doubling, and the notebook always passes it. The inputs' sd is twice `SlipRateStdDev` (checked: exactly 2 on every subsection with an SD), and the CFM's sides are doubled the same way. The multiplier is a setting, so that the doubling can be switched off (1) when slip-rate uncertainties estimated from the data come in: each slip-rate sd is the multiplier × the slip-rate factor × its uncertainty, each side's width with `CFM_SLIPRATES = True`, `SlipRateStdDev` with `False`. The subsections with an SD of 0 keep sd = the target.

**How it is kept.** A residual e = prediction − target on a slip-rate row costs w(e) e² in χ², with w = (σ_ref/σ_lo)² below the target and (σ_ref/σ_hi)² above. Each term is convex and C¹ in the rate, so the log likelihood stays concave in each rupture's rate, and the kernel evaluates it exactly, with no bound or approximation: the slice update of an on rupture uses the exact likelihood (the endpoint bound of the slice sampler's quick floor still holds for a concave function), an off rupture draws its slab value first and gets its exact change in one pass over its column (with the soft band's bound sums in the same pass), the on/off decision uses the exact change, and so do the swaps. Recorded χ² is the split misfit; `block_fit.py` and the slip-rate figure weigh each residual by its side. Spike-and-slab only, without transfer moves. Its exact test (two spike-and-slab rates in two rows, one weighed 6 below its target and 0.15 above, with swaps, without and with the soft band on it, against a grid integral) passes on both backends, P(on) and E[log₁₀ m | on] within 1.4 sd of the exact answers; the same test run with the plain Gaussian fails by 100–400 sd. Without the option every run is bit-identical to before.

## The plate rate (early November 2026; `gibbs.py --plate-rate`, the notebook's `PLATE_RATE` until November 4th, 2026, off by default, and again since November 10th: `True` by default, a hard upper bound at `SLIP_RATE_FACTOR`; on the summed slip rates' strips since November 3rd)

**Since November 10th, 2026** the user brought `PLATE_RATE` back into the notebook as a switch: `True` (the default) keeps every strip's share of the plate rate at most `SLIP_RATE_FACTOR` (0.8 by default), the whole plate rate at the fraction of a fault's slip the ruptures take up, an upper bound only (`gibbs.py --plate-rate SLIP_RATE_FACTOR`, with the rupture set; `plate_rate.py --limits` counts the kept draws inside it); `False` leaves it out. Runs are named `_platemax0.8all` (the factor and `CROSS_SECTIONS`). In a short test at 2e4 with `MISFIT = 'half'`, positivity and `MFD_MONOTONIC`, every recorded draw kept every strip at or below 0.8, every pair of bins in order and the M6.8–6.9 rise within its cap (ρ = 1.46 there with these inputs).

**The idea.** Across the South Island the crustal faults take up much of the relative motion of the Pacific and Australian plates, so the slip on the faults crossed by a line from one plate to the other, added up as vectors, should be a share of the plate rate, with the rest left to deformation off the mapped faults. Elsewhere the subduction zones take up the rest, so the faults' share can only be smaller. The first version applied only between two cross-sections, the region's edges, which both quantities still use to place their strips. One passes through 167.83° E, 44.53° S, where the Alpine Fault goes offshore towards the Puysegur subduction zone. The other passes through 174.4258° E, 41.67585° S, midway between the Needles Fault's NE end (174.4533° E, 41.6900° S) and the SW end of the Wairarapa Fault's Cook Strait section, Wairarapa: Needles (174.3983° E, 41.6617° S), so it cuts across their step-over; beyond it the Hikurangi subduction zone takes up strain. Both subduction zones are handled outside this workflow. (In the first version, the NE edge was where the Hope Fault goes offshore, 173.75° E, 42.30° S.)

**The geometry** (`plate_rate.py`). The plate motion is that of Beavan et al. (2002)'s Euler pole, Pacific relative to Australia, 61.04° S, 184.19° E, 1.08° per Myr: 39.3 mm/yr towards 245° at the SW edge and 41.6 mm/yr towards 256° at the NE one (Australia moves towards 065–076° relative to the Pacific). A line perpendicular to the plate motion everywhere is a great circle through the pole, so the cross-sections are those great circles, each labelled by its bearing from the pole (−37.79° and −21.47° for the two edges), running NNW–SSE. The strips are the summed slip rates' (`CROSS_SECTIONS`, `gibbs.py --cross-sections`; "Summed slip rates"): 8.5 km wide in bearing, the subsections' mean trace length, 72 between the edges and, with `'all'` (the default), 166 that hold a subsection over all of New Zealand (until November 3rd, 2026, the 612 km between the edges were cut into 8 strips of about 76 km, `--plate-strips`). Each strip's share is the average over the cross-sections in it: each subsection counts in proportion to the fraction of the strip's cross-sections that cross its trace. A fault therefore counts once however many of its subsections a strip holds, and a subsection across a strip's edge counts in both strips in proportion. Each subsection contributes its slip rate times its share of the plate rate per unit slip: the horizontal slip of the block on its Pacific side (towards the pole) relative to the block on its Australian side, from its rake, dip and dip direction (Aki-Richards), projected on the local direction of plate motion and divided by the local plate rate. A strike-slip fault parallel to the motion (the Hope) counts almost fully, the Alpine Fault about 0.95 per unit slip, a thrust its horizontal shortening only. The component across the plate motion, which should add up to 0, scatters by up to ±0.2 between strips because it is sensitive to the rakes and dips, so it is reported but not constrained. The fault model's `SlipRate` is taken as the net slip rate in the fault plane. At 211,000 ruptures 1,008 subsections lie between the edges: the Alpine Fault from George to Jacksons (10 of its 12 subsections) to Tophouse, the Wairau and Wairau 2 (its eastern subsection cut by the edge), the whole Hope Fault, the Marlborough and Kaikōura faults, and, since the great circles continue NNW past Cook Strait, the NW Nelson and offshore Taranaki faults; Wellington–Hutt Valley and Ohariu South lie outside, and none of the 13 subsections that cleaning removes ("Cleaning") lies inside. Over all of New Zealand the strips cross 2,325 crustal subsections; those no rupture can reach count with a model slip rate of 0.

**What the targets say.** Strip by strip from SW to NE, the fault model's slip rates (`fault_sections.geojson`) take up 0.69, 0.70, 0.68, 0.67, 0.77, 0.83, 0.80 and 0.84 of the plate rate: the Alpine Fault alone about 0.65 in the four SW strips, the Hope, Awatere, Clarence and Wairau Faults most of it further NE, and the Needles and Kekerengu Faults in the Cook Strait strip. This logic-tree branch's targets are 0.8 of those slip rates (`NZSHM22_SlipRateFactors`), so they take up 0.53–0.67. A constraint that the faults take up the whole plate rate would therefore ask for 1.5–1.9 times the slip-rate targets. `PLATE_RATE = (0.4, 0.8)` instead said that the faults here take up 0.5–1.0 of the plate rate, of which the ruptures take 80% (this branch's factor), in every strip of every model. On the 8.5-km strips, between the edges the fault model takes up 0.66–0.91 and the targets 0.53–0.73; SW of the edges the fault model reaches 0.90 (the Alpine Fault 0.73 of it alone) and NE of them 0.83, so the targets nowhere take up more than 0.73. The one limit that needs no assumption beyond the inputs' own is an upper one: the faults can take up at most the whole plate rate, so the ruptures at most 0.8 of it (`PLATE_RATE = 0.8`, an upper limit only). A lower limit of 0 is not implied beyond the edges: 533 of the 2,325 crustal subsections slip against the plate motion (among them the Bay of Plenty's normal faults), and in 4 Bay of Plenty strips (around 177.3° E, 37.7° S) the targets' share is below 0, down to −0.10. Between the edges no kept draw comes near 0 (none below 0.39), so there `(0, 0.8)` and `0.8` are the same constraint.

**How it is kept.** Each strip's share is linear in the rupture rates (a subsection's slip rate is the sum over its ruptures of rate × average slip, as in the slip-rate rows), so each strip is one constraint row after the rows in the likelihood, ε × share with ε = 1e-9 and a target of 0, kept within ε × its limits (an upper limit only: −∞ below) by the band's machinery, as the MFD's monotonicity is; its effect on χ² is below 1e-18. Some coefficients are negative, for faults whose slip works against the plate motion, which the machinery allows. `plate_rate.py` builds the rows from the rupture set (`indices.csv`, `average_slips.csv`, `fault_sections.geojson`) and first checks that the cleaned problem's slip-rate rows are the rupture set's subsections, each in proportion to its ruptures' average slips. A start above the upper limit is scaled down to it, each chain's rates multiplied by one factor, which keeps positivity and the MFD's monotonicity (a ratio); a start below a lower limit stops the run. At 211,000 ruptures the best fit's highest share on the 8.5-km strips is 0.799 (strip +41, at the Alpine–Kelly–Hope junction near 171.7° E, 43.0° S), just inside 0.8; on the 8 strips its shares were 0.58–0.73, and so were those of the band's start. The band's exact test includes it (a row of both signs kept between two limits, which forbids one rate's off state and cuts its upper side: P(on) and E[log₁₀ m | on] within 1.4 sd of the exact answer on both backends, against 13–67 sd without it).

**What it changes.** In the conservative run with positivity and the monotonic MFD, the posterior mean's shares are 0.50–0.74 and 79% of the kept draws have every strip inside 0.4–0.8; the others leave strip 5 (the Alpine–Kelly–Hope junction, kept draws 0.60–0.87) and, a few, strip 6 above 0.8 and strip 8 (Cook Strait, 0.43–0.58) below 0.4. In 1,200 sweeps at 211,000 ruptures (4 chains, positivity and the monotonic MFD), without the constraint 22% of strip 5's draws were above 0.8 (5.5% of strip 6's, and 1.2% of strip 8's below 0.4), and none with it; χ² was 480 ± 19 against 480 ± 21, at no extra cost per sweep. On the 8.5-km strips, in the conservative run with positivity, the monotonic MFD, the summed slip rates and the soft band (`BAND_SOFT = 1`, without `PLATE_RATE`), 83.1% of the kept draws have every strip at or below 0.8. The others go above it between the edges only: around the Alpine–Kelly–Hope junction (strips +40 to +45, 171.6–171.9° E; up to 0.83 at the kept draws' 97.5%) and along the Hope and Awatere Faults (strips +50, +51 and +59). Beyond the edges no strip's 97.5% exceeds 0.67. In 160 sweeps at 211,000 ruptures (2 chains, positivity, the monotonic MFD and the summed slip rates), the rows cost 22% more per sweep over all of New Zealand and 9% more between the edges (0.046 and 0.041 s, against 0.038 s). With a limit of 0.7, the start was scaled down by 0.876 and every kept draw stayed at or below 0.7. `plate_rate.txt`, written for every run with a rupture set whether or not `PLATE_RATE` is set, gives the shares' ranges SW of the edges, between them and NE of them: of the fault model, the targets, the best fit, the posterior mean and the kept draws, with the share of draws inside the limits. It also lists the strips that come closest to the whole plate rate, with the faults that take most of their share. `plate_rate_strips.csv` beside it has every strip's numbers.

## Summed slip rates (early November 2026; `SLIP_SUM = True`, on by default from November 3rd to 5th, 2026, off from then, and on again since November 9th with `SLIP_SUM_L0 = 100`; projected on the plate motion since November 3rd, 2026)

**The idea.** In place of a share of the plate rate, whose bounds depend on how much of the plate motion the faults, and of that the ruptures, take up, a comparison of the model with the targets themselves: along every cross-section perpendicular to the plate motion (the plate rate's great circles), the model's slip along the plate motion added up (each subsection's slip vector projected on the local plate motion, as for the plate rate) should match the targets' added up, within the uncertainty of that sum. It is a soft term in the misfit, Gaussian, beside the data, and it can be applied to all of New Zealand (`CROSS_SECTIONS = 'all'`, the default): the targets already carry the information that the faults take up less of the plate motion where a subduction zone takes up more. `CROSS_SECTIONS = 'between'` keeps it, and the plate rate, between the two edges. (Until November 3rd, 2026, `SLIP_SUM` itself was `'all'`, `'between'` or `None`; `CROSS_SECTIONS` now sets the strips for both quantities.)

**Each subsection once.** A cross-section crossing subsection i counts its slip rate with weight 1/e_i, e_i the subsection's extent along the plate motion, so that over all the cross-sections each subsection's weight adds up to exactly 1, and in a strip of cross-sections its weight is the fraction of its extent inside the strip. (The plate rate's weights instead favour faults that run along the plate motion, as a budget across each cross-section should.) Since November 3rd, 2026 each subsection's slip rate counts projected on the plate motion: c_i per unit slip, the horizontal slip of the block on its Pacific side relative to the block on its Australian side (from its rake, dip and dip direction, as for the plate rate), along the local direction of plate motion, so a strip's sum is its faults' slip along the plate motion in mm/yr. The projection is signed. Of the 2,312 subsections at 211,000 ruptures, 449 slip almost along the plate motion (c_i > 0.8), 259 mostly across it (|c_i| < 0.2: normal faults striking along it, thrusts striking across it) and add little, and 527 against it, so they subtract (c_i down to −0.79). The targets' slip rates add up to 4,388 mm/yr as they are and to 3,117 mm/yr along the plate motion. Both sides of the comparison use the same weights and projections on the same subsections. (Until then the slip rates were added as they were, in mm/yr.) At 211,000 ruptures every one of the 2,312 subsections in the slip-rate rows has weights adding up to 1 to rounding. A subsection across a strip edge (or, with `'between'`, across an edge of the region) is split by fraction; 81 traces that double back along the plate motion are still counted once in all, since each subsection's weights are fractions of its whole extent; 3 traces that lie along a great circle go whole into their midpoint's strip; parallel strands (George seaward and landward, Resolution–Charles and Nancy, the Hanmer strands) are separate subsections with their own targets, so a cross-section through them gets the full rate. The 13 subsections no rupture can reach are left out of both sums, since no model can predict slip on them. The model's slip rates come from the rupture set's average slips, checked against the slip-rate rows as for the plate rate, and each subsection's target and uncertainty σ_i are the cleaned problem's own (twice the CSV standard deviation in the NZSHM22 inputs, the rate itself where that is 0).

**The uncertainty of a sum.** Copies of one fault section's slip rate are one measurement, so within a strip they are fully correlated, and sections are independent: σ_k² = Σ over sections p of (Σ over i in p of w_ki c_i σ_i)². A strip whose subsections all slip across the plate motion would have σ_k = 0 and be left out; at 211,000 ruptures none is. The sections are the fault model's parent sections (557; within every one, all subsections share one rate and one uncertainty), not the 'auto' sections of cleaning, 37 of which merge two parent sections with equal targets that share ruptures.

**Its form and weight.** Rows that each strip's sum alone checked, whitened with the strips' full covariance, would count more combinations of the slip residuals, and so weigh more, the more strips there were (at 211,000 ruptures, from 25 at 11 strips to 130 at 83 for the posterior's draws), up to the whole slip-rate χ² a second time. The term is instead the integral over the cross-sections, (1 / L₀) ∫ ((model sum − target sum) / σ(s))² ds, whose strips only approximate it: each strip contributes (width / L₀) × ((model sum − target sum) / σ_k)², without whitening between strips (within each, σ_k carries the correlations), and as the strips narrow the term converges (at 211,000 ruptures for the posterior's draws, 52, 36, 28 and 25 at 134, 67, 34 and 17 km with L₀ = 67 km, against about 21 in the limit). The strips are the subsections' mean trace length wide, 8.5 km (72 between the edges, the same width over the rest of New Zealand: 166 strips), where the term is within about 5% of its limit. L₀ sets its weight (`SLIP_SUM_L0`, `gibbs.py --slip-sum-l0`). The default since November 3rd, 2026 is 8.5 km, the strips' own width, so each strip's sum counts as one extra observation: 72 between the edges and 166 over all of New Zealand. For comparison, 120 km is the unprojected target sums' own decorrelation length along the plate motion between the edges (the integral of their correlation, a mean of 120 km and a median of 108 km; 80 and 53 km over all of New Zealand, where the faults are shorter), which follows from the fault traces and the slip-rate uncertainties alone, not from any model or from L₀; it is close to the 612 km between the edges divided by the 5 sections of the Alpine and Wairau Faults there (George to Jacksons, Jacksons to Kaniere, Kaniere to Springs Junction, Springs Junction to Tophouse, Wairau; Wairau 2, cut by the NE edge, counted out), 122 km, because there those five sections dominate the sums' uncertainty. At 120 km the term would count the regional sums' information once more (5.1 measurements between the edges); at 8.5 km it counts it about 14 times, since neighbouring strips' sums share sections. Its weight remains a choice: the term reuses the slip-rate targets, which the likelihood already holds, so it is a regional balance term rather than new information. A run records it apart from χ², whose fit check stays over the data, with the value it would have near the truth (each strip's weight, added up).

**What it changes, projected** (since November 3rd, 2026; 211,000 ruptures). On the kept draws of the conservative run with positivity and the monotonic MFD but without the term, the projected term is 198 (5–95%: 145–254): 104 SW of the edges, 28 between them and 65 NE of them. The furthest strips are offshore Fiordland (165.5–166.1° E, 45.5–45.8° S, z −2.1 to −3.6), where the model's slip along the plate motion falls short of the targets'. The run with the unprojected term brings it to 102 (60, 12 and 28). In 320 sweeps (2 chains, positivity and the monotonic MFD) the projected term fell from 85 at the best fit to 51 on average (5–95%: 42–64), at a χ² of 477 ± 16.

**What it changed, unprojected** (until November 3rd, 2026; 211,000 ruptures, the conservative run with positivity and the monotonic MFD, and 1,200-sweep runs with 4 chains). The posterior's strip sums already have a spread of only 0.3–0.4 of σ_k (measured on 67-km strips): the data pin them more tightly than the summed targets' uncertainty. Without the term the kept draws give it 12.6 (5–95%: 9.8–16.0) against 11.8, most of it NE of the Cook Strait edge (7.8), where the posterior falls short of the target sums (strips around 177.3° E, 37.8° S, z ≈ −3 to −4); between the edges it is 2.1, so there the posterior already matches them. With the term (at L₀ = 122 km, 2% lighter than the default), in the short runs, it fell from 12.6 to 10.7, the NE strips' mean z from −1.20 to −1.13 and the furthest strip's from −4.03 to −3.87, at a χ² of 482 ± 21 against 480 ± 21: a gentle pull, as a weight of five measurements between the edges implies. For comparison, an L₀ of 1.3 km, the mean spacing along the plate motion at which some fault section starts or ends, made the term about 90 times heavier and raised χ² to 519 ± 20. **At the default L₀ of 8.5 km** (the conservative preset with positivity and the monotonic MFD, two replicates, both passing the convergence checks; against the same run without the term), the term over the kept draws fell from about 178 to 54 (5–95%: 42–70; near the truth it would be about 166, but a model fitted to the targets sits closer to them than the truth would), 9 SW of the edges, 12 between them and 32 NE of them. χ² rose from 480 ± 20 to 492 ± 20, the slip-rate sections improving (339 → 335) while the MFD bins (16 → 27, z +1.5) and the paleo rows (125 → 131) worsened: the term raises the slip where the posterior fell short of the target sums, mostly in the North Island, and the extra events push the bins up. `plate_rate.txt` reports the term for every run with a rupture set, SW of the edges, between them and NE of them, for the best fit, the posterior mean and the kept draws, with the strips furthest from their target sums. The balance term's self-test includes its bookkeeping (χ², the balance term and this term recorded apart, on both backends).

## A symmetric log-ratio term (October 2026; tested on the best fit only; a fixed-width version is now the soft band, `BAND_SOFT`)

The question: a third term in the misfit on each prediction's ratio to its target, λ Σ (ln (Gx)_i − ln d_i)², so that a prediction twice its target costs as much as one half of it (each slip-rate section's k copies weighted 1/k, so the section counts once). Unlike the balance rows it is not quadratic in the rates, and that costs tractability. The sampler's per-rupture conditionals would stop being Gaussian, so its exact draws would give way to Metropolis or slice steps. The best fit would need a nonlinear solver instead of NNLS. And the log of a zero prediction is infinite, so under spike-and-slab every row would need at least one rupture on. (The squared relative residual, (Gx/d − 1)², is quadratic, but it only changes each row's weight, from 1/σ² to 1/σ² + w/d². At 211,000 ruptures that weight would fall almost entirely on the slip-rate sections, whose targets are a median 0.80 of their uncertainties, against 2.85 for the paleo rows and 7.9 for the bins.)

It was therefore tried on the best fit first. L-BFGS-B over nonnegative rates, started from the NNLS fit, minimized the Gaussian misfit plus the log term, with and without the balance rows (equal-badness weight), on the 2e4 inputs (one measurement per slip-rate section, bins as given). λ was calibrated four ways:
- **One uncertainty:** a factor-2 miss costs as much as a one-uncertainty miss, λ = 1/(ln 2)² = 2.1.
- **Two uncertainties:** a factor-2 miss costs as much as a two-uncertainty miss, λ = 4/(ln 2)² = 8.3.
- **Matched per measurement:** a factor-2 miss either way costs what doubling costs in the Gaussian term, λ_i = (d_i/σ_i)²/(ln 2)². At 211,000 ruptures the medians are 1.3 for slip-rate sections, 17 for paleo rows and 130 for bins; in the scaled rows the weight is simply (d_i/ln 2)².
- **Equal totals:** doubling every prediction costs the same in both terms, λ = mean (d/σ)²/(ln 2)²: 57 at 2e4, 13.5 at 211,000 ruptures.

χ² is over the data rows, and slip-rate sections are counted by their mean prediction, as in the table above. "Near 0" means a section's mean prediction is below 1% of its target.

- **Its one clear effect.** At every λ the log term lifts the slip-rate sections that the best fit leaves near zero (3 of the 68), for χ² +6 to +9 at λ = 2.1 or matched weights.
- **It balances slowly, and by raising the total.** At λ = 8.3 the overpredicted sections go from 18 to 20 of 68 and the overpredicted paleo rows from 6 to 10 of 40. Meanwhile the total rate rises 7% and one bin reaches 1.30 times its target. At λ = 57 the counts are 22 and 12, with the total 20% higher, bins up to 1.84 times their targets, and χ² 154 against the fit check's 126 ± 16. The tension between the bins and the slip and paleo rows ("Balance between over- and underprediction") remains: meeting the ratio penalty for slip and paleo takes more rate than the bins allow.
- **The balance rows do more.** NNLS with the balance rows alone takes the overpredicted sections from 18 to 25 and the paleo rows from 6 to 16, for χ² +1.4, with the bins' median unchanged. Adding the log term on top takes balance away again (12 paleo rows over at λ = 8.3) while raising the bins.
- **Not tested:** the 211,000-rupture inputs and the posterior. On this evidence the balance term, then on by default, is the better lever. A sampler version of the log term, with its new updates and forced "on" ruptures, is not worth building yet. The fits came from scratch scripts, not part of the code set.

## What this means for the investigation

Use spike-and-slab as the production setting; at 211,000 ruptures the reference is the very conservative run of the configuration adopted in late October 2026 ("The reference run at 211,000 ruptures"). Report sums (subsection, bin and paleo-site totals, and functionals built from them) and per-rupture exceedance probabilities at about 1e-5 and above as data products, using linear means or per-draw functionals rather than geometric means. Report rupture medians and lower quantiles as prior-dependent; under spike-and-slab and θ most are 0. At 211,000 ruptures the subsections' event rates are partly the prior's too: the square-root slab, no longer run, put them at a median 0.62 of the default's with the same fit. Treat each run's χ² distribution as a fit check against the expected value cleaning prints: 126 ± 16 on the corrected inputs with one measurement per slip-rate section, 590 ± 34 at 211,000 ruptures. Whether another downstream functional aᵀm depends on the lower end of the rates can be checked by computing it at two slab floors ("Floors and cutoff").

Choices that remain modelling decisions:
- how the slip-rate rows count (one measurement per section, the default; each subsection its own measurement with `SLIP_SECTIONS = 'none'`; or sections from a mapping file);
- the MFD bins' uncertainty level: the inputs' design value (the default), counted as the two measurements the bins are projected from (three times it, the bin-to-bin shape kept), or a level set for the M7.0–7.1 bin (`MFD_UNCERTAINTY`); the data cannot set it; it moves the bins and the total rate by several percent, and looser levels let single draws leave whole bins empty ("MFD uncertainty");
- positivity or the band (`BAND`: `'positive'` by default, or a factor such as 10), the MFD's monotonicity (`gibbs.py --mfd-monotonic`, off; a notebook setting until November 4th, 2026), the faults' share of the plate rate in each strip (`gibbs.py --plate-rate`, off; at most 0.8 if imposed: the whole plate rate, at the ruptures' 80% of the fault model's slip rates), the summed slip rates along cross-sections perpendicular to the plate motion and their weight L₀ (`SLIP_SUM`, on by default, over all of New Zealand with `CROSS_SECTIONS = 'all'`; `SLIP_SUM_L0`, 8.5 km by default, one extra observation per strip, against 120 km for the target sums' decorrelation length between the edges), and the balance term (`DOWN_THE_MIDDLE`, off by default since the band) and its weight, which decide how the tension between the bins and the slip and paleo rows is shared;
- the prior: spike-and-slab with the log-uniform slab (whose shape, at 211,000 ruptures, moves the subsections' event rates), with its Beta(1, 1) prior on each bin's fraction on (sharing the fractions by tectonic domain or slip-rate section, pooled or not, changed no fit), its slab floor (1e-10; the products held for slab floors from 1e-12 to 1e-5) and the soft top at each bin's target;
- pressure-free priors versus priors that push the bin totals up (the earlier box priors and the optional halfway prior, and far more the optional linear prior), which give much denser solutions, with exceedance probabilities at 1e-5 about 7 times higher; only the pressure-free priors pass the fit check with one measurement per section.

Prior-free extremal bounds for a few key functionals (a linear program with per-row misfit bounds) would complement the posterior and remain an option.

It has three presets (`PRESET`): `'quick'`, `'conservative'` for production, the default, and `'very_conservative'`.

**Row groups.** The likelihood treats all rows alike; the row groups matter for the priors, which work per magnitude bin (the magnitude-bin rows), and for the subsection summaries (the slip-rate rows). Both are detected automatically during cleaning, so `BLOCKS = None` (the default) needs nothing named. The slip-rate rows are the leading rows, up to the first zero-forcing penalty row (right-hand side 0, entries only on penalised ruptures), so a slip-rate row with zero slip does not end them. The magnitude-bin rows are the rows with one constant coefficient whose supports split the ruptures into disjoint groups. Any group named in `BLOCKS` (by original row numbers, which change with the input) is checked against the detection, and cleaning stops on a mismatch. The paleo rows only label the per-group fit summaries; unnamed, they get an automatic label. Within the slip-rate rows, cleaning then finds the sections (runs of copies of one slip rate) and prints them, with notes on targets shared between sections and on neighbours with identical targets that share no rupture, and the fit check; on new inputs, compare the sections with the fault model.

**Folders.** Code, data and outputs can live in separate folders. In the notebook, `DATA_DIR` holds the input files (only read) and `RUN_DIR` receives everything the workflow writes; by default `DATA_DIR` is `~/inversions/NZSHM22_Matrices-211k/` and `RUN_DIR` a new subfolder of it named after the settings and the start time (`SLIP_SECTIONSauto_MFD_UNCERTAINTYas-given_DOWN_THE_MIDDLE_False_preset_conservative_floor-10_positive_mfdmonotonic_rise0.89_slipsumall_L08.5_<unix time>`), and relative paths are taken from the notebook's folder, whose `tools/` holds the scripts. The configuration cell stops with a clear message if a script or data file is missing. Within `RUN_DIR`, the cleaned problem and diagnostics stay at the top, the best fit's outputs go into `nnls/`, and the runs go into `spikeslab/`, each with a copy of `workflow_config.json`. OpenSHA's build information and the simulated annealing's progress, which its own solutions also carry, describe how OpenSHA made them and are not written. Before writing, it checks that the rates and the rupture set describe the same ruptures, and stops otherwise: the same number, and, for every rupture cleaning kept, the same fault sections (`indices.csv` against the rupture's slip-rate rows, row r being section r) and a magnitude (`properties.csv`) inside its bin; all 18,243 kept ruptures pass both at 2e4, and all 204,538 at 211,000 ruptures. The same script writes any other rate vector's rates, a representative's say (`--column rep_07`). Each run writes `<out>.npz`, holding traces, predictions and (with draws stored) per-rate mean, sd, quantiles and R̂; `<out>_samples.npy`, all draws as float32 with shape (chains, draws, n), unless `--no-samples` is given; and `<out>_stream.npz`, running summaries kept during the run, written either way. The running summaries hold, per rupture: per-chain, per-half sums of the clipped log rate and its square (split R̂, exactly), per-chain batch means (effective draws), counts above 1e-5 and 1e-4, sums of the rate and of its part below the detection scale, and a log-rate histogram in 0.01-decade bins (`--hist-width`; quantiles and modes), saved compressed; and, per draw, the subsection and bin totals. They match the draw-based values exactly, and the quantiles to within the bin width (typically 0.001–0.004 decades). From them `make_tables.py` (which the notebook runs) always writes rate vectors (`rate_vectors.csv` and `.npz`, in each prior's folder; for runs made before running summaries existed, from their stored draws): one row per rupture of the original problem, indexed as the columns of `cru_A`, with penalised ruptures flagged and set to 0, giving the posterior mean, median, mode, and the 2.275%, 15.865%, 84.135% and 97.725% values. The mode is defined on a log scale: 0 if a rate below 1e-16 has probability at least 0.5, otherwise the peak of the smoothed log-rate density above 1e-16; for weakly constrained ruptures it is poorly defined, since that density is flat over the prior's range, and under spike-and-slab and θ most modes are 0. The notebook runs without draws (`--no-samples`): the convergence checks and tables come from the running summaries. Representative draws for hazard (`REPRESENTATIVES`, 100 by default; 0 turns them off): each run keeps 1,000 whole draws, with or without `--no-samples` (`gibbs.py --keep-draws`, recording only: `<out>_kept.npy`, float32, one row per draw, and `<out>_kept_index.npy`, each draw's chain and record number), and `select_draws.py` picks weighted representatives from the replicates' pooled kept draws. It describes each draw by its participation rate per subsection and magnitude bin, clusters the logarithms of those rates (k-means on their principal components), takes the kept draw nearest each cluster centre, and calibrates the weights so the weighted mean participation rates come close to the exact posterior means. The representatives are columns `rep_01`, `rep_02`, ... of `rate_vectors.csv`; `representatives.csv` gives each one's weight, cluster share, run, chain, record, χ² and total rate; `rate_vectors.npz` gains the pooled kept draws (`kept`, `kept_index`), `rep` and `rep_weight`; and `select_report.txt` compares 10, 20 and 40 representatives with the full posterior (means of the total, bin and subsection totals against the exact means; 16%, 50% and 84% points against every recorded draw; each representative's χ² and total rate against the posterior's 1–99% range; the weights). The weights are quadrature weights for the posterior as a whole, and the `mean` column remains the exact source for mean hazard, which is linear in the rates. The fit figures and the fault-by-fault figures draw the kept draws one by one, as x's (since early November 2026): each draw's section means, paleo predictions, bin totals, fault means, counts of ruptures on by rate, and fault rates by magnitude bin, faint, so that where the draws gather shows darkest, with the posterior mean as a dot in the mark's colour; a single model, such as the NZSHM22 solution, compares with a single draw, not with the mean. In the 211,000-rupture reference run the target lies inside that range for 12 of the 18 bins; the six bins from M8.0 sit above theirs. Several rate vectors give panels side by side. A rupture's magnitude is known only to its bin: the bin rows are counted up from 6.80–6.90 in steps of 0.1 in their original order (`--lowest-bin`, `--bin-width`), an order the script checks against rupture sizes (on the corrected inputs the median number of subsections per rupture rises from 3 in the lowest bin to 113 in the highest, rank correlation +1.00). `plot_slip_rates.py`, run by the notebook after it, plots each slip-rate section's target against the mean model's prediction (G times the rate vectors' `mean` column, which is also the posterior mean of the prediction), on a linear and on a log scale (`fig_slip_rates.png`, with a section table and a row table in `fig_slip_rates.txt`). ## Open items

- **Spike-and-slab's long run:** at 211,000 ruptures the very conservative run of the configuration adopted in late October 2026 is done, and passes with eight slowly mixing ruptures (R̂ at most 1.022, 1.9% of the total rate; "The reference run at 211,000 ruptures"). On the corrected 2e4 inputs the very conservative run under the defaults is still to do. With `'two-measurements'` it has run, in 1 h 20 min on the M2, and passed in all three replicates: 0.2% of its ruptures (35–42) had R̂ above the noise baseline, the worst 1.009–1.013, and the replicates agreed rupture by rupture as expected (sd 0.991–0.999). The very conservative θ run (counting each subsection) also met the convergence targets (χ²'s R̂ on the 1.005 line in one replicate), but 4.5–4.7% of its ruptures had R̂ above the noise baseline, the worst 1.35–1.38, with errors that hold across replicates: a minority of ruptures that switch on and off stays slow there. Moves that exchange the active role between alternative ruptures remain open; a lower swap threshold would give seven of the 211,000-rupture reference run's eight slow ruptures a partner.
- **The fit shortfall at 211,000 ruptures:** the paleo rows are fitted at 2.2 per measurement, and the mean model sits about 1 uncertainty below the paleo targets and 0.4–0.9 below the slip-rate targets, under every prior variant tried ("Fits and the prior's lower end"). It comes from the bins counting events while the posterior takes each bin's average magnitude, and from the paleo rows' tension with the rest of the data. Whether to weight the paleo rows more against the bins (their uncertainties, in the inputs) is a modelling decision.
- **Subsection event rates at 211,000 ruptures:** they depend on the slab's shape (the square-root slab's a median 0.62 of the default's, with the same fit), so they are partly the prior's. The square-root slab was dropped as the cross-check at the end of October 2026, so this stays a caveat on those rates rather than a check run for each result set.
- **The band:** its factor (10) is a modelling choice the data do not make. It is stricter than the data wherever a target is under about one uncertainty, so under it χ² falls below the fit check (−4.4 sd at 211,000 ruptures), which then no longer tests the uncertainties; a fit check under the band would need its expected χ² from simulated data. The quick-preset reference values under the band (TESTING.md), a very conservative run on the 2e4 inputs, and the band at another factor (3, say) are still to do.
- **Dispersed starts:** chains started from simulated-annealing solutions or perturbed best fits (`gibbs.py --init`, from the command line), still untested (see TESTING.md). The 211,000-rupture reference run's 48 chains all started from the best fit, so their agreement cannot rule out good fits that this start never reaches.
- **Representative draws:** in the first test (conservative preset, spike-and-slab, 2 × 1,000 kept draws, counting each subsection), 20 representatives matched the posterior mean of the total rate (to 0.0%) and stayed inside the posterior's χ² and total-rate ranges with no zero weight, but the worst bin total was 4.6% off its exact mean, 76% of subsection means were within 10%, and 68–79% of the subsections' 16%, 50% and 84% points were within 15%, short of the proposed 2%, 90% and 90%. Forty representatives did little better (2.4%, 79%, 74–81%), and the quick preset gave much the same, so run length is not the limit. About a third of the subsections have totals whose coefficient of variation exceeds 0.45 (median 0.30), more than 20 draws can average to within 10%. With one measurement per slip-rate section (quick preset), 20 representatives matched 61% of subsection means within 10% under spike-and-slab and 53% under θ (40: 66% and 63%). With the MFD bins counted as two measurements their posterior spread triples, to about 30% per bin, and 20 representatives matched 58% of subsection means within 10% and the worst bin to 16% (quick preset, spike-and-slab). Revisit these tolerances with the hazard team, or calibrate the weights on the subsection and bin totals directly. At 211,000 ruptures, from the very conservative reference run, 100 representatives still fall short on the subsections (68–75% of their 16%, 50% and 84% points within 15%), one weight calibrates to 0, and the check that every representative lies inside the posterior's 1–99% ranges fails by chance most of the time for that many ("The reference run at 211,000 ruptures").
- **Evidence:** done for single-box floors, where it keeps rising as the floor drops; not done for spike-and-slab or θ (if wanted, from a few tempered runs by hand).
- **Hope: Conway's slip-rate uncertainty** (left as given, October 2026): the fault model gives 15.8 ± 0.2 mm/yr, and the inputs 12.64 mm/yr (every section's rate scaled by 0.8, its standard deviation not) with twice the standard deviation as the uncertainty, as for every section: a target of 31.6 uncertainties (rows 151–157 at 2e4, 719–725 at 211k), where the next tightest section, Clarence: Southwest, has 6.7 and the median is 1.6 (0.8 at 211k). Every solution holds the section at its target (the best fit, the posterior and simulated annealing at 12.65–12.74 mm/yr). A best fit with the uncertainty widened tenfold puts it at about 15.7 mm/yr, raises the paleo site on it (row 860, and 8916 at 211k) from 0.45 to about 0.6 of its target, and moves nothing else noticeably (bin totals under 0.5%, the total rate 0.05%, no other section by more than 0.16 of its uncertainty). Worth checking upstream whether 0.2 mm/yr is intended.
- **Sparse sections:** check the sections whose totals collapse against the fault model; the slip-rate figure is a first look. With one measurement per section, in quick runs, spike-and-slab and θ predict 18 sections (72 subsections) at under a quarter of their targets, all with targets only 0.6–2.4 uncertainties above zero, so each section's mean prediction stays within 2.3 uncertainties of its target and the data do not rule out near-zero slip there (counting each subsection, 41–43 subsections, within 1.42). Their χ² now sits 1 sd above the fit check, so the earlier worry that it fell well below, pointing at generous uncertainties, is gone. Paleo site row 853 is the same story at a site: the best fit nearly matches it, but both priors predict about 0 there in most draws (`fig_paleo_rates.png`).
- **Slip-rate sections:** detected as runs of copies (68 on the corrected inputs). A mapping of subsections to sections from the fault model (`SLIP_SECTIONS = 'file.txt'`) would settle the 32 sections that share their target with another and the 6 neighbouring pairs with identical targets that share no rupture, and should be used for new inputs if the detection looks doubtful.
- **Geodetic slip rates:** rows that vary from subsection to subsection are left as given, each its own measurement. If a geodetic model's errors are correlated along a fault (they come from a smooth fit), each row counts as more independent information than it carries; that would need a covariance, or an effective number of measurements per fault.
- **Uniform slip along a section:** the fit check assumes each section's true slip rate is uniform along it; real variation along a section adds to the expected χ², and the weighting tolerates it at √k times the measurement's uncertainty per subsection. Whether that tolerance suits the fault model is worth a look.
- **Scaling to 5e5:** filtered swap moves, with timing tests at about 1e5 ruptures first. Cleaning already works on the sparse G throughout (53 s at 211,000 ruptures; TESTING.md, "Before 5e5"), running summaries already allow runs without stored draws (`--no-samples`, as the notebook runs), and the sampler's per-draw bookkeeping works on each chain's nonzero rates, so it no longer grows with the number of ruptures (TESTING.md, "Run lengths, timing and storage").
- **Extremal bounds:** prior-free bounds for chosen functionals.
- **MFD covariance:** the bins' uncertainty is set by one multiplier (`MFD_UNCERTAINTY`), which keeps them independent. A correlated covariance, the two measurements' within-branch uncertainty projected onto the bins plus a small model error per bin, would let the MFD shift and tilt as a whole while staying smooth. It needs whitened bin rows, dense across the bins, which the per-bin priors and bin targets would have to be adapted to ("MFD uncertainty").
- **Optional refinement:** a similarity prior between overlapping ruptures.
- **HMC and evidence scripts:** `rhmc.py` (log-space HMC, the independent cross-check described above) and `evidence.py` are set aside from the code set, in `cru_retired_scripts.zip`. An HMC cross-check of the current priors would bring `rhmc.py` back; evidence comparisons can be done by hand with a few tempered runs.
- **NUTS:** the cross-check from the design notes was not run, since there is no JAX in the sandbox. Log-space HMC already uses NUTS's transform and agrees with Gibbs.
