# CRU 2e4 configuration: rupture rates under log-uniform priors

Development run in a claude.ai sandbox (1 CPU core, 4 GB RAM), October 2026. Inputs: `cru_A.txt` (G) and `cru_D.txt` (d), with σ = 1 for every row (the rows are already normalized by their uncertainties). Every number below comes from the scripts in this folder and can be regenerated with the commands at the end. Timings are for one core; the M2 runs chains in parallel.

The corrected inputs clean to 446 rows (the effective number of constraints) and 18,243 rupture rates; the earlier inputs, used for much of the analysis below, cleaned to 447 × 18,356. Every χ² is on the cleaned rows.

**How to read the numbers.** The defaults are spike-and-slab, one measurement per slip-rate section, and the MFD bins' uncertainties as the inputs give them. The Summary and "Slip-rate sections" give their results on the corrected inputs; "MFD uncertainty" gives what counting the bins as the two measurements they are projected from would change; the sections on spike-and-slab and θ explain the two priors and the tests behind them. Many of the analyses further down were made earlier, on the earlier inputs, under box priors and counting each slip-rate subsection as its own measurement; their numbers are as measured, and are marked where it matters.

**Default model.**

- **Prior:** spike-and-slab per magnitude bin (`--prior spikeslab`, `gibbs.py`'s default; `PRIOR_KIND = 'spikeslab'` in the notebook). Each rupture is either off, with a rate of exactly 0, or on, with a log-uniform rate from a slab floor at 1e-10 up to a soft top at its bin's target rate R_j, whose width is the bin's own uncertainty. The fraction of ruptures switched on is estimated in each bin under a Beta(1, 1) prior, so the data, not a floor or a depth, decide how many ruptures are active, and the prior puts no pressure on the bin totals.
- **Alternative:** θ = 1 (`--prior binbox-theta:1:0`; `PRIOR_KIND = 'theta'`): per-bin log-uniform boxes below the same top, with depths chosen so that the prior puts no pressure on the bin totals. It reaches the same answer by another route and is the main cross-check.
- **Likelihood:** Gaussian, exp(−χ²/2) with the given σ, for every prior; there is no setting for it. Each run's χ² distribution serves as a fit check. The χ² cap, an optional model class that kept only models with χ² at most a threshold, was removed in October 2026 ("Earlier priors").
- **MFD bins:** their uncertainties as the inputs give them (`preliminaries.py --mfd-uncertainty as-given`; `MFD_UNCERTAINTY = 'as-given'` in the notebook): 10% at M7.0–7.1, a design value, growing as R_j^−0.25 towards the rarer bins, each bin its own measurement. `'two-measurements'` counts the bins as the two measurements they are projected from, the rate of M ≥ 5 earthquakes and the b-value, multiplying every bin's uncertainty by √(bins / 2) = 3; a number sets the M7.0–7.1 bin's relative uncertainty ("MFD uncertainty").
- **Slip-rate rows:** one measurement per fault section. The inputs copy each section's slip rate to its k subsections; cleaning divides each section's rows by √k, so the measurement counts once (`preliminaries.py --slip-sections auto`; `SLIP_SECTIONS = 'auto'` in the notebook). Rows unlike their neighbours keep their weight.
- **Sampler:** Gibbs in log rate, exact, with exact on/off steps under spike-and-slab and exact swap moves between near-duplicate ruptures.

Box priors with a common floor, the per-bin boxes (`binbox-floor:-12`, the default until October 2026) and the single log-boxcar before them, still run in `gibbs.py`, for comparison only: their priors push the bin totals up, and with one measurement per section the per-bin boxes fail the fit check by a wide margin ("Earlier priors"). A linearly uniform prior is not used; a later section summarizes why.

Three sites are about two uncertainties low: rows 860 and 861, which the best fit also leaves at about half their targets, and row 853, which the best fit nearly matches but whose posterior median prediction is about 0, its ruptures off in most draws. θ gives the same.

The data determine sums of rates and the upper part of the rate population, but not the rates of individual weak ruptures:
- **Data-determined:** subsection and bin totals, the number of ruptures above about 1e-5, and each rupture's posterior probability of exceeding 1e-5 or 1e-4. Under spike-and-slab they moved by no more than Monte Carlo noise for slab floors from 1e-12 to 1e-5, and spike-and-slab and θ = 1, two different treatments of the lower end, agree on them (exceedance probabilities within 0.02–0.16 in log₁₀, counting each subsection).
- **Prior-determined:** individual rates below the detection scale (about 3e-5 to 2e-4 for one rupture alone), including medians and lower quantiles. Most ruptures are off in most draws, so their medians and modes are 0, and whether a weak rupture is on reflects the prior on the fractions.
- **The largest prior sensitivity:** priors that push the bin totals up, the earlier box priors, give much denser solutions: about 490 ruptures above 1e-5 per draw instead of about 100, and exceedance probabilities at 1e-5 about 7 times higher (counting each subsection). Only the pressure-free priors pass the fit check.

Gibbs in log rate is exact and needs no tuning. Under spike-and-slab a minority of ruptures switch on and off slowly (0.3% of them in quick runs, carrying 16–18% of the total rate), and the fractions on mix slowly in short runs, so its runs are long; its sweeps are cheap, because off ruptures rarely change any residuals. Log-space HMC agreed with Gibbs rupture by rupture (under the single box), and the self-tests check every prior against exact answers.

## Slip-rate sections: one measurement per section

A fault section's slip rate is one measurement. The inputs copy it, with its uncertainty, to each of the section's k subsections: the 388 slip-rate rows carry only 46 distinct values. Counted as independent, each section's slip rate entered the likelihood k times, as if its uncertainty were √k smaller (2.9 times on average). The default now counts each measurement once: cleaning divides each section's rows by √k (`preliminaries.py --slip-sections auto`; `SLIP_SECTIONS = 'auto'` in the notebook).

**What the weighting does.** For a section with target s and subsection predictions y_1 … y_k, in units of its uncertainty σ,

Σ_j (y_j − s)² / (k σ²) = (ȳ − s)² / σ² + Σ_j (y_j − ȳ)² / (k σ²).

The section's mean prediction ȳ is fitted to the measurement once, with the stated uncertainty, and the subsections' departures from their own mean are penalized as if each subsection's uncertainty were σ√k. Dividing the rows by √k is therefore the same as giving each subsection's copy the uncertainty σ√k: the measurement keeps its information about the section as a whole, the copies stop counting as k independent measurements, and the subsections are still asked, loosely, to share one slip rate.

**Finding the sections.** The inputs carry no section labels, so cleaning looks for runs of neighbouring rows that hold copies: identical targets (as normalized in the inputs) and identical coefficients for every rupture both rows contain. On the corrected inputs:
- 68 sections of 2–21 subsections (median 5), and no single subsections;
- all 320 neighbouring pairs with identical targets that share a rupture also have identical coefficients; of the 35 neighbouring pairs with different targets that share ruptures, 1 would pass the coefficient test, so both tests are needed;
- 6 pairs of neighbours with identical targets share no rupture, so they cannot be confirmed as copies and count as separate sections: the 62 runs of identical values become 68 sections;
- 32 sections share their target with another section (geologic slip rates are often round numbers); a mapping file would settle whether any of them belong together.

Rows unlike their neighbours keep their weight, so a geodetic slip-rate model, whose values vary from subsection to subsection, is used as given, each row its own measurement. On synthetic variants of the inputs, continuously varying targets gave 388 single subsections, as did equal slip rates with differing uncertainties; the geologic inputs give the 68 sections. `--slip-sections FILE` (`SLIP_SECTIONS = 'file.txt'`, in `DATA_DIR`) takes the sections from a file of `original_row section` lines instead, for example from the fault model: a missing or extra slip-rate row stops cleaning, and a section whose rows have different targets is noted. `--slip-sections none` (`SLIP_SECTIONS = 'none'`) keeps every subsection as its own measurement, the inputs as given, as a sensitivity test in its own `RUN_DIR`; it reproduces the earlier results byte for byte (the cleaned problem, the diagnostics, the rate vectors and every table). The cleaned problem records the row scales, each slip-rate row's section, the setting and the fit check (`row_scale`, `section`, `slip_sections`, `chi2_expect`).

**The fit check.** If the uncertainties are right, a model matching the truth has an expected χ² of Σ_s k_s w_s² + n_other, with variance 2 Σ_s (k_s w_s²)² + 2 n_other, where w_s is the weight of section s's rows and n_other the number of other rows: one shared error counted k_s times widens the spread. With w_s = 1/√k_s each section contributes 1 ± √2, giving 68 + 58 = 126 ± 16 on the corrected inputs. With `'none'` it is 446 ± 80 (446 ± 83 earlier, when the 62 runs of identical values were taken as the sections). The expectation assumes each section's slip rate is uniform along it; real variation along a section would add to it. Cleaning prints the fit check, and the notebook compares every run against it.

## MFD uncertainty: how much the bins count

**The question.** The 18 bin rows hold the regional magnitude–frequency distribution (MFD) that the rupture rates must reproduce, M6.8–8.6 in steps of 0.1, each bin a target rate R_j with a relative uncertainty. They are not independent observations. They are a projection of two measurements, the rate of M ≥ 5 earthquakes and the b-value, whose own uncertainties the logic tree explores through alternative inputs. And their uncertainty level is a design value, not a measurement: the inputs give σ_j / R_j = 0.1 (R_j / R_7.0)^−0.25 exactly, 10.0% at M7.0–7.1, from 9.8% at the highest-rate bin (6.9–7.0) to 15.4% at 8.5–8.6. The 0.1 is set where cru_A and cru_D are built, a holdover of the UCERF3 practice of weighting the data types against each other, whereas the slip-rate and paleo rows are each normalized by their measured uncertainties. The power 0.25, also set there, splits the difference between two views: each bin checked against the earthquakes observed in it, whose Poisson counts grow tenfold per unit of magnitude for b = 1 (power 0.5), and every bin taken from the same N(M ≥ 5) and b (power 0). Cleaning keeps that shape and sets only the level.

**What the inputs' level implies.** Counted as 18 independent measurements, the bins pin the MFD as a whole far more tightly than one bin's uncertainty suggests: from the bin rows alone, the bins' total rate is known to ±2.8% and their slope to ±0.024 in b (a weighted least-squares line through the bin centres). The posterior followed: in quick runs its total rate was ±2.5%, its bins sat +0.4% to +7.3% above their targets, and their misfit was almost all the model's freedom (17.1 of 18.1; "Row-block fit" below), so the data could not say whether that width was right.

**The two-measurements rule** (`'two-measurements'`). If each bin's uncertainty is right as that bin's own, the bins together should carry the information of the two measurements they come from, not of 18. Write the bins' log rates as a projection of two parameters (log N, b) with covariance S, and each bin's variance as its share of it, σ_j² = J_j S J_jᵀ. Treated as independent, with every uncertainty multiplied by c, the bins carry information Jᵀ D⁻¹ J / c² about the two parameters, against S⁻¹ for the measurements themselves; matching the two on average (tr(S Jᵀ D⁻¹ J) / c² = 2, the number of measurements) gives c² = B / 2 for B bins, whatever the projection. So cleaning multiplies every bin's uncertainty by √(B / 2), 3 for the 18 bins, dividing its row of G and d by it. It is the composite-likelihood count that divides each slip-rate section's k copies by √k. The bins then have 30% at M7.0–7.1 (29.4% to 46.3% across the bins), and the bin rows alone give ±8.3% on the bins' total rate and ±0.071 on their slope: the inputs' design value now describes the MFD as a whole, about one bin's worth, rather than each of 18 independent bins. The targets R_j are unchanged, and the soft top's width, the bin's relative uncertainty, follows (0.112–0.165 decades, against 0.041–0.062).

**The settings** (`MFD_UNCERTAINTY` in the notebook; `preliminaries.py --mfd-uncertainty`):
- `'as-given'` (the default): the inputs' uncertainties, each bin its own measurement. The cleaned problem's matrix, targets and row scales are exactly those from before the setting existed, and quick runs reproduce the earlier numbers exactly.
- `'two-measurements'`: the rule above.
- a number u: the relative uncertainty (one sd) of the M7.0–7.1 bin, the other bins keeping the inputs' shape. 0.1 reproduces the inputs, and 0.3 is `'two-measurements'` for these 18 bins. The bins are counted up from 6.80–6.90 in steps of 0.1 in their original row order, as for the rates-by-magnitude figure, and that order is checked against rupture sizes; cleaning stops if no bin starts at M7.0.

Cleaning prints the inputs' uncertainties and the power they follow (R_j^−0.250 here, exactly), the multiplier, and what the bin rows then say about the MFD as a whole. The cleaned problem records the setting and the multiplier (`mfd_uncertainty`, `mfd_scale`), and its `row_scale` includes the multiplier. Another setting changes the problem both priors share, so it needs its own `RUN_DIR`; the notebook warns if a `RUN_DIR` holds another prior's runs made on a problem cleaned otherwise.

- **The data pull the bins up once they weigh less.** At the inputs' level the bins sat within a few percent of their targets; counted as two measurements they rise a median 8%, and the total rate 5%, as the best fit's bins do (+7% to +18%, median +14%). The slip rates and paleo rates ask for somewhat more large earthquakes than the target MFD, the mild overprediction also seen in simulated-annealing models, which the inputs' level held to a few percent. The posterior total sits about 0.9 of the bin rows' combined uncertainty above the targets' total, so the two remain consistent.
- **The bins still follow their width.** The data narrow each bin's spread by only about a tenth (posterior sd a median 0.91 of the bin's uncertainty, 0.96 at the inputs' level), and the bins' misfit is still almost all freedom (17.0 of 18.0). The data cannot estimate the level: a hierarchical scale on the bins, or one estimated from their misfit, would mostly return its own prior. The level is a modelling judgment, and the rule applies the inputs' design value to the MFD as a whole.
- **The observational rows fit slightly better.** The paleo rows' χ² falls from 51.6 to 48.7 (the mean model's from 34.4 to 30.5), the slip-rate sections' barely moves (70.6 to 70.3), and the total falls from 140 to 137.
- **The products barely move.** Subsection totals rise a median 4% (5–95%: −2% to +18%, against −7% to +17% between two replicates), and exceedance probabilities move by no more than Monte Carlo noise (log₁₀ P(rate > 1e-5) a median −0.01, 5–95% −0.18 to +0.16, against ±0.27 between replicates). The population is unchanged.
- **Spike-and-slab and θ still agree** on the fit, the total rate, the rate of M ≥ 7.5, the slope and the products (subsection totals ×1.01, 5–95% 0.88 to 1.11; exceedance at 1e-5 a median −0.07 in log₁₀, within noise), but no longer bin by bin at the top: θ puts the 8.2–8.5 bins 22–36% above their targets and the 8.5–8.6 bin 19% below, where spike-and-slab has +5% to +16%. With the bins' bands three times wider, how the rate splits among the few largest bins is up to the prior; their sum is not (M ≥ 8.0: ×1.08 under spike-and-slab, ×1.12 under θ).
- **Mixing improves slightly** under spike-and-slab: 31–40 slow-mixing ruptures (0.2%) carrying 11–13% of the total rate, against 59–63 (16–18%); under θ, 230–241 (1.3%, 21%).
- **Representative draws are coarser for the bins.** With the bins' posterior spread tripled, 20 representatives match the bins' means less closely (the worst bin 16% off at the quick preset, against 6% at the inputs' level) and 58% of subsection means to within 10% (61%).
- **Single draws can leave a whole bin empty.** An empty bin costs one over its relative uncertainty squared in χ²: 42–104 at the inputs' level, but only 5–12 with the bins counted as two measurements, and spike-and-slab can switch off all of a bin's ruptures at once. In a very conservative spike-and-slab run with `'two-measurements'` (16 chains × 100,000 sweeps, three replicates, each passing the convergence checks), some bin fell below 1% of its target in 4.19%, 4.21% and 4.29% of the replicates' draws, and a bin sat at exactly 0 in 1.4% of all draws. The bins at M8.2–8.5 emptied most often (0.5–0.7% of draws each; their 1st percentiles at 4.5–11% of their targets), the others in 0.03–0.34% of draws. The replicates agree, so this is the posterior, not slow mixing; as given, none of the 16,000 draws of a conservative spike-and-slab run (8 chains × 20,000 sweeps, two replicates) took a bin below 0.53 of its target. Bin medians and means hardly notice, but anything built from single draws does, the representatives among them, and so does a straight line fitted to a draw's bin totals in log space: summarize such a slope by its median and quantiles, as the table does, not by its mean and sd. A looser level would empty bins more often: at `MFD_UNCERTAINTY = 1` (100% at M7.0–7.1) an empty bin costs at most about 1 in χ² (not yet measured).

**The fit check** is unchanged, 126 ± 16, still one per bin: in a draw the bins' totals scatter independently, each within its band, so they contribute about 18 whatever their width (18.0 here). Counting the bins as two measurements is about how much they weigh in the likelihood, not about how many residuals they leave; that is unlike a slip-rate section, whose copies leave nearly one residual between them. A band much wider than the bins' true error would show as a bins' χ² well below 18, but with the data this weak on the bins it shows little either way.

**Within a logic-tree branch.** At the inputs' level each branch's MFD is held to ±2.8% on its total rate and ±0.024 in b by the bin rows alone (±2.5% on the total in the posterior), so the spread of the MFD comes almost entirely from the tree's N and b branches, which is what the default assumes. `'two-measurements'` gives each branch a spread of its own, ±8% on the total rate and ±0.07 in b (±6.7% on the total in the posterior), which adds in quadrature to the tree's.

**A fuller treatment** would give the bins a correlated covariance: the two measurements' within-branch uncertainty projected onto the bins (rank two), plus a small model error per bin, so that the MFD could shift and tilt as a whole while staying smooth, which no single multiplier can do. Whitened, the bin rows would become dense combinations of the bins, whereas the per-bin priors and the bin targets now rely on each row being a plain sum over its bin; it is listed under open items.

**History: block reweighting** (`BLOCK_WEIGHTS`, removed in October 2026). Before this setting, a pilot run could reweight the three row blocks to equal misfits per measurement or to equal totals. It counted the bins as 18 measurements and could hardly move them, since their misfit is the model's freedom whatever their width: `'equal-per-measurement'` changed the uncertainties by 3–7% and the products by no more than Monte Carlo noise, and `'equal-total'` tightened the bins (×0.62) and pulled them onto their targets. The MFD setting replaced it: of the three blocks' uncertainties, the bins' is the one that is a design value rather than a measurement, so it is set directly.

## Spike-and-slab (default)

Each rupture is either off, with a rate of exactly 0, or on, with a rate from a slab: log-uniform from a slab floor (1e-10 by default, the notebook's `FLOOR`) up to a soft top at its bin's target rate R_j. The fraction of ruptures switched on is estimated separately in each bin, under a Beta(1, 1) prior (`--prior spikeslab:A:TOP:a:b`; `spikeslab` alone gives A = −10, a soft top and Beta(1, 1)).

This separates two jobs that a floor does for a log-uniform box. "Off" is an exact zero, so no floor is needed to define it, and the slab's lower end only has to lie below the scale at which all ruptures together become invisible to the data (about 2e-9 on the corrected inputs; "Floors and cutoff"). How many ruptures are active becomes an explicit quantity that the data estimate in each bin, rather than a side effect of a floor or a depth. The fractions are per bin because rupture counts vary 23-fold across bins (164 to 3,708): a single fraction would hand the crowded bins proportionally more active ruptures, which is the source of the box priors' pressure on the bin totals ("θ = 1").

**The targets and the top.** Each bin row is its constraint divided by the bin's uncertainty: coefficient 1/σ_j for every rupture in the bin, right-hand side R_j/σ_j. So R_j is d_j divided by the row's coefficient, whatever the uncertainty function, and d_j itself is one over the relative uncertainty. On the corrected inputs the targets run from 3.4e-4 to 2.1e-3 per year and sum to 0.0214. The inputs give them relative uncertainties of 9.8–15.4% (10% at M7.0–7.1), which the default keeps; `'two-measurements'` multiplies them by 3, to 29–46% ("MFD uncertainty"), which changes the bins' weight in χ², but not the targets. No rupture can carry more than its bin's true total, which the bin row gives as R_j with its uncertainty σ_j, so the slab (like θ's boxes) ends at R_j with a Gaussian shoulder in log rate of sd log₁₀(1 + σ_j/R_j), 0.041–0.062 decades under the default (0.112–0.165 with the bins counted as two measurements). Unlike a hard top at R_j, it does not truncate the plausible case of a bin whose true total is somewhat above R_j and dominated by one rupture; unlike a hard top at a multiple of R_j, it needs no arbitrary factor; and it follows the bins' uncertainties automatically if they are revised. `TOP` may instead be `hard` (at R_j) or a number F (hard, at F × R_j). Using the targets to place the top is a mild double use of the data, and dominant ruptures do press against it (in about 80% of draws, by up to about three of the bin's uncertainties, counting each subsection), which is why it is soft. An exact test (`selftest_edges`, part of `--selftest`) checks two rates with different shoulder widths, pushed above their tops by the data, against a grid integral on both backends (all |z| ≤ 1.1).

**Sampling.** Because a log-uniform prior is scale-invariant, writing m_i = R_j u_i turns every bin's range into the same one for u, below 1, with G's columns multiplied by their bin's R_j; the sampler runs on u and records rates. The update is exact and needs no numerical integrals. An off rupture keeps a slab value drawn from the slab itself (a pseudo-prior), and whether a rupture is on is a Gibbs step with probability π·L(m) / [π·L(m) + (1 − π)·L(0)], computed from quantities the update already has. An on rupture's rate is updated by the exact slice sampler in log rate. The fractions are redrawn exactly from their Beta conditionals after every sweep (`--hyper-every 1`, the default for this prior), and swap moves still apply between near-duplicates in the same bin, so they can exchange the active role. An exact test (`selftest_spikeslab`, part of `--selftest`) enumerates the on/off states of two rates in one bin, with the fraction integrated out in closed form, against grid integrals on both backends (all |z| ≤ 2.1). Its sweeps are several times cheaper than θ's: off ruptures rarely change any residuals, and swaps between two off ruptures are skipped.

## θ = 1: pressure-free per-bin boxes (alternative)

**Why box priors push.** With its top at R_j, a log-uniform box W_j decades deep makes the prior expect θ_j = n_j / (W_j ln 10) of the bin's ruptures per unit of ln(rate). For many ruptures, the prior distribution of the bin's total is then a generalized Dickman distribution: in units of R_j, its density below the top is proportional to s^(θ−1). Its slope at the target is θ_j − 1, so the prior shifts the bin total by roughly σ_j²(θ_j − 1), where σ_j is the bin row's relative uncertainty. Shallow boxes (θ_j > 1) push up, very deep ones (θ_j < 1) push down, and θ_j = 1 is flat below R_j. The density then falls gently above R_j, a resistance to overshoot of only 1–2%. Box priors with a common floor have θ_j of about 4 to 200, and runs of the single box (floors 1e-10 and 1e-12) and of per-bin boxes (10 and 12 decades deep) bore this out (earlier inputs, counting each subsection): the predicted pull (a median +26% to +39%) ranked the bins as the observed one did (+16% to +22%; rank correlation 0.83–0.87). The observed shift is about 60% of the prior-only prediction, because the slip-rate and paleo rows pull the totals back. What matters is θ, not where the top sits.

**Pressure-free depths.** W_j = n_j / ln 10 decades gives θ_j = 1: from 71 decades (the 164-rupture bin) to 1,610 (the 3,708-rupture bin) on the corrected inputs. A simulation for the median bin confirms the flat density below R_j (0.96–1.04 in relative terms) and the fall above it (0.93, 0.74, 0.62 at 1.1, 1.3 and 1.5 R_j). Within a factor of 2 of these depths (θ between 0.5 and 2) the pull stays near 1%. Rates of 10^-1,600 are no problem in log space; they are exactly zero in the predictions.

**Implementation.** `--prior binbox-theta:θ:SE[:TOP]` sets each bin's depth to n_j / (θ ln 10) decades below the same top as spike-and-slab's (`PRIOR_KIND = 'theta'`, `THETA = 1`). The C kernel and the NumPy fallback accept a lower edge per rupture. An exact test (`selftest_zlo`, part of `--selftest`) checks two rates with different lower edges against a grid integral, with soft and hard edges, on both backends (all |z| ≤ 2.3). Swaps are restricted to same-bin pairs. Transfer moves are not available.

## Floors and cutoff

One setting, `FLOOR` (1e-10), marks the rate below which nothing matters. It is spike-and-slab's slab floor, the lower end of its on range (`spikeslab:{FLOOR}:{BOX_TOP}` in the notebook; −10 is also `gibbs.py`'s default), and the cutoff below which rates count as equal in the convergence checks, the figures and the preliminaries' collective-scale check (the notebook passes `--diag-floor={FLOOR}` to `gibbs.py`, `preliminaries.py`, `summarize_runs.py` and `plot_rate_magnitude.py`, whose defaults are −10). As a cutoff it never changes the sampling; under θ, whose depths come from θ, it is only the cutoff. The per-bin boxes, if run, take it as their floor (−12 reproduces their earlier default).

Rates below 1e-10 are negligible:
- **Per rupture:** under a Poisson model, a rupture at 1e-10 per year has a 95% chance of never occurring in 500 million years, about the age of New Zealand's oldest surface rocks (the chance of at least one occurrence is 1 − e^(−0.05) = 4.9%).
- **Age of the Earth:** at 1e-10 per year the mean recurrence interval, 10 billion years, is more than twice the age of the Earth.
- **Collectively:** with every rupture at the same rate, no prediction moves by more than 0.1σ below 1.8e-9 for the corrected 2e4 inputs with one measurement per slip-rate section (6.9e-10 counting each subsection), so all 18,243 ruptures together at 1e-10 would move no prediction by more than about 0.006σ (0.015σ counting each subsection). Under spike-and-slab only a few hundred ruptures are on at a time, so the margin is wider still. The scale falls as more ruptures share the busiest subsection (here original row 156, crossed by 7,234 ruptures), so `preliminaries.py` reports it for each problem and says whether `FLOOR` lies below it; check it on the 5e5 inputs, where it may approach 1e-10.

**History.** The slab floor and the cutoff were 1e-10 when they were separate settings (`SLAB_FLOOR` and `DIAG_FLOOR`, early October 2026). They were lowered to 1e-12 to share one setting with the per-bin boxes' floor, which needed 1e-12 to pass the fit check of the time, and returned to 1e-10 when spike-and-slab became the default, since neither spike-and-slab nor θ needs the lower value.

**The slab floor does not change the products.** With one measurement per section (quick preset, two replicates per floor), moving the slab floor from 1e-12 to 1e-10 changed every product by no more than Monte Carlo noise: subsection totals by a median 0.1% (5–95%: −9% to +11%; two replicates at the same floor differ by −9% to +20%), exceedance probabilities at 1e-5 by a median 0.02 in log₁₀ (5–95%: −0.20 to +0.13, against −0.29 to +0.18 between replicates), mean rates by a median 0.02 decades, and bin totals by at most 0.6%. Only the number of ruptures switched on at rates the data cannot see changed, from about 497 to 379 per draw, and the fractions on mixed slightly better (worst R̂ 1.03 against 1.04). Counting each subsection, raising the slab floor further, to 1e-8, 1e-7, 1e-6 or 1e-5, likewise left χ², the total rate, the bins, subsection totals, exceedance at 1e-5 and 1e-4 and the mean rates unchanged within the spread between seeds; only the number of ruptures on fell, by about 53 per decade, and a few near-empty subsections' medians became exactly 0. A slab floor of 1e-11 instead of 1e-10 (two seeds each, 8 chains × 10,000 sweeps) cost nothing per sweep and switched on about 50 more ruptures per draw at undetectable rates.

**The cutoff changes only the diagnostics.** θ's draws are identical whatever `FLOOR` is; only their scoring changes. With the cutoff at 1e-10, θ's quick runs flag 195–243 ruptures (1.1–1.3%) as slow-mixing, against 259–288 at 1e-12: θ's ruptures spend about 0.8% of their draws between 1e-12 and 1e-10 (counting each subsection), visits too rare to mix well in short runs and of no consequence, and the slow minority carries 23–24% of the total rate either way. Spike-and-slab's diagnostics barely depend on the cutoff. Compare slow-mixing counts only between runs scored at the same cutoff.

## Summarizing the draws

**χ².** Every χ² in this report is the mean over posterior draws, not the χ² of the posterior-mean model m̄. Because the predictions are linear in the rates, the two differ by exactly the total posterior variance of the predictions: E[χ²(m)] = χ²(m̄) + Σ_j Var(y_j).

| run (corrected inputs, one measurement per section, quick preset) | mean χ² over draws | χ² of the posterior-mean model | total predictive variance |
|---|---|---|---|
| spike-and-slab | 140.2 ± 11.7 | 86.1 | 54.1 |
| θ = 1 | 141.8 ± 11.8 | 88.4 | 53.4 |

With the MFD bins counted as two measurements the three were 137.0 ± 12.1, 80.4 and 56.7 under spike-and-slab, and 137.2 ± 11.9, 84.2 and 53.0 under θ. The mean over draws is the fit check. The posterior-mean model's χ² is the number to compare with single models such as NNLS (χ²_min = 31.25; 27.19 with the bins counted as two measurements) or simulated annealing; under spike-and-slab and θ, though, the posterior-mean model is a summary rather than a plausible sample, far less sparse than any draw. About half the excess of a typical draw over the best fit is predictive spread and half systematic.

**Means.** For anything that adds rates (totals, subsection and bin sums, hazard), use means in linear space, or better, compute the quantity for each draw and then summarize. Linear means add up exactly and are robust to the prior's lower end, because each rate's linear mean is dominated by its data-informed upper tail. Geometric means (means in log space) follow the lower end: under the single box they moved 1.85 decades for 4 decades of floor while linear means moved 0.06, and summed over ruptures they gave a total 25 times too small. Under spike-and-slab most draws of a rupture are exactly 0, so geometric means are not even defined. For a single rupture's distribution, use quantiles, which are the same in either space; for weak ruptures they are prior-dependent.

**How much of each total the data see individually.** For each subsection and bin, `undetectable_share` (in the notebook's tables) is the share of its posterior-mean total carried by rates below their own rupture's detection scale σ/‖G_i‖, the rate at which one rupture alone, others fixed, would move χ² by 1. Rates between the collective-negligibility scale (about 2e-9) and that scale are individually invisible but collectively not: a crowd of them can make up a total, and how many there are is largely the prior's choice. From the quick runs:

| | bin totals: median (range) | subsection totals: median |
|---|---|---|
| spike-and-slab | 11% (10% to 13%) | 11% |
| θ = 1 | 10% (8% to 34%) | 15% |
| best nonnegative fit (reference) | 5% (0% to 28%) | — |

Under spike-and-slab and θ, as in the best fit, totals rest mostly on individually detectable rates; counting each subsection, the shares were 7–8% (6% for the best fit). With the MFD bins counted as two measurements the detection scales rise and so do the shares: 17% under spike-and-slab and 22% under θ for the bins (subsections 17% and 24%), and 18% for the best fit. Under the earlier box priors about four-fifths of a typical total or more rested on rates the data cannot see one by one (80% counting each subsection, 91% with one measurement per section). A total with a large share is prior-dependent, however tight its interval looks.

## Samplers and convergence

**Gibbs in log rate** (`gibbs.py`, C kernel). Each rate is drawn exactly from its conditional by a one-dimensional slice sampler in log m, so hard and soft edges are handled exactly; under spike-and-slab an exact Gibbs step also switches each rupture on or off. One sweep costs one pass over the nonzeros of G. The likelihood is flat across the "off" plateau, so one update can carry a rate in or out of it, and ruptures switch freely between clearly on and clearly off; under the single box each did so about 0.30 times per recorded draw, giving about 980 effective draws per 1,000 for nearly every rupture.

**Swap moves** (`--swap-cos 0.99`, on in the notebook) exchange the rates of near-duplicate pairs in the same bin (338,177 pairs with cosine above 0.99 on the corrected inputs with one measurement per section); they are exact, and let near-duplicates exchange the active role. **Transfer moves**, which shift rate between a rupture and up to four sub-ruptures reproducing its column, exist for the single box only (`build_transfers.py`, set aside in `cru_retired_scripts.zip`); they were exact and cheap but gave no measurable per-rupture gain at a 1e-12 floor.

**Convergence.** Even for perfectly independent draws the worst R̂ over 18,000 rates is above 1 (about 1.015 for 4 chains × 250 draws), so R̂ must be judged against that noise baseline, not against 1. Under spike-and-slab and θ a minority of ruptures switch on and off slowly and stay above it: in quick runs 0.3% of ruptures under spike-and-slab (carrying 16–18% of the total rate) and 1.1–1.3% under θ (23–24%), and in the very conservative θ run 4.5–4.7%, with the worst R̂ 1.35–1.38. Longer runs help them only slowly; moves that exchange the active role between alternative ruptures would. χ² and the total rate mix much better (R̂ near 1.01 at the quick preset), and spike-and-slab's number of ruptures on and fractions on are checked too.

**HMC cross-check** (`rhmc.py --param log`, set aside in `cru_retired_scripts.zip`). Under the single box at a floor of 1e-8 (plain likelihood), log-space HMC and Gibbs agreed rupture by rupture on mean log rate: standardized differences had mean +0.009 and sd 1.004, with 0.23% beyond 3 (0.27% expected), and the median absolute difference was 0.034 decades. HMC needed its mass from the Gibbs variances of log rate and its starting points from Gibbs end states; on one core, Gibbs delivered about 22 effective draws per rupture per second against 7–13 for HMC.

**Self-tests** (`gibbs.py --selftest`) compare against exact answers. They cover:
- a truncated normal 30 sd beyond its bound;
- two- and three-rate problems with walls under flat, exponential and log-uniform priors, with soft and hard edges;
- swap and transfer moves;
- per-rupture lower and upper box edges (θ's depths and the soft top);
- the spike-and-slab prior, by enumerating on/off states;
- the retired hierarchical prior.

All pass on both the C and NumPy backends, and multi-threaded Gibbs is bit-identical to single-threaded Gibbs.

## Earlier priors

The workflow's earlier priors were log-uniform boxes ending at a common floor. They still run in `gibbs.py`, for comparison, but are not part of the workflow's tests. Their analyses, on the earlier inputs and counting each slip-rate subsection as its own measurement, established the robust products and showed why the floor could not be chosen from the data.

**Per-bin boxes** (`--prior binbox-floor:-12`, the default until October 2026; `PRIOR_KIND = 'binbox'`). Each bin's ruptures had a log-uniform box from the same soft top at R_j down to a hard floor at 1e-12 common to all bins (`binbox:W` ends each box W decades below its R_j instead). They mixed well (about 980 effective draws per 1,000 for nearly every rupture) and fitted better than the single box before them (χ² 557 against 599 at 10 decades deep), but their depth, 8.5–9.3 decades below the targets, made θ_j ≫ 1, so the prior pushed the bin totals up. On the corrected inputs, counting each subsection, they gave χ² 663 ± 29 (2.6 sd above the fit check then, 446 ± 83), bins a median 23% above their targets (−86% to +85%), about 1,170 ruptures carrying 90% of the rate, and 80% of a typical bin's total carried by individually undetectable rates. Between floors of 1e-12 and 1e-10 their products moved little (subsection totals by a median 6%, exceedance at 1e-5 by 0.05 in log₁₀), but the floor or depth set the sparsity, and the data could not choose it. With one measurement per section they failed the fit check by 26 sd (547 ± 32 against 126 ± 16), after which the default changed to spike-and-slab.

**The single log-boxcar** (`--prior logbox:A:B:SE`, the default before that). Each rate's prior was flat in log₁₀ m between 10^A and 10^B (−12 and −2) with half-Gaussian edges of sd SE decades (1; 0 for hard edges). Most of the floor analyses were done with it:

- **The upper part of the rate population was set by the data:** 400–570 ruptures above 1e-5 and 30–45 above 1e-4 in each draw, for every floor. Below about 1e-6 the rates simply filled the prior's plateau down to the floor, and rupture medians moved about 0.46 decades per decade of floor.
- **Sums followed the floor only weakly.** Rates below 1e-8 carried only 0.04–0.07% of the total rate. Each subsection's total changed, relative to a 1e-12 floor, by a median of +4% at 1e-10 (5–95%: −6% to +13%), +12% at 1e-8 and −9% at 1e-20, mostly through the over-prediction, which shrank as the floor fell; with the χ² cap, by 1.5% between 1e-8 and 1e-12.
- **Exceedance probabilities moved much less than medians.** Relative to a 1e-12 floor, the median change in log₁₀ P(rate > 1e-5) was +0.09, +0.04 and −0.15 at floors 1e-8, 1e-10 and 1e-20, and in log₁₀ P(> 1e-4) −0.08, −0.04 and −0.03: the data fix how many ruptures sit above 1e-5 (around 400–450 per draw), and largely which ones may. These and the sums are the robust products the notebook reports.
- **The floor acted as a sparsity setting.** Lowering it improved the fit but concentrated the rate: the ruptures carrying 90% of it fell from 2,030 at 1e-8 to 1,311, 969 and 474 at 1e-10, 1e-12 and 1e-20, with no corner to pick on the misfit–sparsity curve. The fit check of the time (447 ± 84) rejected 1e-8 (+6.5 sd) and 1e-10 (+3.2 sd) and accepted 1e-12 (+1.8 sd) and 1e-20, which is where the 1e-12 floor came from.
- **The evidence could not choose the floor.** Stepping-stone estimates (`evidence.py`, set aside; within 0.04 in log Z of exact integrals on a two-rupture problem) rose steadily as the floor dropped: −13,416, −7,408 and −5,151 nats at 1e-8, 1e-12 and 1e-16. Prior draws imply a total rate of about 200/yr for a box from 1e-12 to 1e-2, against 0.0226/yr from the bin targets, and with the top fixed a lower floor is the box's only way to make the data more probable a priori. Placing the box on the average data-imposed rate (`logbox-centre:W:SE`, `logbox-mean:W:SE`) did not fix this: centring raises the top with the centre.
- **Edges:** hard edges instead of soft ones raised individual rates by about 0.55 decades and fitted worse (χ² 1,517 against 986 at a 1e-8 floor). A soft lower edge of sd 1 decade acts as about one extra decade of depth.

**The misfit cap** (removed in October 2026). This optional model class kept only models with χ² ≤ X (by default the fit check's expected value; 447 when these runs were made), sampled by coordinate hit-and-run on that set, so draws sat on its boundary and X was in effect the misfit chosen. It was a separate model class, not a filter: under the plain likelihood no draw of the single box at 1e-12 satisfied χ² ≤ 447 (599 ± 28). With the single box it removed the over-prediction while moving individual rates by only about 0.05 decades (total rate 0.0260 against 0.0268) and made sums less sensitive to the floor; tighter caps (X = 200 and 100) pulled the bins toward their targets but did not converge in 2,400 sweeps. The pressure-free priors pass the fit check under the plain likelihood, so the cap was no longer needed, and the Gaussian likelihood is now the only one.

**Hierarchical lognormal prior** (`--prior lognormal`, retired). log₁₀ m_i ~ Normal(μ_bin, s²), with one centre per magnitude bin and one shared within-bin spread under broad hyperpriors (s uniform on 0.05–5 decades, each μ_bin normal with mean −6 and sd 10 decades), sampled exactly, with centred and non-centred hyperparameter updates (ASIS), and passing its self-tests. It was not double-dipping: the bins come from the forward model, and the centres and spread were estimated jointly with the rates. A pilot (χ² 257, total 0.0231, spread about 1.55 decades) and a long run (4 chains × 36,000 sweeps: χ² 239, total 0.0230, bins 1.02× their targets, about 40 ruptures above 1e-4) found roughly the pressure-free answer, but the hyperparameters never converged: the spread's R̂ was 1.86, and it was still rising (from 2.10 to 2.33 decades between the halves of the kept draws) while the centres fell. Widening the spread while lowering the centres keeps each bin's total fixed, so this was a slide along a ridge, driven by the same pull toward sparsity; partial non-centring (`--pnc-pilot`) and a fixed spread (`--s-fixed`) did not help. Spike-and-slab replaced it as the way to let the data set the sparsity.

## Why not a linearly uniform prior

A prior uniform in each rate on [0, 1] encodes the expectation that rates are spread linearly, and in 18,356 dimensions it is far from neutral. Positivity turns the directions the data cannot see into a cone whose volume grows as roughly t^18,355 when all predictions scale by t. That volume overwhelms a likelihood with σ = 1.

| flat prior on [0, 1] | plain likelihood | uniform on χ² ≤ 447 |
|---|---|---|
| χ² | 10,274 ± 160 (log-HMC: 10,262 ± 156) | 446.3–447.0 (on the boundary) |
| predictions projected on the data | 1.709 ± 0.008 | 1.024 ± 0.002 |
| total rate | 0.0518 | 0.0267 |
| per-rupture marginals | exponential (median sd/mean 0.999) | exponential (median sd/mean 1.004) |

Gibbs reaches the flat-prior state within its first four sweeps from the best fit, and log-HMC reaches it independently. Per row, flat-prior predictions range from about 0.1 to 5.7 times the observed values. Its rate population is compressed into roughly 1e-8 to 1e-4, peaking near 1e-6, rather than spreading over many decades. Its subsection totals are 2.1× those of the single log-box at 1e-12 (median; 5–95%: 0.7–3.1×), and its rupture medians sit about 1.3 decades above those of the 1e-8 log-box.

The flat-prior runs also served as the first sampler validation. Gibbs and log-space HMC agreed on all 18,356 posterior means: standardized differences had mean −0.020 and sd 1.000, with 0.23% beyond 3. Gibbs delivered about 100 effective draws per rupture per second against 10–15 for HMC. Reflective HMC in linear space failed outright. Each rate lies within a few percent of its conditional sd from the wall at zero, so trajectories made about 28,000 reflections per chain per 0.1 time units. Acceptance was zero at step sizes well below the stability limit, and reached 0.50 only at h = 3e-4. This is why every sampler here works in log rate.

## What this means for the investigation

Use spike-and-slab as the production setting, with θ = 1 as its cross-check. Report sums (subsection, bin and paleo-site totals, and functionals built from them) and per-rupture exceedance probabilities at about 1e-5 and above as data products, using linear means or per-draw functionals rather than geometric means. Report rupture medians and lower quantiles as prior-dependent; under spike-and-slab and θ most are 0. Where spike-and-slab and θ agree, a result does not depend on how the lower end of the rates is treated; where they differ, report both. Treat each run's χ² distribution as a fit check against the expected value cleaning prints: 126 ± 16 on the corrected inputs with one measurement per slip-rate section. Whether any other downstream functional aᵀm is data-determined can be checked the same way, by computing it under both priors.

Choices that remain modelling decisions:
- how the slip-rate rows count (one measurement per section, the default; each subsection its own measurement with `SLIP_SECTIONS = 'none'`; or sections from a mapping file);
- the MFD bins' uncertainty level: the inputs' design value (the default), counted as the two measurements the bins are projected from (three times it, the bin-to-bin shape kept), or a level set for the M7.0–7.1 bin (`MFD_UNCERTAINTY`); the data cannot set it; it moves the bins and the total rate by several percent, and looser levels let single draws leave whole bins empty ("MFD uncertainty");
- the prior: spike-and-slab (the default) or θ = 1, with spike-and-slab's Beta(1, 1) prior on the fractions on, its slab floor (1e-10; the products held for slab floors from 1e-12 to 1e-5) and the soft top at each bin's target;
- pressure-free priors versus priors that push the bin totals up (the earlier box priors), which give much denser solutions, with exceedance probabilities at 1e-5 about 7 times higher; only the pressure-free priors pass the fit check with one measurement per section.

Prior-free extremal bounds for a few key functionals (a linear program with per-row misfit bounds) would complement the posterior and remain an option.

## Running it

`cru_workflow.ipynb` runs the whole workflow from one configuration cell: self-tests, cleaning (with one measurement per slip-rate section, `SLIP_SECTIONS = 'auto'`; `'none'` counts each subsection, and a file name takes the sections from a mapping file in `DATA_DIR`; and with the MFD bins' uncertainties as the inputs give them, `MFD_UNCERTAINTY = 'as-given'`, or counted as two measurements, `'two-measurements'`, or a level for the M7.0–7.1 bin), and replicate runs of the default model (`PRIOR_KIND = 'spikeslab'`: spike-and-slab with its slab floor at `FLOOR = -10`). The prior is chosen by `PRIOR_KIND`, one at a time: `'spikeslab'` (the default) or `'theta'` (θ = 1); `'binbox'`, the earlier default, still runs, for comparison. Each run of the notebook gives one result set; to see how a result depends on the prior, run it again with the other prior (a second setting of the same prior in its own `RUN_DIR`). The notebook then runs convergence checks against the targets in TESTING.md and the R̂ noise baseline (and, for spike-and-slab, on the number of ruptures on and each bin's fraction on), reports the slow-mixing minority separately with its share of the total rate, and writes CSV tables of the results, keyed by original rupture and row numbers (the rupture table also records each rupture's R̂ and effective draws). It has a quick preset, a conservative production preset and a very conservative one, with separate run lengths for each prior.

**Row groups.** The likelihood treats all rows alike; the row groups matter for the priors, which work per magnitude bin (the magnitude-bin rows), and for the subsection summaries (the slip-rate rows). Both are detected automatically during cleaning, so `BLOCKS = None` (the default) needs nothing named. The slip-rate rows are the leading rows, up to the first zero-forcing penalty row (right-hand side 0, entries only on penalised ruptures), so a slip-rate row with zero slip does not end them. The magnitude-bin rows are the rows with one constant coefficient whose supports split the ruptures into disjoint groups. Any group named in `BLOCKS` (by original row numbers, which change with the input) is checked against the detection, and cleaning stops on a mismatch. The paleo rows only label the per-group fit summaries; unnamed, they get an automatic label. Within the slip-rate rows, cleaning then finds the sections (runs of copies of one slip rate) and prints them, with notes on targets shared between sections and on neighbours with identical targets that share no rupture, and the fit check; on new inputs, compare the sections with the fault model.

**Folders.** Code, data and outputs can live in separate folders. In the notebook, `DATA_DIR` holds the input files (read only) and `RUN_DIR` receives everything the workflow writes; both default to the notebook's folder, which holds the scripts, and relative paths are taken from it. The configuration cell stops with a clear message if a script or data file is missing. Within `RUN_DIR`, the cleaned problem and diagnostics stay at the top, and each prior's runs go into a subfolder named after it (`spikeslab/` or `theta/`, each with a copy of `workflow_config.json`). The CSV tables and summary figure go in the prior's folder, which also gets a log of everything the notebook prints (its messages, the scripts' output, warnings, and errors with their tracebacks; in Jupyter also when each cell started and how long it took, and which figures were shown), `workflow_log_<date>-<time>.txt`, a new one each time the configuration cell runs. Runs with different priors therefore never overwrite each other; re-running a prior replaces its files, but not its earlier logs, so a second setting of the same prior needs its own `RUN_DIR`, as does another `SLIP_SECTIONS` or `MFD_UNCERTAINTY`, which change the cleaned problem both priors share (the notebook warns if a `RUN_DIR` holds another prior's runs made on a problem cleaned otherwise). From the command line, run the scripts by their path from the run folder and pass the data files' paths:

```
cd /path/to/runs
python /path/to/code/preliminaries.py --A /path/to/data/cru_A.txt --d /path/to/data/cru_D.txt --out cru_clean.npz
python /path/to/code/gibbs.py --problem cru_clean.npz --out main_rep0      # plus the run settings below
```

Each script finds the diagnostics from `preliminaries.py` (`prelim_results.npz`) next to the problem file it is given, or wherever `--prelim` points. Outputs go wherever `--out` points (for `compare_runs.py`, `--outdir`), relative to the working folder. The commands below assume everything is run from the run folder, with the scripts and data files reachable by those paths.

Requirements: numpy, scipy, pandas and matplotlib, plus a C compiler for the Gibbs kernel. On macOS, `cc` comes with the Xcode command line tools (`xcode-select --install`). Without a compiler `gibbs.py` falls back to NumPy, about 100× slower. The kernel compiles once into `~/.cache/cru_gibbs/`.

The individual commands:


Outputs: `cru_clean.npz` is the cleaned problem (under the defaults, with each slip-rate section's rows divided by √k; it records the row scales, the sections, the slip-section setting and the fit check, and the MFD setting and multiplier, `mfd_uncertainty` and `mfd_scale`). `prelim_results.npz`, `prelim_summary.txt` and `prelim.png` are the diagnostics; for an `--out` other than `cru_clean.npz` these names get the `--out` stem as a prefix. Each run writes `<out>.npz`, holding traces, predictions and (with draws stored) per-rate mean, sd, quantiles and R̂; `<out>_samples.npy`, all draws as float32 with shape (chains, draws, n), unless `--no-samples` is given; and `<out>_stream.npz`, running summaries kept during the run, written either way. The running summaries hold, per rupture: per-chain, per-half sums of the clipped log rate and its square (split R̂, exactly), per-chain batch means (effective draws), counts above 1e-5 and 1e-4, sums of the rate and of its part below the detection scale, and a log-rate histogram in 0.01-decade bins (`--hist-width`; quantiles and modes), saved compressed; and, per draw, the subsection and bin totals. They match the draw-based values exactly, and the quantiles to within the bin width (typically 0.001–0.004 decades). From them the notebook always writes rate vectors (`rate_vectors.csv` and `.npz`, in each prior's folder; for runs made before running summaries existed, from their stored draws): one row per rupture of the original problem, indexed as the columns of `cru_A`, with penalised ruptures flagged and set to 0, giving the posterior mean, median, mode, and the 2.275%, 15.865%, 84.135% and 97.725% values. The mode is defined on a log scale: 0 if a rate below 1e-16 has probability at least 0.5, otherwise the peak of the smoothed log-rate density above 1e-16; for weakly constrained ruptures it is poorly defined, since that density is flat over the prior's range, and under spike-and-slab and θ most modes are 0. In the notebook, `OUTPUT_DRAWS = False` runs without draws: the convergence checks, tables and summary figure then come from the running summaries. Representative draws for hazard (`REPRESENTATIVES`, 20 by default; 0 turns them off): each run keeps 1,000 whole draws whatever `OUTPUT_DRAWS` says (`gibbs.py --keep-draws`, recording only: `<out>_kept.npy`, float32, one row per draw, and `<out>_kept_index.npy`, each draw's chain and record number), and `select_draws.py` picks weighted representatives from the replicates' pooled kept draws. It describes each draw by its participation rate per subsection and magnitude bin, clusters the logarithms of those rates (k-means on their principal components), takes the kept draw nearest each cluster centre, and calibrates the weights so the weighted mean participation rates come close to the exact posterior means. The representatives are columns `rep_01`, `rep_02`, ... of `rate_vectors.csv`; `representatives.csv` gives each one's weight, cluster share, run, chain, record, χ² and total rate; `rate_vectors.npz` gains the pooled kept draws (`kept`, `kept_index`), `rep` and `rep_weight`; and `select_report.txt` compares 10, 20 and 40 representatives with the full posterior (means of the total, bin and subsection totals against the exact means; 16%, 50% and 84% points against every recorded draw; each representative's χ² and total rate against the posterior's 1–99% range; the weights). The weights are quadrature weights for the posterior as a whole, and the `mean` column remains the exact source for mean hazard, which is linear in the rates. `plot_rate_magnitude.py`, run by the notebook after the summary figure, plots log10 of each rupture's posterior mean rate (the rate vectors' `mean` column) against its magnitude bin, with each bin's target total R_j and its total of the means, and puts means below 10^`FLOOR`, including 0, in a band at the bottom (`fig_rate_magnitude.png`, with a per-bin table in `fig_rate_magnitude.txt`); several rate vectors give panels side by side. A rupture's magnitude is known only to its bin: the bin rows are counted up from 6.80–6.90 in steps of 0.1 in their original order (`--lowest-bin`, `--bin-width`), an order the script checks against rupture sizes (on the corrected inputs the median number of subsections per rupture rises from 3 in the lowest bin to 113 in the highest, rank correlation +1.00). `plot_slip_rates.py`, run by the notebook after it, plots each slip-rate section's target against the mean model's prediction (G times the rate vectors' `mean` column, which is also the posterior mean of the prediction), on a linear and on a log scale (`fig_slip_rates.png`, with a section table and a row table in `fig_slip_rates.txt`). Each section's mean prediction over its subsections is a blue dot, with its subsections in grey behind it. The rows are divided by their uncertainties in the inputs, which do not give the uncertainties themselves, so both axes are in units of each section's measurement uncertainty (any √k weighting is undone for display): a grey band marks predictions within one uncertainty of their targets, and the sections furthest from their targets in those units are labelled with their original rows. The subtitle gives the mean model's χ² over the slip-rate rows as the likelihood weights them (for the best fit 17.0 under the defaults, 14.1 with the MFD bins counted as two measurements and 38.3 counting each subsection, as cleaning reports) and the share of sections within one and two uncertainties; several rate vectors give one row of panels each. `plot_paleo_rates.py`, run by the notebook after it, does the same for the paleo rows (every row in neither the slip-rate nor the magnitude-bin group, unless `--paleo-block` names them) on log-log axes (`fig_paleo_rates.png`, with a per-row table in `fig_paleo_rates.txt`): each row's target against the mean model's prediction, with the posterior 16–84% range of the prediction as a vertical bar, taken from the predictions recorded by the runs next to the rate vectors (`main_rep0.npz`, ...). In units of each row's uncertainty a point only moves along the 1:1 line, so its distance from the line is the ratio of prediction to target, and since a target in these units is one over its relative uncertainty, the best-measured sites lie furthest right; the shaded bands mark predictions within one and two uncertainties, and the rows furthest from their targets are labelled. Several rate vectors give one panel each. `compare_runs.py --a <run> --b <run>` compares two runs rate by rate and draws a figure (`fig_compare.png`). `compare_priors.py <run> <run> ...` produces the tables comparing priors (spike-and-slab against θ, say): fit, bin totals against targets, sparsity, convergence, and changes in the robust products.

For large configurations the draws dominate disk use: at 211,000 ruptures and the very conservative preset, about 68 GB per spike-and-slab or θ run. Without draws (`OUTPUT_DRAWS = False`, or `--no-samples`), each run keeps only its running summaries: compressed on disk to a small fraction of the draws, though the finer histogram needs about 1.4 GB of memory during the run at that size.

## Before routine use

What to test and watch for before routine use is in a separate document, `TESTING.md`: whether a new setup reproduces these results, whether the runs converge (including the very conservative preset), whether the conclusions survive the modelling choices, whether the workflow will scale, a checklist for every run, and the known weak spots.

- **Spike-and-slab's long run:** the very conservative run on the corrected inputs under the defaults is still to do. - **Representative draws:** in the first test (conservative preset, spike-and-slab, 2 × 1,000 kept draws, counting each subsection), 20 representatives matched the posterior mean of the total rate (to 0.0%) and stayed inside the posterior's χ² and total-rate ranges with no zero weight, but the worst bin total was 4.6% off its exact mean, 76% of subsection means were within 10%, and 68–79% of the subsections' 16%, 50% and 84% points were within 15%, short of the proposed 2%, 90% and 90%. Forty representatives did little better (2.4%, 79%, 74–81%), and the quick preset gave much the same, so run length is not the limit. About a third of the subsections have totals whose coefficient of variation exceeds 0.45 (median 0.30), more than 20 draws can average to within 10%. With one measurement per slip-rate section (quick preset), 20 representatives matched 61% of subsection means within 10% under spike-and-slab and 53% under θ (40: 66% and 63%). With the MFD bins counted as two measurements their posterior spread triples, to about 30% per bin, and 20 representatives matched 58% of subsection means within 10% and the worst bin to 16% (quick preset, spike-and-slab). Revisit these tolerances with the hazard team, or calibrate the weights on the subsection and bin totals directly.
- **Evidence:** done for single-box floors, where it keeps rising as the floor drops; not done for spike-and-slab or θ (if wanted, from a few tempered runs by hand).
- **Sparse sections:** check the sections whose totals collapse against the fault model; the slip-rate figure is a first look. With one measurement per section, in quick runs, spike-and-slab and θ predict 18 sections (72 subsections) at under a quarter of their targets, all with targets only 0.6–2.4 uncertainties above zero, so each section's mean prediction stays within 2.3 uncertainties of its target and the data do not rule out near-zero slip there (counting each subsection, 41–43 subsections, within 1.42). Their χ² now sits 1 sd above the fit check, so the earlier worry that it fell well below, pointing at generous uncertainties, is gone. Paleo site row 853 is the same story at a site: the best fit nearly matches it, but both priors predict about 0 there in most draws (`fig_paleo_rates.png`).
- **Slip-rate sections:** detected as runs of copies (68 on the corrected inputs). A mapping of subsections to sections from the fault model (`SLIP_SECTIONS = 'file.txt'`) would settle the 32 sections that share their target with another and the 6 neighbouring pairs with identical targets that share no rupture, and should be used for new inputs if the detection looks doubtful.
- **Geodetic slip rates:** rows that vary from subsection to subsection are left as given, each its own measurement. If a geodetic model's errors are correlated along a fault (they come from a smooth fit), each row counts as more independent information than it carries; that would need a covariance, or an effective number of measurements per fault.
- **Uniform slip along a section:** the fit check assumes each section's true slip rate is uniform along it; real variation along a section adds to the expected χ², and the weighting tolerates it at √k times the measurement's uncertainty per subsection. Whether that tolerance suits the fault model is worth a look.
- **Scaling to 5e5:** sparse preliminaries and filtered swap moves, with timing tests at about 1e5 ruptures first; running summaries already allow runs without stored draws (`OUTPUT_DRAWS = False`).
- **Extremal bounds:** prior-free bounds for chosen functionals.
- **MFD covariance:** the bins' uncertainty is set by one multiplier (`MFD_UNCERTAINTY`), which keeps them independent. A correlated covariance, the two measurements' within-branch uncertainty projected onto the bins plus a small model error per bin, would let the MFD shift and tilt as a whole while staying smooth. It needs whitened bin rows, dense across the bins, which the per-bin priors and bin targets would have to be adapted to ("MFD uncertainty").
- **Optional refinement:** a similarity prior between overlapping ruptures.
- **HMC and evidence scripts:** `rhmc.py` (log-space HMC, the independent cross-check described above) and `evidence.py` are set aside from the code set, in `cru_retired_scripts.zip`. An HMC cross-check of the current priors would bring `rhmc.py` back; evidence comparisons can be done by hand with a few tempered runs.
- **NUTS:** the cross-check from the design notes was not run, since there is no JAX in the sandbox. Log-space HMC already uses NUTS's transform and agrees with Gibbs.
