# CRU 2e4 configuration: rupture rates under log-uniform priors

Development run in a claude.ai sandbox (1 CPU core, 4 GB RAM), October 2026. Inputs: `cru_A.txt` (G) and `cru_D.txt` (d), with σ = 1 for every row (the rows are already normalized by their uncertainties). Every number below comes from the scripts in this folder and can be regenerated with the commands at the end. Timings are for one core; the M2 runs chains in parallel.

The corrected inputs clean to 446 rows (the effective number of constraints) and 18,243 rupture rates; the earlier inputs, used for much of the analysis below, cleaned to 447 × 18,356. Every χ² is on the cleaned rows.

**How to read the numbers.** The defaults are spike-and-slab, one measurement per slip-rate section, and the MFD bins' uncertainties as the inputs give them. The Summary and "Slip-rate sections" give their results on the corrected inputs; "MFD uncertainty" gives what counting the bins as the two measurements they are projected from would change; the sections on spike-and-slab and θ explain the two priors and the tests behind them. Many of the analyses further down were made earlier, on the earlier inputs, under box priors and counting each slip-rate subsection as its own measurement; their numbers are as measured, and are marked where it matters.

**Default model.**

- **Prior:** spike-and-slab per magnitude bin (`--prior spikeslab`, `gibbs.py`'s default and the notebook's only prior). Each rupture is either off, with a rate of exactly 0, or on, with a log-uniform rate from a slab floor at 1e-10 up to a soft top at its bin's target rate R_j, whose width is the bin's own uncertainty. The fraction of ruptures switched on is estimated in each bin under a Beta(1, 1) prior, so the data, not a floor or a depth, decide how many ruptures are active, and the prior puts no pressure on the bin totals.
- **Cross-check:** none is run routinely. The square-root slab (`--slab sqrt`, a `gibbs.py` option for the command line: the same spike-and-slab with an on rupture's rate uniform in √rate, density m^−1/2 instead of 1/m, so small rates cost more and fewer ruptures are on) took that role in October 2026 and was dropped at the end of the month, the request behind it having been misphrased; its results stay on record ("Fits and the prior's lower end"). θ = 1 (`--prior binbox-theta:1:0`, a `gibbs.py` option: per-bin log-uniform boxes with depths chosen so that the prior puts no pressure on the bin totals) played the role before it and is kept for comparison only.
- **Likelihood:** Gaussian, exp(−χ²/2) with the given σ, for every prior, plus by default a balance term (`DOWN_THE_MIDDLE = True`; `gibbs.py --down-the-middle`): each data type's relative shortfall, summed predictions against summed targets, costs A s² with one A for all types, or, with `DOWN_THE_MIDDLE = 'zero-model'`, each type's own A, its zero-model misfit ("Balance between over- and underprediction"). Each run's χ² distribution, over the data rows alone, serves as a fit check. The χ² cap, an optional model class that kept only models with χ² at most a threshold, was removed in October 2026 ("Earlier priors").
- **MFD bins:** their uncertainties as the inputs give them (`preliminaries.py --mfd-uncertainty as-given`; `MFD_UNCERTAINTY = 'as-given'` in the notebook): 10% at M7.0–7.1, a design value, growing as R_j^−0.25 towards the rarer bins, each bin its own measurement. `'two-measurements'` counts the bins as the two measurements they are projected from, the rate of M ≥ 5 earthquakes and the b-value, multiplying every bin's uncertainty by √(bins / 2) = 3; a number sets the M7.0–7.1 bin's relative uncertainty ("MFD uncertainty").
- **Slip-rate rows:** one measurement per fault section. The inputs copy each section's slip rate to its k subsections; cleaning divides each section's rows by √k, so the measurement counts once (`preliminaries.py --slip-sections auto`; `SLIP_SECTIONS = 'auto'` in the notebook). Rows unlike their neighbours keep their weight.
- **Sampler:** Gibbs in log rate, exact, with exact on/off steps under spike-and-slab and exact swap moves between near-duplicate ruptures.

Box priors with a common floor, the per-bin boxes (`binbox-floor:-12`, the default until October 2026) and the single log-boxcar before them, still run in `gibbs.py`, for comparison only: their priors push the bin totals up, and with one measurement per section the per-bin boxes fail the fit check by a wide margin ("Earlier priors"). A linearly uniform prior is not used; a later section summarizes why.

The per-bin boxes, the default until October 2026, gave 547 ± 32, 26 sd above, because their prior pushed the bin totals up; after that result the default changed.
- **Total rate:** 0.0218 per year, against a bin-target total of 0.0214. Bin totals sit a median 1.9% above their targets (+0.4% to +7.3%), close to the best fit's +1.0% to +4.9%: the prior puts no pressure on them.
- **Population:** about 380 ruptures are switched on in each draw, most at rates the data cannot see; about 100 are above 1e-5 and 45 above 1e-4, and about 47 carry 90% of the total rate.
- **Slip rates:** the mean predictions of 72% of the 68 sections lie within one measurement uncertainty of their targets, and 97% within two. 18 low-slip sections (72 subsections) are predicted below a quarter of their targets, all with targets only 0.6–2.4 uncertainties above zero.
- **Block fit:** each row block fits about as its stated uncertainties say: χ² per independent measurement 1.04 for the 68 slip-rate sections, 1.00 for the 18 bins and 1.29 for the 40 paleo rows (+1.3 sd). The bins' misfit is almost all the model's freedom (17.1 of 18.1), so it cannot test their width.
- **Paleo rates:** the mean model's χ² over the 40 paleo rows is 34 (78% within one uncertainty, 92% within two). Most sites are predicted somewhat below their targets (median ratio 0.82), as in the best fit (0.94, with 34 of 40 below). Three sites are about two uncertainties low: rows 860 and 861, which the best fit also leaves at about half their targets, and row 853, which the best fit nearly matches but whose posterior median prediction is about 0, its ruptures off in most draws. θ gives the same.
- **MFD uncertainty:** the bins' uncertainty level is a design value (10% at M7.0–7.1), and counted as 18 independent measurements the bins pin the MFD's total rate to ±2.8%. Counted instead as the two measurements they are projected from (`MFD_UNCERTAINTY = 'two-measurements'`: every bin's uncertainty ×3), they let the slip-rate and paleo rows raise the bins a median 7.9% above their targets and the total rate by 5%, fit the paleo rows slightly better (χ² per measurement 1.22) and move subsection totals a median 4%, with exceedance probabilities and the population within Monte Carlo noise. Single draws, though, can then leave a whole bin empty: some bin fell below 1% of its target in 4.2–4.3% of the draws of a very conservative run, and in none of the 16,000 draws of a conservative run as given ("MFD uncertainty").

The data determine sums of rates and the upper part of the rate population, but not the rates of individual weak ruptures:
- **Data-determined:** subsection and bin totals, the number of ruptures above about 1e-5, and each rupture's posterior probability of exceeding 1e-5 or 1e-4. Under spike-and-slab they moved by no more than Monte Carlo noise for slab floors from 1e-12 to 1e-5, and spike-and-slab and θ = 1, two different treatments of the lower end, agree on them (exceedance probabilities within 0.02–0.16 in log₁₀, counting each subsection).
- **Prior-determined:** individual rates below the detection scale (about 3e-5 to 2e-4 for one rupture alone), including medians and lower quantiles. Most ruptures are off in most draws, so their medians and modes are 0, and whether a weak rupture is on reflects the prior on the fractions.
- **The largest prior sensitivity:** priors that push the bin totals up, the earlier box priors, give much denser solutions: about 490 ruptures above 1e-5 per draw instead of about 100, and exceedance probabilities at 1e-5 about 7 times higher (counting each subsection). Only the pressure-free priors pass the fit check.

At 211,000 ruptures two things change ("Fits and the prior's lower end"). The mean model's shortfall in the slip and paleo rates grows, because the posterior spreads each bin's rate over its ruptures' magnitudes as the bin's average does while the best fit takes the larger ones; no change to the slab or to how the fractions on are shared removes it, and the paleo rows are the block in tension. And the subsections' event rates depend on the slab: the square-root slab puts them at a median 0.62 of the default's, with the same fit.

The reference posterior at 211,000 ruptures is the very conservative run of the configuration adopted in late October 2026 (one measurement per slip-rate section, the bins as given, the balance term at each type's own weight, a slab floor of 1e-12). Its three replicates pass every convergence check, with χ² 554 ± 24 against 590 ± 34 and eight slowly mixing ruptures carrying 1.9% of the total rate; judged from its kept draws, its chains agree on each rupture's whole distribution as perfectly mixed chains would, and its products are the conservative run's to within Monte Carlo noise ("The reference run at 211,000 ruptures").

Gibbs in log rate is exact and needs no tuning. Under spike-and-slab a minority of ruptures switch on and off slowly (0.3% of them in quick runs, carrying 16–18% of the total rate), and the fractions on mix slowly in short runs, so its runs are long; its sweeps are cheap, because off ruptures rarely change any residuals. Log-space HMC agreed with Gibbs rupture by rupture (under the single box), and the self-tests check every prior against exact answers.

## Slip-rate sections: one measurement per section

A fault section's slip rate is one measurement. The inputs copy it, with its uncertainty, to each of the section's k subsections: the 388 slip-rate rows carry only 46 distinct values. Counted as independent, each section's slip rate entered the likelihood k times, as if its uncertainty were √k smaller (2.9 times on average). The default now counts each measurement once: cleaning divides each section's rows by √k (`preliminaries.py --slip-sections auto`; `SLIP_SECTIONS = 'auto'` in the notebook).

**What the weighting does.** For a section with target s and subsection predictions y_1 … y_k, in units of its uncertainty σ,

Σ_j (y_j − s)² / (k σ²) = (ȳ − s)² / σ² + Σ_j (y_j − ȳ)² / (k σ²).

The section's mean prediction ȳ is fitted to the measurement once, with the stated uncertainty, and the subsections' departures from their own mean are penalized as if each subsection's uncertainty were σ√k. Dividing the rows by √k is therefore the same as giving each subsection's copy the uncertainty σ√k: the measurement keeps its information about the section as a whole, the copies stop counting as k independent measurements, and the subsections are still asked, loosely, to share one slip rate.

**Finding the sections.** The inputs carry no section labels, so cleaning looks for runs of neighbouring rows that hold copies: identical targets (as normalized in the inputs) and identical coefficients for every rupture both rows contain. On the corrected inputs:
- 68 sections of 2–21 subsections (median 5), and no single subsections;
- all 320 neighbouring pairs with identical targets that share a rupture also have identical coefficients; of the 35 neighbouring pairs with different targets that share ruptures, 1 would pass the coefficient test, so both tests are needed;
- 6 pairs of neighbours with identical targets share no rupture, so they cannot be confirmed as copies and count as separate sections: the 62 runs of identical values become 68 sections;
- 32 sections share their target with another section (geologic slip rates are often round numbers); a mapping file would settle whether any of them belong together.

Rows unlike their neighbours keep their weight, so a geodetic slip-rate model, whose values vary from subsection to subsection, is used as given, each row its own measurement. On synthetic variants of the inputs, continuously varying targets gave 388 single subsections, as did equal slip rates with differing uncertainties; the geologic inputs give the 68 sections. `--slip-sections FILE` (`SLIP_SECTIONS = 'file.txt'`, in `DATA_DIR`) takes the sections from a file of `original_row section` lines instead, for example from the fault model: a missing or extra slip-rate row stops cleaning, and a section whose rows have different targets is noted. `--slip-sections none` (`SLIP_SECTIONS = 'none'`) keeps every subsection as its own measurement, the inputs as given, as a sensitivity test in its own `RUN_DIR`; it reproduces the earlier results byte for byte (the cleaned problem, the diagnostics, the rate vectors and every table). The cleaned problem records the row scales, each slip-rate row's section, the setting and the fit check (`row_scale`, `section`, `slip_sections`, `chi2_expect`).

**The fit check.** If the uncertainties are right, a model matching the truth has an expected χ² of Σ_s k_s w_s² + n_other, with variance 2 Σ_s (k_s w_s²)² + 2 n_other, where w_s is the weight of section s's rows and n_other the number of other rows: one shared error counted k_s times widens the spread. With w_s = 1/√k_s each section contributes 1 ± √2, giving 68 + 58 = 126 ± 16 on the corrected inputs. With `'none'` it is 446 ± 80 (446 ± 83 earlier, when the 62 runs of identical values were taken as the sections). The expectation assumes each section's slip rate is uniform along it; real variation along a section would add to it. Cleaning prints the fit check, and the notebook compares every run against it.

## MFD uncertainty: how much the bins count

**The question.** The 18 bin rows hold the regional magnitude–frequency distribution (MFD) that the rupture rates must reproduce, M6.8–8.6 in steps of 0.1, each bin a target rate R_j with a relative uncertainty. They are not independent observations. They are a projection of two measurements, the rate of M ≥ 5 earthquakes and the b-value, whose own uncertainties the logic tree explores through alternative inputs. And their uncertainty level is a design value, not a measurement: the inputs give σ_j / R_j = 0.1 (R_j / R_7.0)^−0.25 exactly, 10.0% at M7.0–7.1, from 9.8% at the highest-rate bin (6.9–7.0) to 15.4% at 8.5–8.6. The 0.1 is set where cru_A and cru_D are built, a holdover of the UCERF3 practice of weighting the data types against each other, whereas the slip-rate and paleo rows are each normalized by their measured uncertainties. The power 0.25, also set there, splits the difference between two views: each bin checked against the earthquakes observed in it, whose Poisson counts grow tenfold per unit of magnitude for b = 1 (power 0.5), and every bin taken from the same N(M ≥ 5) and b (power 0). Cleaning keeps that shape and sets only the level.

**What the inputs' level implies.** Counted as 18 independent measurements, the bins pin the MFD as a whole far more tightly than one bin's uncertainty suggests: from the bin rows alone, the bins' total rate is known to ±2.8% and their slope to ±0.024 in b (a weighted least-squares line through the bin centres). The posterior followed: in quick runs its total rate was ±2.5%, its bins sat +0.4% to +7.3% above their targets, and their misfit was almost all the model's freedom (17.1 of 18.1; "Row-block fit" below), so the data could not say whether that width was right.

**The two-measurements rule** (`'two-measurements'`). If each bin's uncertainty is right as that bin's own, the bins together should carry the information of the two measurements they come from, not of 18. Write the bins' log rates as a projection of two parameters (log N, b) with covariance S, and each bin's variance as its share of it, σ_j² = J_j S J_jᵀ. Treated as independent, with every uncertainty multiplied by c, the bins carry information Jᵀ D⁻¹ J / c² about the two parameters, against S⁻¹ for the measurements themselves; matching the two on average (tr(S Jᵀ D⁻¹ J) / c² = 2, the number of measurements) gives c² = B / 2 for B bins, whatever the projection. So cleaning multiplies every bin's uncertainty by √(B / 2), 3 for the 18 bins, dividing its row of G and d by it. It is the composite-likelihood count that divides each slip-rate section's k copies by √k. The bins then have 30% at M7.0–7.1 (29.4% to 46.3% across the bins), and the bin rows alone give ±8.3% on the bins' total rate and ±0.071 on their slope: the inputs' design value now describes the MFD as a whole, about one bin's worth, rather than each of 18 independent bins. The targets R_j are unchanged, and the soft top's width, the bin's relative uncertainty, follows (0.112–0.165 decades, against 0.041–0.062).

**The settings** (`MFD_UNCERTAINTY` in the notebook; `preliminaries.py --mfd-uncertainty`):
- `'as-given'` (the default): the inputs' uncertainties, each bin its own measurement. The cleaned problem's matrix, targets and row scales are exactly those from before the setting existed, and quick runs reproduce the earlier numbers exactly.
- `'two-measurements'`: the rule above.
- a number u: the relative uncertainty (one sd) of the M7.0–7.1 bin, the other bins keeping the inputs' shape. 0.1 reproduces the inputs, and 0.3 is `'two-measurements'` for these 18 bins. The bins are counted up from 6.80–6.90 in steps of 0.1 in their original row order, as for the rates-by-magnitude figure, and that order is checked against rupture sizes; cleaning stops if no bin starts at M7.0.

Cleaning prints the inputs' uncertainties and the power they follow (R_j^−0.250 here, exactly), the multiplier, and what the bin rows then say about the MFD as a whole. The cleaned problem records the setting and the multiplier (`mfd_uncertainty`, `mfd_scale`), and its `row_scale` includes the multiplier. Another setting changes the problem both priors share, so it needs its own `RUN_DIR`; the notebook warns if a `RUN_DIR` holds another prior's runs made on a problem cleaned otherwise.

- **Representative draws are coarser for the bins.** With the bins' posterior spread tripled, 20 representatives match the bins' means less closely (the worst bin 16% off at the quick preset, against 6% at the inputs' level) and 58% of subsection means to within 10% (61%).
- **Single draws can leave a whole bin empty.** An empty bin costs one over its relative uncertainty squared in χ²: 42–104 at the inputs' level, but only 5–12 with the bins counted as two measurements, and spike-and-slab can switch off all of a bin's ruptures at once. In a very conservative spike-and-slab run with `'two-measurements'` (16 chains × 100,000 sweeps, three replicates, each passing the convergence checks), some bin fell below 1% of its target in 4.19%, 4.21% and 4.29% of the replicates' draws, and a bin sat at exactly 0 in 1.4% of all draws. The bins at M8.2–8.5 emptied most often (0.5–0.7% of draws each; their 1st percentiles at 4.5–11% of their targets), the others in 0.03–0.34% of draws. The replicates agree, so this is the posterior, not slow mixing; as given, none of the 16,000 draws of a conservative spike-and-slab run (8 chains × 20,000 sweeps, two replicates) took a bin below 0.53 of its target. Bin medians and means hardly notice, but anything built from single draws does, the representatives among them, and so does a straight line fitted to a draw's bin totals in log space: summarize such a slope by its median and quantiles, as the table does, not by its mean and sd. A looser level empties bins far more often. At `MFD_UNCERTAINTY = 1` (100% at M7.0–7.1, 98–154% across the bins) an empty bin costs only 0.42–1.04 in χ², and in a very conservative spike-and-slab run at that level (the same preset, three replicates, each passing the convergence checks) some bin fell below 1% of its target in 80.8–81.1% of the replicates' draws, and some bin sat at exactly 0 in 40.7% of all draws. Every bin emptied: each fell below 1% of its target in 3.7–15.8% of the draws and sat at exactly 0 in 1.2–5.6% of them, the bins at M8.2–8.5 most often, so every bin's 1st percentile is 0.

**The fit check** is unchanged, 126 ± 16, still one per bin: in a draw the bins' totals scatter independently, each within its band, so they contribute about 18 whatever their width (18.0 here). Counting the bins as two measurements is about how much they weigh in the likelihood, not about how many residuals they leave; that is unlike a slip-rate section, whose copies leave nearly one residual between them. A band much wider than the bins' true error would show as a bins' χ² well below 18, but with the data this weak on the bins it shows little either way.

**Within a logic-tree branch.** At the inputs' level each branch's MFD is held to ±2.8% on its total rate and ±0.024 in b by the bin rows alone (±2.5% on the total in the posterior), so the spread of the MFD comes almost entirely from the tree's N and b branches, which is what the default assumes. `'two-measurements'` gives each branch a spread of its own, ±8% on the total rate and ±0.07 in b (±6.7% on the total in the posterior), which adds in quadrature to the tree's.

**A fuller treatment** would give the bins a correlated covariance: the two measurements' within-branch uncertainty projected onto the bins (rank two), plus a small model error per bin, so that the MFD could shift and tilt as a whole while staying smooth, which no single multiplier can do. Whitened, the bin rows would become dense combinations of the bins, whereas the per-bin priors and the bin targets now rely on each row being a plain sum over its bin; it is listed under open items.

## Spike-and-slab (default)

Each rupture is either off, with a rate of exactly 0, or on, with a rate from a slab: log-uniform from a slab floor (1e-10 by default, the notebook's `FLOOR`) up to a soft top at its bin's target rate R_j. The fraction of ruptures switched on is estimated separately in each bin, under a Beta(1, 1) prior (`--prior spikeslab:A:TOP:a:b`; `spikeslab` alone gives A = −10, a soft top and Beta(1, 1)).

This separates two jobs that a floor does for a log-uniform box. "Off" is an exact zero, so no floor is needed to define it, and the slab's lower end only has to lie below the scale at which all ruptures together become invisible to the data (about 2e-9 on the corrected inputs; "Floors and cutoff"). How many ruptures are active becomes an explicit quantity that the data estimate in each bin, rather than a side effect of a floor or a depth. The fractions are per bin because rupture counts vary 23-fold across bins (164 to 3,708): a single fraction would hand the crowded bins proportionally more active ruptures, which is the source of the box priors' pressure on the bin totals ("θ = 1").

**The targets and the top.** Each bin row is its constraint divided by the bin's uncertainty: coefficient 1/σ_j for every rupture in the bin, right-hand side R_j/σ_j. So R_j is d_j divided by the row's coefficient, whatever the uncertainty function, and d_j itself is one over the relative uncertainty. On the corrected inputs the targets run from 3.4e-4 to 2.1e-3 per year and sum to 0.0214. The inputs give them relative uncertainties of 9.8–15.4% (10% at M7.0–7.1), which the default keeps; `'two-measurements'` multiplies them by 3, to 29–46% ("MFD uncertainty"), which changes the bins' weight in χ², but not the targets. No rupture can carry more than its bin's true total, which the bin row gives as R_j with its uncertainty σ_j, so the slab (like θ's boxes) ends at R_j with a Gaussian shoulder in log rate of sd log₁₀(1 + σ_j/R_j), 0.041–0.062 decades under the default (0.112–0.165 with the bins counted as two measurements). Unlike a hard top at R_j, it does not truncate the plausible case of a bin whose true total is somewhat above R_j and dominated by one rupture; unlike a hard top at a multiple of R_j, it needs no arbitrary factor; and it follows the bins' uncertainties automatically if they are revised. `TOP` may instead be `hard` (at R_j) or a number F (hard, at F × R_j). Using the targets to place the top is a mild double use of the data, and dominant ruptures do press against it (in about 80% of draws, by up to about three of the bin's uncertainties, counting each subsection), which is why it is soft. An exact test (`selftest_edges`, part of `--selftest`) checks two rates with different shoulder widths, pushed above their tops by the data, against a grid integral on both backends (all |z| ≤ 1.1).

**Sampling.** Because a log-uniform prior is scale-invariant, writing m_i = R_j u_i turns every bin's range into the same one for u, below 1, with G's columns multiplied by their bin's R_j; the sampler runs on u and records rates. The update is exact and needs no numerical integrals. An off rupture keeps a slab value drawn from the slab itself (a pseudo-prior), and whether a rupture is on is a Gibbs step with probability π·L(m) / [π·L(m) + (1 − π)·L(0)], computed from quantities the update already has. An on rupture's rate is updated by the exact slice sampler in log rate. The fractions are redrawn exactly from their Beta conditionals after every sweep (`--hyper-every 1`, the default for this prior), and swap moves still apply between near-duplicates in the same bin, so they can exchange the active role. An exact test (`selftest_spikeslab`, part of `--selftest`) enumerates the on/off states of two rates in one bin, with the fraction integrated out in closed form, against grid integrals on both backends (all |z| ≤ 2.1). Its sweeps are several times cheaper than θ's: off ruptures rarely change any residuals, and swaps between two off ruptures are skipped.

**Results.** On the corrected inputs with one measurement per section, see "Slip-rate sections" (χ² 140 ± 12, bins +1.9%, about 380 ruptures on per draw, 47 carrying 90% of the rate; with the bins counted as two measurements, "MFD uncertainty": 137 ± 12, +7.9%, 360 and 46). Only the number of ruptures switched on at undetectable rates changes, and the estimated fractions absorb it. Floors up to 1e-5 gave the same, and with one measurement per section 1e-10 and 1e-12 agree within Monte Carlo noise ("Floors and cutoff").

- **Sparse sections:** some subsections end up with almost no rate, as under θ; with one measurement per section, 18 sections (72 subsections) are predicted below a quarter of their targets, each within 2.3 uncertainties of it ("Slip-rate sections").

## θ = 1: pressure-free per-bin boxes (the alternative until October 2026)

**Why box priors push.** With its top at R_j, a log-uniform box W_j decades deep makes the prior expect θ_j = n_j / (W_j ln 10) of the bin's ruptures per unit of ln(rate). For many ruptures, the prior distribution of the bin's total is then a generalized Dickman distribution: in units of R_j, its density below the top is proportional to s^(θ−1). Its slope at the target is θ_j − 1, so the prior shifts the bin total by roughly σ_j²(θ_j − 1), where σ_j is the bin row's relative uncertainty. Shallow boxes (θ_j > 1) push up, very deep ones (θ_j < 1) push down, and θ_j = 1 is flat below R_j. The density then falls gently above R_j, a resistance to overshoot of only 1–2%. Box priors with a common floor have θ_j of about 4 to 200, and runs of the single box (floors 1e-10 and 1e-12) and of per-bin boxes (10 and 12 decades deep) bore this out (earlier inputs, counting each subsection): the predicted pull (a median +26% to +39%) ranked the bins as the observed one did (+16% to +22%; rank correlation 0.83–0.87). The observed shift is about 60% of the prior-only prediction, because the slip-rate and paleo rows pull the totals back. What matters is θ, not where the top sits.

**Pressure-free depths.** W_j = n_j / ln 10 decades gives θ_j = 1: from 71 decades (the 164-rupture bin) to 1,610 (the 3,708-rupture bin) on the corrected inputs. A simulation for the median bin confirms the flat density below R_j (0.96–1.04 in relative terms) and the fall above it (0.93, 0.74, 0.62 at 1.1, 1.3 and 1.5 R_j). Within a factor of 2 of these depths (θ between 0.5 and 2) the pull stays near 1%. Rates of 10^-1,600 are no problem in log space; they are exactly zero in the predictions.

**Implementation.** `--prior binbox-theta:θ:SE[:TOP]` sets each bin's depth to n_j / (θ ln 10) decades below the same top as spike-and-slab's (θ = 1: `binbox-theta:1:0`). The C kernel and the NumPy fallback accept a lower edge per rupture. An exact test (`selftest_zlo`, part of `--selftest`) checks two rates with different lower edges against a grid integral, with soft and hard edges, on both backends (all |z| ≤ 2.3). Swaps are restricted to same-bin pairs. Transfer moves are not available.

- **The variants.** The square-root slab (`--slab sqrt`): an on rupture's rate is uniform in √rate below the same top, density m^−1/2 instead of the log-uniform's 1/m, so its pull of each rate towards 0 is half as strong, while small rates cost more and fewer ruptures are on. The grouped fractions: one fraction on per magnitude bin and main tectonic domain (the rupture set's `Domain` of most of the rupture's subsections: 64 groups at 2e4, 254 at 211,000 ruptures) or main slip-rate section (468 and 2,991 groups), each under Beta(1, 1); or pooled, each group's fraction drawn from Beta(μ_j κ_j, (1 − μ_j) κ_j) around its bin's μ_j ~ Beta(1, 1), with the pooling strength κ_j log-uniform from 0.1 to 1e7, all estimated (exactly: μ_j and κ_j by slice sampling with the groups' fractions integrated out, then the fractions from their Beta conditionals; exact tests against enumeration passed).
- **None changes the fit.** The pooled models chose κ_j of about 10² (medians over the bins 10^2.1–10^2.7), close to one shared fraction per bin: the data themselves pick per-bin sharing. Independent fractions per bin and domain switch more ruptures on and raise the bins (+5% at 211,000 ruptures), with the slip rates a little closer and the paleo rates no closer; per bin and section, the bins rise 30% and the paleo rates are still about 0.9 uncertainties short.
- **Why: the bins count events, not moment.** Each bin spans 0.1 in magnitude, a factor of 1.41 in moment. The best fit puts each bin's rate on its larger ruptures (rate-weighted magnitude 0.020–0.021 above the bin's average over all its ruptures, at 2e4 and at 211,000 ruptures), while every posterior, whatever the prior variant, sits at the average (0.000–0.003 above). The data's pull towards the larger ruptures is spread thinly over thousands of interchangeable choices, against far more combinations near the bin's average. So at the same event rates the posterior delivers 8% less moment than the best fit at 2e4, and at 211,000 ruptures, where the best fit also raises its bins by 8%, 15% less. The slip and paleo rates come up short accordingly.
- **The best fit overfits.** Its χ² is 31 at 2e4 and 90 at 211,000 ruptures, against fit checks of 126 ± 16 and 590 ± 34: it fits the data far more closely than a model matching the truth would. The posterior's draws pass the fit check (140 and 545). By block, the slip rates are fitted within their uncertainties (0.76 per measurement at 211,000 ruptures), and the paleo rows are the block in tension, at 2.2 per measurement at 211,000 ruptures (1.3 at 2e4) under every variant. Closing that gap would take a different balance between the data (the paleo rows' uncertainties against the bins'), a modelling decision on the inputs rather than the prior.
- **At 211,000 ruptures the lower end matters.** The square-root slab fits as the default does but spreads the events differently. Its subsections' event rates (the summed rates of the ruptures through each) are a median 0.62 of the default's very conservative values (5–95%: 0.08–1.08; 0.26–1.10 for the three-quarters of subsections with the most events). A quick run from the best fit and a longer one from zero (8 chains × 12,000 sweeps) agree on this. Quick runs of the default scatter around its very conservative values with a median of 1.00 (5–95%: 0.57–1.39), and its very conservative replicates agree within ±7% (5–95%). At 2e4 the two slabs agree within about ±7% (5–95%) for the three-quarters of subsections with the most events. So at 211,000 ruptures the data leave much of how the events spread over the subsections to the prior. θ = 1's conservative run there puts them at 2.3 times the default's (5–95%: 0.87–6.0), but θ cannot mix at that size.
- **The square-root slab as the cross-check** (October 2026; dropped at the end of the month). It mixes as the log-uniform slab does (in the longer run, R̂ 1.011 for χ², 1.001 for the ruptures on and 1.013 for the slowest bin's fraction on) and costs about 10% more per sweep (24 ms against 21–22 ms for 8 chains at 211,000 ruptures). An off rupture's slab value is drawn down from the top, m = e^z_top (1 − u f)², with no logarithm. The quick smoke test at 2e4 gave χ² 138 ± 12, bins +1.4%, about 62 ruptures on per draw (55 above 1e-5) and 30 carrying 90% of the rate. An exact test (`selftest_spikeslab` with the tilt, part of the full `--selftest`) checks it against enumeration and grid integrals on both backends.
- **θ retired.** θ = 1 fitted no better (at 211,000 ruptures 0.81 per measurement for slip and 2.28 for paleo), shares the log-uniform's pull, and cannot cross its boxes from about 2e5 ruptures. It cannot take the square-root tilt either: its boxes hold every rupture, so the tilt would push all of a bin's rates up together, the flat prior's runaway in a milder form. The square-root slab took over its role as the cross-check; θ is kept for comparison only.
- **Not kept.** The grouped and pooled fractions on changed no fit, so they were not added to the code set.

## Floors and cutoff

One setting, `FLOOR` (1e-10), marks the rate below which nothing matters. It is spike-and-slab's slab floor, the lower end of its on range (`spikeslab:{FLOOR}:soft` in the notebook; −10 is also `gibbs.py`'s default), and the cutoff below which rates count as equal in the convergence checks, the figures and the preliminaries' collective-scale check (the notebook passes `--diag-floor={FLOOR}` to `gibbs.py`, `preliminaries.py`, `summarize_runs.py` and `plot_rate_magnitude.py`, whose defaults are −10). As a cutoff it never changes the sampling; under θ, whose depths come from θ, it is only the cutoff. The per-bin boxes, if run, take it as their floor (−12 reproduces their earlier default).

Rates below 1e-10 are negligible:
- **Per rupture:** under a Poisson model, a rupture at 1e-10 per year has a 95% chance of never occurring in 500 million years, about the age of New Zealand's oldest surface rocks (the chance of at least one occurrence is 1 − e^(−0.05) = 4.9%).
- **Age of the Earth:** at 1e-10 per year the mean recurrence interval, 10 billion years, is more than twice the age of the Earth.
- **Collectively:** with every rupture at the same rate, no prediction moves by more than 0.1σ below 1.8e-9 for the corrected 2e4 inputs with one measurement per slip-rate section (6.9e-10 counting each subsection), so all 18,243 ruptures together at 1e-10 would move no prediction by more than about 0.006σ (0.015σ counting each subsection). Under spike-and-slab only a few hundred ruptures are on at a time, so the margin is wider still. The scale falls as more ruptures share the busiest subsection (here original row 156, crossed by 7,234 ruptures), so `preliminaries.py` reports it for each problem and says whether `FLOOR` lies below it; check it on the 5e5 inputs, where it may approach 1e-10.

**History.** The slab floor and the cutoff were 1e-10 when they were separate settings (`SLAB_FLOOR` and `DIAG_FLOOR`, early October 2026). They were lowered to 1e-12 to share one setting with the per-bin boxes' floor, which needed 1e-12 to pass the fit check of the time, and returned to 1e-10 when spike-and-slab became the default, since neither spike-and-slab nor θ needs the lower value.

**The slab floor does not change the products.** With one measurement per section (quick preset, two replicates per floor), moving the slab floor from 1e-12 to 1e-10 changed every product by no more than Monte Carlo noise: subsection totals by a median 0.1% (5–95%: −9% to +11%; two replicates at the same floor differ by −9% to +20%), exceedance probabilities at 1e-5 by a median 0.02 in log₁₀ (5–95%: −0.20 to +0.13, against −0.29 to +0.18 between replicates), mean rates by a median 0.02 decades, and bin totals by at most 0.6%. Only the number of ruptures switched on at rates the data cannot see changed, from about 497 to 379 per draw, and the fractions on mixed slightly better (worst R̂ 1.03 against 1.04). Counting each subsection, raising the slab floor further, to 1e-8, 1e-7, 1e-6 or 1e-5, likewise left χ², the total rate, the bins, subsection totals, exceedance at 1e-5 and 1e-4 and the mean rates unchanged within the spread between seeds; only the number of ruptures on fell, by about 53 per decade, and a few near-empty subsections' medians became exactly 0. A slab floor of 1e-11 instead of 1e-10 (two seeds each, 8 chains × 10,000 sweeps) cost nothing per sweep and switched on about 50 more ruptures per draw at undetectable rates.

**The cutoff changes only the diagnostics.** θ's draws are identical whatever `FLOOR` is; only their scoring changes. With the cutoff at 1e-10, θ's quick runs flag 195–243 ruptures (1.1–1.3%) as slow-mixing, against 259–288 at 1e-12: θ's ruptures spend about 0.8% of their draws between 1e-12 and 1e-10 (counting each subsection), visits too rare to mix well in short runs and of no consequence, and the slow minority carries 23–24% of the total rate either way. Spike-and-slab's diagnostics barely depend on the cutoff. Compare slow-mixing counts only between runs scored at the same cutoff.

## Cleaning

The penalty entries in `cru_A.txt` are exactly 1e20 (not 1e21). `preliminaries.py` follows the two-step recipe and prints the count after each step. The description below is of the earlier inputs; the corrected inputs are summarized under "Note on the inputs" and in `prelim_summary.txt`.

Step 1 removes the 13 rows containing 1e20 entries (original rows 680, 682–686 and 688–694, all with d = 1 and no other entries) from G and d, together with the 292 parameters they apply to. That gives 741 × 18,356.

Step 2 removes all-zero rows and columns. There are no all-zero columns, but 294 rows are now all-zero. Rows 681 and 687 were empty in the raw matrix (d = 1). Rows 388–679 each held a single entry of 1e4 on one of the 292 removed parameters, with d = 0: a second, softer zero-forcing penalty, one row per removed parameter. Removing them leaves 447 × 18,356, with 1,074,239 nonzeros, all positive (minimum 41.6). The 447 remaining rows are the effective number of constraints.

**Soft zero-forcing rows** (step 1b, October 2026). The 1e4 rows with d = 0 are zero-forcing penalties too, softer ones (the weight is probably a legacy of how the inputs are built). In the 2e4 inputs every one of them is on a parameter a 1e20 row already removes, so step 2 removes them as all-zero rows. In the 211,000-rupture inputs, 301 of them (original rows 3649–7140) are on ruptures that no 1e20 row covers. As given they would cap those ruptures softly (a rate of 1e-4 costs 1 in χ²), and the fit check would count them as 301 measurements although they hold no data. Cleaning therefore treats every row after the slip-rate rows that has d = 0 and a single entry left as zero-forcing: its rupture is removed, with a rate of 0 by construction like the penalty rows' ruptures, and the row with it. `--keep-soft-penalties` keeps them as the inputs give them, and the fit check then leaves them out. At 211k the cleaned problem is 2,395 × 204,538 and the fit check 590 ± 34 (891 ± 42 with the 301 rows counted); the 2e4 results are unchanged.

The removed rows contributed only a constant χ² of 2, so keeping them would not change the posterior. This was checked directly with a 741-row version (`--keep-empty-rows`). With the same seed, the Gibbs sampler gave bit-identical draws, with every χ² exactly 2 higher. If the empty rows are ever kept, χ² comparisons must include the constant; the fit check recorded at cleaning includes it.

The rows fall into three blocks. Original rows 0–387 (388 rows, varying coefficients, d from 0.6 to 31.6) look like slip-rate rows. Rows 695–713 (19 rows, one constant coefficient per row, d from 6.5 to 10.2) look like magnitude–frequency bins; every rupture appears in exactly one of them. Rows 714–753 (40 rows, varying coefficients, d from 1.5 to 10.7) look like paleo-type rows. These labels are my reading of the structure; `--blocks` names them. Rename as appropriate.

## Summarizing the draws

**χ².** Every χ² in this report is the mean over posterior draws, not the χ² of the posterior-mean model m̄. Because the predictions are linear in the rates, the two differ by exactly the total posterior variance of the predictions: E[χ²(m)] = χ²(m̄) + Σ_j Var(y_j).

| run (corrected inputs, one measurement per section, quick preset) | mean χ² over draws | χ² of the posterior-mean model | total predictive variance |
|---|---|---|---|
| spike-and-slab | 140.2 ± 11.7 | 86.1 | 54.1 |
| θ = 1 | 141.8 ± 11.8 | 88.4 | 53.4 |

With the MFD bins counted as two measurements the three were 137.0 ± 12.1, 80.4 and 56.7 under spike-and-slab, and 137.2 ± 11.9, 84.2 and 53.0 under θ. The mean over draws is the fit check. The posterior-mean model's χ² is the number to compare with single models such as NNLS (χ²_min = 31.25; 27.19 with the bins counted as two measurements) or simulated annealing; under spike-and-slab and θ, though, the posterior-mean model is a summary rather than a plausible sample, far less sparse than any draw. About half the excess of a typical draw over the best fit is predictive spread and half systematic.

**Means.** For anything that adds rates (totals, subsection and bin sums, hazard), use means in linear space, or better, compute the quantity for each draw and then summarize. Linear means add up exactly and are robust to the prior's lower end, because each rate's linear mean is dominated by its data-informed upper tail. Geometric means (means in log space) follow the lower end: under the single box they moved 1.85 decades for 4 decades of floor while linear means moved 0.06, and summed over ruptures they gave a total 25 times too small. Under spike-and-slab most draws of a rupture are exactly 0, so geometric means are not even defined. For a single rupture's distribution, use quantiles, which are the same in either space; for weak ruptures they are prior-dependent.

The lowest decades depend on run length, since a rupture switched on in few draws gets its mean from those visits alone: as given, quick runs leave 32 ruptures below 1e-10 and 884 below 1e-8 (1,112 below 1e-8 with `'two-measurements'`; TESTING.md), the conservative run 9 below 1e-8, and the very conservative runs none. **How much of each total the data see individually.** For each subsection and bin, `undetectable_share` (in the notebook's tables) is the share of its posterior-mean total carried by rates below their own rupture's detection scale σ/‖G_i‖, the rate at which one rupture alone, others fixed, would move χ² by 1. Rates between the collective-negligibility scale (about 2e-9) and that scale are individually invisible but collectively not: a crowd of them can make up a total, and how many there are is largely the prior's choice. From the quick runs:

|---|---|---|

With the MFD bins counted as two measurements the detection scales rise and so do the shares: 17% under spike-and-slab and 22% under θ for the bins (subsections 17% and 24%), and 18% for the best fit. Under the earlier box priors about four-fifths of a typical total or more rested on rates the data cannot see one by one (80% counting each subsection, 91% with one measurement per section). A total with a large share is prior-dependent, however tight its interval looks.

## Samplers and convergence

**Gibbs in log rate** (`gibbs.py`, C kernel). Each rate is drawn exactly from its conditional by a one-dimensional slice sampler in log m, so hard and soft edges are handled exactly; under spike-and-slab an exact Gibbs step also switches each rupture on or off. One sweep costs one pass over the nonzeros of G. Each thread moves its chains through the ruptures together, reading each column once for up to four of them, and the slice sampler takes the stepping-out steps that a bound shows would pass without evaluating them; every chain's arithmetic is unchanged (bit-identical output), and at 211,000 ruptures the kernel became 1.7–2 times faster under spike-and-slab and 2.2 times under θ (October 2026). The likelihood is flat across the "off" plateau, so one update can carry a rate in or out of it, and ruptures switch freely between clearly on and clearly off; under the single box each did so about 0.30 times per recorded draw, giving about 980 effective draws per 1,000 for nearly every rupture.

**Swap moves** (`--swap-cos 0.99`, always on in the notebook since October 2026) exchange the rates of near-duplicate pairs in the same bin (338,177 pairs with cosine above 0.99 on the corrected inputs with one measurement per section; 1,165,613 at 211,000 ruptures), one pass per recorded draw; they are exact, and let near-duplicates exchange the active role. Under spike-and-slab a pair does work only when one of its ruptures is on. A controlled test (October 2026; quick preset, inputs as given, balance term on, chains from zero, two replicates with and two without, same seeds) measured their effect. At 211,000 ruptures under spike-and-slab they cost no measurable time (44 s per run either way, about 3,500 accepted swaps per pass per chain) and gave the 43 ruptures on in at least 5% of draws a median 396 effective draws of 1,000 against 135 (2.3 times at 2e4, 373 against 150); the slow-mixing minority fell from 500–574 ruptures (32–33% of the rate) to 200–204 (19–20%), and the replicate-to-replicate spread of those ruptures' probabilities of a rate above 1e-5 from 0.060 to 0.017. Under θ every pair swaps on every pass (acceptance 1.000), which at 211,000 ruptures cost 59% more time per sweep (78 against 49 ms) for the same effective draws per second, though fewer slow-mixing ruptures (1,912–1,924 against 5,817–5,912). Neither prior's fit, total rate, bins or subsection totals moved beyond Monte Carlo noise, and the swap moves do not reduce the subsection totals' noise (7.4% and 7.6% between replicates at 211,000 ruptures), which comes from slower directions. **Transfer moves**, which shift rate between a rupture and up to four sub-ruptures reproducing its column, exist for the single box only (`build_transfers.py`, set aside in `cru_retired_scripts.zip`); they were exact and cheap but gave no measurable per-rupture gain at a 1e-12 floor.

**Convergence.** Even for perfectly independent draws the worst R̂ over 18,000 rates is above 1 (about 1.015 for 4 chains × 250 draws), so R̂ must be judged against that noise baseline, not against 1. Under spike-and-slab and θ a minority of ruptures switch on and off slowly and stay above it: in quick runs 0.3% of ruptures under spike-and-slab (carrying 16–18% of the total rate) and 1.1–1.3% under θ (23–24%), and in the very conservative θ run 4.5–4.7%, with the worst R̂ 1.35–1.38. Longer runs help them only slowly; moves that exchange the active role between alternative ruptures would. χ² and the total rate mix much better (R̂ near 1.01 at the quick preset), and spike-and-slab's number of ruptures on and fractions on are checked too.

**HMC cross-check** (`rhmc.py --param log`, set aside in `cru_retired_scripts.zip`). Under the single box at a floor of 1e-8 (plain likelihood), log-space HMC and Gibbs agreed rupture by rupture on mean log rate: standardized differences had mean +0.009 and sd 1.004, with 0.23% beyond 3 (0.27% expected), and the median absolute difference was 0.034 decades. HMC needed its mass from the Gibbs variances of log rate and its starting points from Gibbs end states; on one core, Gibbs delivered about 22 effective draws per rupture per second against 7–13 for HMC.

**Self-tests** (`gibbs.py --selftest`) compare against exact answers. They cover:
- a truncated normal 30 sd beyond its bound;
- two- and three-rate problems with walls under flat, exponential and log-uniform priors, with soft and hard edges;
- swap and transfer moves;
- per-rupture lower and upper box edges (θ's depths and the soft top);
- the spike-and-slab prior, by enumerating on/off states;
- the retired hierarchical prior.

All pass on both the C and NumPy backends, and multi-threaded Gibbs is bit-identical to single-threaded Gibbs. The notebook runs `--selftest current` (about 30 s): only what its default uses, the per-rupture lower and upper edges, spike-and-slab, the balance term, and swap moves under a log-box prior; the full suite (about 4 minutes) is for after any change to the sampler.

## Earlier priors

The workflow's earlier priors were log-uniform boxes ending at a common floor. They still run in `gibbs.py`, for comparison, but are not part of the workflow's tests. Their analyses, on the earlier inputs and counting each slip-rate subsection as its own measurement, established the robust products and showed why the floor could not be chosen from the data.

**Per-bin boxes** (`--prior binbox-floor:-12`, the default until October 2026). Each bin's ruptures had a log-uniform box from the same soft top at R_j down to a hard floor at 1e-12 common to all bins (`binbox:W` ends each box W decades below its R_j instead). They mixed well (about 980 effective draws per 1,000 for nearly every rupture) and fitted better than the single box before them (χ² 557 against 599 at 10 decades deep), but their depth, 8.5–9.3 decades below the targets, made θ_j ≫ 1, so the prior pushed the bin totals up. On the corrected inputs, counting each subsection, they gave χ² 663 ± 29 (2.6 sd above the fit check then, 446 ± 83), bins a median 23% above their targets (−86% to +85%), about 1,170 ruptures carrying 90% of the rate, and 80% of a typical bin's total carried by individually undetectable rates. Between floors of 1e-12 and 1e-10 their products moved little (subsection totals by a median 6%, exceedance at 1e-5 by 0.05 in log₁₀), but the floor or depth set the sparsity, and the data could not choose it. With one measurement per section they failed the fit check by 26 sd (547 ± 32 against 126 ± 16), after which the default changed to spike-and-slab.

**Tried again at 211,000 ruptures (late October 2026).** The per-bin boxes were briefly a notebook option again (`binbox-floor:{FLOOR}:0:soft`, from `FLOOR` up to the soft top), then taken out for simplicity. Conservative runs (8 chains × 5,000 sweeps, thin 8; `FLOOR = -12`, each subsection its own measurement, no balance term) on the 211,000-rupture inputs and on the SRunc1 inputs (slip-rate uncertainties halved) mixed well, both passing the convergence checks with their replicates agreeing, but fitted far worse than spike-and-slab with the same settings. χ² was 10,095 ± 144 and 10,153 ± 137 against the fit check's 2,395 ± 185 (spike-and-slab: 1,110 and 1,810). The bins sat a median 2.28 and 1.89 times their targets (0.03–3.32 and 0.02–2.46; spike-and-slab 1.05 and 1.19), rising with each bin's number of ruptures, and the total rates were 0.0696 and 0.0657 (targets 0.0372; spike-and-slab 0.0378 and 0.0417). With every rupture always on, 88–99% of each bin's and subsection's total sat in rates individually invisible to the data (the implied prior bin totals were 11 to 1,330 times the targets). That diffuse rate inflated the bins and put 113 and 69 slip-rate sections above twice their targets (3 under spike-and-slab), mostly sections with small targets relative to their uncertainties. Those weakly constrained sections follow the prior either way: spike-and-slab's sparsity leaves them near zero, and the boxes' pressure lifts them above their targets. A sweep cost about 7 times spike-and-slab's (0.17 s for 8 chains on 4 threads).

Prior draws imply a total rate of about 200/yr for a box from 1e-12 to 1e-2, against 0.0226/yr from the bin targets, and with the top fixed a lower floor is the box's only way to make the data more probable a priori. Placing the box on the average data-imposed rate (`logbox-centre:W:SE`, `logbox-mean:W:SE`) did not fix this: centring raises the top with the centre.
- **Edges:** hard edges instead of soft ones raised individual rates by about 0.55 decades and fitted worse (χ² 1,517 against 986 at a 1e-8 floor). A soft lower edge of sd 1 decade acts as about one extra decade of depth.

**The misfit cap** (removed in October 2026). This optional model class kept only models with χ² ≤ X (by default the fit check's expected value; 447 when these runs were made), sampled by coordinate hit-and-run on that set, so draws sat on its boundary and X was in effect the misfit chosen. It was a separate model class, not a filter: under the plain likelihood no draw of the single box at 1e-12 satisfied χ² ≤ 447 (599 ± 28). With the single box it removed the over-prediction while moving individual rates by only about 0.05 decades (total rate 0.0260 against 0.0268) and made sums less sensitive to the floor; tighter caps (X = 200 and 100) pulled the bins toward their targets but did not converge in 2,400 sweeps. The pressure-free priors pass the fit check under the plain likelihood, so the cap was no longer needed, and the Gaussian likelihood is now the only one.

**Hierarchical lognormal prior** (`--prior lognormal`, retired). log₁₀ m_i ~ Normal(μ_bin, s²), with one centre per magnitude bin and one shared within-bin spread under broad hyperpriors (s uniform on 0.05–5 decades, each μ_bin normal with mean −6 and sd 10 decades), sampled exactly, with centred and non-centred hyperparameter updates (ASIS), and passing its self-tests. It was not double-dipping: the bins come from the forward model, and the centres and spread were estimated jointly with the rates. A pilot (χ² 257, total 0.0231, spread about 1.55 decades) and a long run (4 chains × 36,000 sweeps: χ² 239, total 0.0230, bins 1.02× their targets, about 40 ruptures above 1e-4) found roughly the pressure-free answer, but the hyperparameters never converged: the spread's R̂ was 1.86, and it was still rising (from 2.10 to 2.33 decades between the halves of the kept draws) while the centres fell. Widening the spread while lowering the centres keeps each bin's total fixed, so this was a slide along a ridge, driven by the same pull toward sparsity; partial non-centring (`--pnc-pilot`) and a fixed spread (`--s-fixed`) did not help. Spike-and-slab replaced it as the way to let the data set the sparsity.

## Why not a linearly uniform prior

A prior uniform in each rate on [0, 1] encodes the expectation that rates are spread linearly, and in 18,356 dimensions it is far from neutral. Positivity turns the directions the data cannot see into a cone whose volume grows as roughly t^18,355 when all predictions scale by t. That volume overwhelms a likelihood with σ = 1.

| flat prior on [0, 1] | plain likelihood | uniform on χ² ≤ 447 |
|---|---|---|
| χ² | 10,274 ± 160 (log-HMC: 10,262 ± 156) | 446.3–447.0 (on the boundary) |
| predictions projected on the data | 1.709 ± 0.008 | 1.024 ± 0.002 |
| total rate | 0.0518 | 0.0267 |
| per-rupture marginals | exponential (median sd/mean 0.999) | exponential (median sd/mean 1.004) |

Gibbs reaches the flat-prior state within its first four sweeps from the best fit, and log-HMC reaches it independently. Per row, flat-prior predictions range from about 0.1 to 5.7 times the observed values. Its rate population is compressed into roughly 1e-8 to 1e-4, peaking near 1e-6, rather than spreading over many decades. Its subsection totals are 2.1× those of the single log-box at 1e-12 (median; 5–95%: 0.7–3.1×), and its rupture medians sit about 1.3 decades above those of the 1e-8 log-box.

**With each rate's top at its bin's target (October 2026).** `--prior linear` (a `gibbs.py` option, for the command line) makes each rupture's rate uniform from 0 to its bin's target rate R_j, drawn by the same exact truncated-normal updates, with no spike and no floor. The top caps each rupture, not the bin: a priori each bin's total is n_j R_j / 2, 82 to 1,854 times its target at 2e4 and 86 to 11,344 times at 211,000 ruptures, and the cone still overwhelms the data (quick preset, inputs as given, one measurement per slip-rate section):

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

The mean model overpredicts the slip rates (by +0.03, +1.4, +4.2 and +13 uncertainties for targets below 1, 1–2, 2–4 and above 4 uncertainties at 2e4, and +4.7 to +33 at 211,000 ruptures) and the paleo rates (+2.8 to +8.1, and +1.3 to +26), the mirror image of the log-rate priors' shortfall ("Fits and the prior's lower end"). Its chains settle within 8 sweeps of the best fit, with R̂ 1.00 and draws 8 sweeps apart nearly independent, but each sweep costs about three times spike-and-slab's, since every rate moves every sweep: 67 ms for 8 chains and 112 ms for 16 at 211,000 ruptures, recording every 8 sweeps. An exact test (`selftest_linear`, part of `--selftest current`) checks two rates with different tops against a grid integral on both backends. The top never binds: over 400 draws the largest rate reached 12.5% of its R_j at 2e4 and 3.4% at 211,000 ruptures (no rate passed half its top), while the bin totals came to 1.9–5.7 and 4.8–15 times their targets. So a soft top would change nothing: the inflation comes from the number of ruptures each bin adds up, which a cap on single ruptures cannot touch.

**Halfway between linear and log-uniform (October 2026).** `--prior halfway` (a `gibbs.py` option, for the command line) draws each rupture's rate from a 50/50 mix of a uniform range and a log-uniform range, both from 10^`FLOOR` up to its bin's target rate R_j with the soft top (`halfway:A:TOP:W` sets the floor, the top and the uniform part's share W), so each value has a 50% prior chance of coming from either; every rupture is always on. (A 50/50 bet on the whole population instead, every rate linear or every rate log-uniform, would be settled by the data: the linear prior's fit is worse by thousands of χ² units, so its posterior weight would be nil.) Its two ends are the linear prior above and the per-bin log-uniform boxes down to the same floor (`binbox-floor:-10`). It is sampled exactly in log rate, where the mix has density mA e^(z − z_top) + mB, each part normalized over the range and the top's shoulder. Halfway in prior probability is not halfway in the result: the halfway prior lands almost on its log-uniform end. Most rates sit far below R_j, where the log-uniform part's density (proportional to 1/m) exceeds the uniform part's (flat) by orders of magnitude, so the data assign almost every value to it. In the posterior, 1.5% of the values come from the uniform part at 2e4, carrying 28% of the total rate, and 0.4% at 211,000 ruptures, carrying 7%: the few largest rates. Both ends push the bin totals up, because every rupture is always on with a range reaching R_j: the log-uniform boxes by θ_j − 1 with θ_j = n_j / (W_j ln 10), which grows with each bin's rupture count ("θ = 1: pressure-free per-bin boxes"), hence far more at 211,000 ruptures. Spike-and-slab avoids that by estimating each bin's fraction on. The halfway prior mixes as fast as the linear prior (R̂ 1.00, and at the quick preset the 5th percentile of the rupture rates' effective draws is about 0.8 per draw), but a sweep costs about six times spike-and-slab's: 140 ms for 8 chains and 262 ms for 16 at 211,000 ruptures, recording every 8 sweeps. An exact test (`selftest_halfway`, part of `--selftest current`) checks two rates with different floors and soft tops against grid integrals, with the two parts normalized on the grid, independently of the kernel's closed forms; its answers move by several times its Monte Carlo error when the share changes from 0.5 to 0.3 or 0.7.

The flat-prior runs also served as the first sampler validation. Gibbs and log-space HMC agreed on all 18,356 posterior means: standardized differences had mean −0.020 and sd 1.000, with 0.23% beyond 3. Gibbs delivered about 100 effective draws per rupture per second against 10–15 for HMC. Reflective HMC in linear space failed outright. Each rate lies within a few percent of its conditional sd from the wall at zero, so trajectories made about 28,000 reflections per chain per 0.1 time units. Acceptance was zero at step sizes well below the stability limit, and reached 0.50 only at h = 3e-4. This is why every sampler here works in log rate.

## Randomized best fits (October 2026)

`randomized_nnls.py` (in the notebook, `RANDOMIZED_NNLS` set to a number of fits) runs, in place of the sampler, best fits to noise-perturbed data: each the best nonnegative fit (NNLS, as cleaning computes it) to the data plus a fresh draw of their stated noise on the cleaned rows, so with one measurement per slip-rate section and the MFD bins at `MFD_UNCERTAINTY`'s level, as the sampler sees them. Its figures draw each target against the fits' median prediction, with their 16–84% and 2.5–97.5% ranges and the unperturbed best fit: the slip-rate sections, the magnitude bins and the paleo rows (`fig_rnnls_slip_rates.png`, `fig_rnnls_mfd.png` and `fig_rnnls_paleo_rates.png` in `RUN_DIR/rnnls/`, each with its table). `rate_vectors.csv` there gives the fits' mean rate per rupture and the share of fits in which each rupture is nonzero, and with the model's rupture set the notebook writes the fits' mean rates as an OpenSHA solution (`rnnls/solution/`). The fits run in parallel processes, one thread each; fit k's noise comes from the k-th seed spawned from `--seed`, so the results do not depend on how many run at a time. A self-test (`randomized_nnls.py --selftest`) checks that for a full-rank problem whose rates all lie well above 0 the fits scatter as the posterior under a flat prior would (variances within 9% of σ²(GᵀG)⁻¹ over 400 fits, around the best fit), and that the fits do not depend on the number of processes.

**What it is.** The best fit's sampling distribution: how far the best fit moves when the noise in the data changes. For a linear model with Gaussian errors and no active constraint that is also the posterior under a flat prior; here it is not:
- **It never counts the models that fit equally well.** The data constrain at most as many combinations of rates as there are rows, and every fit has at most one nonzero rate per row. The noise does reshuffle which ruptures carry the rate (at 2e4, 40 fits used 1,579 different ruptures, with a median 21% of each fit's nonzero rates on ruptures the unperturbed best fit uses), but the solver picks each fit's ruptures, with no weighting among the alternatives.
- **It has no prior,** so where the data are silent the solver decides.
- **It misses where the posterior's probability lies:** among the far more numerous models that fit slightly less well ("Fits and the prior's lower end"). Each fit is an optimum, with the best fit's habits, each bin's larger ruptures among them.
- **Positivity biases it upward.** Noise that pushes a near-zero prediction down cannot be followed, since no rate goes below 0, while noise that pushes it up is; so the fits' totals sit above the best fit's, the more so the more rows have targets near 0 (at 211,000 ruptures, 331 of the 507 slip-rate sections' targets are under one uncertainty).

## Balance between over- and underprediction (October 2026; `DOWN_THE_MIDDLE`, on by default)

The question: penalize fits in which a data type's targets are mostly underpredicted (or mostly overpredicted), for the slip-rate sections, the MFD bins and the paleo rows separately. Counting over- against underpredictions is the sign test, a standard check of residuals that assumes independent errors; it is not known as a term in an inversion's objective. Putting it there makes a loss-based ("generalized") posterior, whose weight must be calibrated against the noise. A count also changes in steps, so NNLS cannot use it. And with the weight proposed for it, the zero model's misfit for the data type spread over its measurements, each unit of imbalance would cost 20, 76 and 18 in χ² for the slip sections, bins and paleo rows at 2e4 (3.5, 65 and 14 at 211,000 ruptures), so even a model matching the truth would pay over 100 from chance imbalance at 2e4.

What was tested instead is the smooth analogue: each data type's summed residual, in units of each measurement's uncertainty, penalized as w (Σ residuals)², which targets mean rather than median balance. It is one extra row of G and d per data type, so it works unchanged in the sampler and in NNLS, and the randomized best fits rebuild its targets from each fit's own perturbed data. Two weights: the proposed rule (the zero model's summed residual costs the zero model's misfit for the type: w = Σ d² / (Σ targets)²), which came to 3.4, 1.0 and 1.3 times (3.2, 1.0 and 1.3 at 211,000 ruptures) the calibrated weight, which counts each type's total as one more measurement (w = 1 / N_type). - **The weights.** The natural unit is one extra measurement per data type: under the stated noise a type's summed residual (or its count of over- minus underpredictions) is about ±√N by chance, so (Σ residuals)² / N (or (over − under)² / N) is a standardized statistic squared, weighing as much as one datum in χ²; that is the calibrated weight, and a model matching the truth pays about 1 per type. On that scale, though, a large type all one-sided costs far more than a small one. The equal-badness weight makes it equally bad for any type to be all underpredicted: it penalizes each type's relative shortfall, Σ residuals / Σ targets (−1 for the zero model in every type), with one common weight, the largest that keeps every type's term at or below one extra measurement, min over types of (Σ targets)² / N. The slip-rate sections set it at both sizes, so their term keeps the calibrated weight while the bins' gets 0.30 of it at 2e4 and 0.49 at 211,000 ruptures, and the paleo rows' 0.74 and 0.80. For counts the same rule gives a common weight of N for the smallest type, 18 for the bins, on each type's (over − under)² / N². Either way the term pulls only as far as the imbalance exceeds chance: at the posterior the slip sections' summed residual is about 10 of its chance spread at 211,000 ruptures, so even one measurement's weight pulls there.
- **The imbalance is the data's, not only the posterior's.** The best fit itself underpredicts 50 of the 68 slip-rate sections and 34 of the 40 paleo rows at 2e4 while overpredicting all 18 bins, and 428 of 507 sections and 56 of 65 paleo rows at 211,000 ruptures: sign-test z of 4 to 15, far beyond chance. The bins want less rate than the slip and paleo rows. Under spike-and-slab the paleo shortfall is the largest (z −6.6, a mean residual of −1.0 at 211,000 ruptures).
- **In the posterior the balance rows halve the mean shortfall.** With the proposed weight, slip from −0.47 to −0.24 and paleo from −0.63 to −0.33 at 2e4 (−0.58 to −0.29 and −1.00 to −0.48 at 211,000 ruptures), and the paleo rows' fit improves (χ² per measurement 1.29 to 1.14; 2.22 to 1.54). At 2e4 the total rate does not move; at 211,000 ruptures it rises 3%, with the bins 6% above their targets (χ² per bin 1.78 against 1.00). So the posterior meets the balance mostly by choosing better-placed or larger ruptures within each bin, the freedom "Fits and the prior's lower end" describes, and at 211,000 ruptures partly by raising the bins. The calibrated weight does about half as much, and the equal-badness weight about the same as the calibrated one (the slip sections, which set it, keep the calibrated weight; the bins and paleo rows get a little less).
- **Mean balance is not sign balance.** At 211,000 ruptures, 331 of the 507 sections have targets under one uncertainty; their predictions sit slightly below them, so the sections' signs stay unbalanced (−279) even as their mean residual halves. Balancing the signs would mean raising hundreds of low-slip sections just above their targets.
- **For the randomized best fits** the slip rates are already balanced in the mean; the balance rows mostly bring the bins down (from 18% to 12% over at 211,000 ruptures) and the paleo rows up.
- **As implemented** (`DOWN_THE_MIDDLE = True`, the notebook's default; `gibbs.py --down-the-middle`): the equal-badness weight. `cru_common.balance_rows` builds one row per data type, √A / T_t times the type's sum of rows (T_t its sum of targets), with target √A, so the row's residual is √A s_t; `gibbs.py` appends them to G and d, so the kernel is unchanged and every prior takes them, and the swap moves' column inner products include them (checked against the extended matrix to 4e-15 at 211,000 ruptures). The recorded χ² and predictions stay over the data rows, so the fit check, the row-block fit and the figures measure the fit to the data alone; the balance term (`balance`) and each type's relative shortfall (`balance_s`) are recorded on their own, and each run's log gives A, each type's share of a measurement and its mean shortfall. The best fit and the randomized best fits ignore it. An exact test (`selftest_balance`, part of `--selftest current`) checks the rows against a direct computation of the shortfalls, the zero model's cost A in every type, the weights, and the run's bookkeeping, under both weightings. Without the flag every prior's output is bit-identical to before. A quick 2e4 run with swap moves gave χ² 137 ± 11 over the data, a balance term of 15 on average, and shortfalls of −15% (slip-rate sections), +4% (bins) and −11% (paleo rows). `DOWN_THE_MIDDLE = 'zero-model'` (`--down-the-middle zero-model`) gives each type its own A instead, its zero-model misfit, so s = −1 or +1 costs a type what the zero model costs it in χ²: the proposed weight of the table above, 1.6–3.4 times the equal-badness weight (A = 1382, 1375 and 717 for the slip-rate sections, bins and paleo rows at 2e4, against 408.5; 1787, 1162 and 883 at 211,000 ruptures, against 552.6). Without the option the equal-badness output is bit-identical to before (the run file records A once per type).
- **It weights the data, so it is a modelling decision.** The balance rows count each data type's total a second time: once more at the calibrated weight, 1–3.4 times more at the proposed one. That arbitrates the tension between the bins and the slip and paleo rows by a choice of weights, and the posterior's uncertainty in those totals is then understated by the same factor. As a check rather than a penalty, the sign counts and mean residuals per data type are cheap to report for any run.

## A symmetric log-ratio term (October 2026; tested on the best fit only, not in the code)

The question: a third term in the misfit on each prediction's ratio to its target, λ Σ (ln (Gx)_i − ln d_i)², so that a prediction twice its target costs as much as one half of it (each slip-rate section's k copies weighted 1/k, so the section counts once). Unlike the balance rows it is not quadratic in the rates, and that costs tractability. The sampler's per-rupture conditionals would stop being Gaussian, so its exact draws would give way to Metropolis or slice steps. The best fit would need a nonlinear solver instead of NNLS. And the log of a zero prediction is infinite, so under spike-and-slab every row would need at least one rupture on. (The squared relative residual, (Gx/d − 1)², is quadratic, but it only changes each row's weight, from 1/σ² to 1/σ² + w/d². At 211,000 ruptures that weight would fall almost entirely on the slip-rate sections, whose targets are a median 0.80 of their uncertainties, against 2.85 for the paleo rows and 7.9 for the bins.)

It was therefore tried on the best fit first. L-BFGS-B over nonnegative rates, started from the NNLS fit, minimized the Gaussian misfit plus the log term, with and without the balance rows (equal-badness weight), on the 2e4 inputs (one measurement per slip-rate section, bins as given). λ was calibrated four ways:
- **One uncertainty:** a factor-2 miss costs as much as a one-uncertainty miss, λ = 1/(ln 2)² = 2.1.
- **Two uncertainties:** a factor-2 miss costs as much as a two-uncertainty miss, λ = 4/(ln 2)² = 8.3.
- **Matched per measurement:** a factor-2 miss either way costs what doubling costs in the Gaussian term, λ_i = (d_i/σ_i)²/(ln 2)². At 211,000 ruptures the medians are 1.3 for slip-rate sections, 17 for paleo rows and 130 for bins; in the scaled rows the weight is simply (d_i/ln 2)².
- **Equal totals:** doubling every prediction costs the same in both terms, λ = mean (d/σ)²/(ln 2)²: 57 at 2e4, 13.5 at 211,000 ruptures.

χ² is over the data rows, and slip-rate sections are counted by their mean prediction, as in the table above. "Near 0" means a section's mean prediction is below 1% of its target.

- **Its one clear effect.** At every λ the log term lifts the slip-rate sections that the best fit leaves near zero (3 of the 68), for χ² +6 to +9 at λ = 2.1 or matched weights.
- **It balances slowly, and by raising the total.** At λ = 8.3 the overpredicted sections go from 18 to 20 of 68 and the overpredicted paleo rows from 6 to 10 of 40. Meanwhile the total rate rises 7% and one bin reaches 1.30 times its target. At λ = 57 the counts are 22 and 12, with the total 20% higher, bins up to 1.84 times their targets, and χ² 154 against the fit check's 126 ± 16. The tension between the bins and the slip and paleo rows ("Balance between over- and underprediction") remains: meeting the ratio penalty for slip and paleo takes more rate than the bins allow.
- **The balance rows do more.** NNLS with the balance rows alone takes the overpredicted sections from 18 to 25 and the paleo rows from 6 to 16, for χ² +1.4, with the bins' median unchanged. Adding the log term on top takes balance away again (12 paleo rows over at λ = 8.3) while raising the bins.
- **Not tested:** the 211,000-rupture inputs and the posterior. On this evidence the balance term, already on by default, is the better lever. A sampler version of the log term, with its new updates and forced "on" ruptures, is not worth building yet. The fits came from scratch scripts, not part of the code set.

## What this means for the investigation

Use spike-and-slab as the production setting; at 211,000 ruptures the reference is the very conservative run of the configuration adopted in late October 2026 ("The reference run at 211,000 ruptures"). Report sums (subsection, bin and paleo-site totals, and functionals built from them) and per-rupture exceedance probabilities at about 1e-5 and above as data products, using linear means or per-draw functionals rather than geometric means. Report rupture medians and lower quantiles as prior-dependent; under spike-and-slab and θ most are 0. At 211,000 ruptures the subsections' event rates are partly the prior's too: the square-root slab, no longer run, put them at a median 0.62 of the default's with the same fit. Treat each run's χ² distribution as a fit check against the expected value cleaning prints: 126 ± 16 on the corrected inputs with one measurement per slip-rate section, 590 ± 34 at 211,000 ruptures. Whether another downstream functional aᵀm depends on the lower end of the rates can be checked by computing it at two slab floors ("Floors and cutoff").

Choices that remain modelling decisions:
- how the slip-rate rows count (one measurement per section, the default; each subsection its own measurement with `SLIP_SECTIONS = 'none'`; or sections from a mapping file);
- the MFD bins' uncertainty level: the inputs' design value (the default), counted as the two measurements the bins are projected from (three times it, the bin-to-bin shape kept), or a level set for the M7.0–7.1 bin (`MFD_UNCERTAINTY`); the data cannot set it; it moves the bins and the total rate by several percent, and looser levels let single draws leave whole bins empty ("MFD uncertainty");
- the balance term (`DOWN_THE_MIDDLE`, on by default) and its weight, which decide how the tension between the bins and the slip and paleo rows is shared;
- the prior: spike-and-slab with the log-uniform slab (whose shape, at 211,000 ruptures, moves the subsections' event rates), with its Beta(1, 1) prior on each bin's fraction on (sharing the fractions by tectonic domain or slip-rate section, pooled or not, changed no fit), its slab floor (1e-10; the products held for slab floors from 1e-12 to 1e-5) and the soft top at each bin's target;
- pressure-free priors versus priors that push the bin totals up (the earlier box priors and the optional halfway prior, and far more the optional linear prior), which give much denser solutions, with exceedance probabilities at 1e-5 about 7 times higher; only the pressure-free priors pass the fit check with one measurement per section.

Prior-free extremal bounds for a few key functionals (a linear program with per-row misfit bounds) would complement the posterior and remain an option.

It has a quick preset, a conservative production preset and a very conservative one, with separate run lengths for each prior.

**Row groups.** The likelihood treats all rows alike; the row groups matter for the priors, which work per magnitude bin (the magnitude-bin rows), and for the subsection summaries (the slip-rate rows). Both are detected automatically during cleaning, so `BLOCKS = None` (the default) needs nothing named. The slip-rate rows are the leading rows, up to the first zero-forcing penalty row (right-hand side 0, entries only on penalised ruptures), so a slip-rate row with zero slip does not end them. The magnitude-bin rows are the rows with one constant coefficient whose supports split the ruptures into disjoint groups. Any group named in `BLOCKS` (by original row numbers, which change with the input) is checked against the detection, and cleaning stops on a mismatch. The paleo rows only label the per-group fit summaries; unnamed, they get an automatic label. Within the slip-rate rows, cleaning then finds the sections (runs of copies of one slip rate) and prints them, with notes on targets shared between sections and on neighbours with identical targets that share no rupture, and the fit check; on new inputs, compare the sections with the fault model.

**Folders.** Code, data and outputs can live in separate folders. Outputs: `cru_clean.npz` is the cleaned problem (under the defaults, with each slip-rate section's rows divided by √k; it records the row scales, the sections, the slip-section setting and the fit check, and the MFD setting and multiplier, `mfd_uncertainty` and `mfd_scale`). `prelim_results.npz`, `prelim_summary.txt` and `prelim.png` are the diagnostics, and `nnls/nnls_vectors.npz` is the best fit as rate vectors (its `mean` column in the original rupture numbering, removed ruptures 0), which the notebook draws right after cleaning with the three fit scripts described below (`fig_slip_rates_nnls.png`, `fig_rate_magnitude_nnls.png` and `fig_paleo_rates_nnls.png`, also in `nnls/`, each with its table in `.txt`; the labels then speak of the model, not the mean model), and `nnls/nnls_vectors.csv` holds the same rates as a table with the rows of `rate_vectors.csv`, one per rupture of the inputs (columns `rupture`, `penalised` and `best_fit`, removed ruptures 0); for an `--out` other than `cru_clean.npz` these names get the `--out` stem as a prefix. Given the model's OpenSHA rupture set (the `ruptures/` folder of an OpenSHA solution for the same model: `RUPTURE_SET = 'auto'`, the default, finds the one OpenSHA solution folder in `DATA_DIR`, such as `NZSHM22_InversionSolution-211k`, and a name picks one), the notebook also writes the best fit as an OpenSHA fault system solution, `nnls/solution/` (`make_solution.py`): `rates.csv` (`Rupture Index,Annual Rate`, one row per rupture of the rupture set, numbers as OpenSHA writes them), `modules.json` and the rupture set's sub-seismogenic on-fault MFDs (`sub_seismo_on_fault_mfds.csv`, which the NZSHM22 solutions carry). With the rupture set's files they make a solution OpenSHA loads; `make_solution.py --zip FILE` writes the complete archive. OpenSHA's build information and the simulated annealing's progress, which its own solutions also carry, describe how OpenSHA made them and are not written. Before writing, it checks that the rates and the rupture set describe the same ruptures, and stops otherwise: the same number, and, for every rupture cleaning kept, the same fault sections (`indices.csv` against the rupture's slip-rate rows, row r being section r) and a magnitude (`properties.csv`) inside its bin; all 18,243 kept ruptures pass both at 2e4, and all 204,538 at 211,000 ruptures. The same script writes any other rate vector as a solution, the posterior mean or a representative (`--column mean`, `--column rep_07`). Each run writes `<out>.npz`, holding traces, predictions and (with draws stored) per-rate mean, sd, quantiles and R̂; `<out>_samples.npy`, all draws as float32 with shape (chains, draws, n), unless `--no-samples` is given; and `<out>_stream.npz`, running summaries kept during the run, written either way. The running summaries hold, per rupture: per-chain, per-half sums of the clipped log rate and its square (split R̂, exactly), per-chain batch means (effective draws), counts above 1e-5 and 1e-4, sums of the rate and of its part below the detection scale, and a log-rate histogram in 0.01-decade bins (`--hist-width`; quantiles and modes), saved compressed; and, per draw, the subsection and bin totals. They match the draw-based values exactly, and the quantiles to within the bin width (typically 0.001–0.004 decades). From them `make_tables.py` (which the notebook runs) always writes rate vectors (`rate_vectors.csv` and `.npz`, in each prior's folder; for runs made before running summaries existed, from their stored draws): one row per rupture of the original problem, indexed as the columns of `cru_A`, with penalised ruptures flagged and set to 0, giving the posterior mean, median, mode, and the 2.275%, 15.865%, 84.135% and 97.725% values. The mode is defined on a log scale: 0 if a rate below 1e-16 has probability at least 0.5, otherwise the peak of the smoothed log-rate density above 1e-16; for weakly constrained ruptures it is poorly defined, since that density is flat over the prior's range, and under spike-and-slab and θ most modes are 0. The notebook runs without draws (`--no-samples`): the convergence checks, tables and summary figure come from the running summaries. Representative draws for hazard (`REPRESENTATIVES`, 20 by default; 0 turns them off): each run keeps 1,000 whole draws, with or without `--no-samples` (`gibbs.py --keep-draws`, recording only: `<out>_kept.npy`, float32, one row per draw, and `<out>_kept_index.npy`, each draw's chain and record number), and `select_draws.py` picks weighted representatives from the replicates' pooled kept draws. It describes each draw by its participation rate per subsection and magnitude bin, clusters the logarithms of those rates (k-means on their principal components), takes the kept draw nearest each cluster centre, and calibrates the weights so the weighted mean participation rates come close to the exact posterior means. The representatives are columns `rep_01`, `rep_02`, ... of `rate_vectors.csv`; `representatives.csv` gives each one's weight, cluster share, run, chain, record, χ² and total rate; `rate_vectors.npz` gains the pooled kept draws (`kept`, `kept_index`), `rep` and `rep_weight`; and `select_report.txt` compares 10, 20 and 40 representatives with the full posterior (means of the total, bin and subsection totals against the exact means; 16%, 50% and 84% points against every recorded draw; each representative's χ² and total rate against the posterior's 1–99% range; the weights). The weights are quadrature weights for the posterior as a whole, and the `mean` column remains the exact source for mean hazard, which is linear in the rates. `plot_rate_magnitude.py`, run by the notebook after the summary figure, plots log10 of each rupture's posterior mean rate (the rate vectors' `mean` column) against its magnitude bin, with each bin's target total R_j and its total of the means, and puts means below 10^`FLOOR`, including 0, in a band at the bottom (`fig_rate_magnitude.png`, with a per-bin table in `fig_rate_magnitude.txt`). The table also counts each bin's ruptures by decade of mean rate: below 10^`FLOOR`, including 0 (the bottom band), then each decade from 10^`FLOOR` up to 1e-2, and at or above 1e-2, so that every rupture is counted once ("Summarizing the draws"). Above it a shorter panel shows each bin's total over its target, with the band within one uncertainty of the target, at the level the likelihood gives the bins, and the posterior 16–84% range of each bin total as a bar, taken from the predictions the runs recorded (`main_rep0.npz`, ...; the table adds the 16%, 50% and 84% points, and the total rate's): on the rate axis below, a spread of ±10% is about as tall as the dot. In the 211,000-rupture reference run the target lies inside that range for 12 of the 18 bins; the six bins from M8.0 sit above theirs. Several rate vectors give panels side by side. A rupture's magnitude is known only to its bin: the bin rows are counted up from 6.80–6.90 in steps of 0.1 in their original order (`--lowest-bin`, `--bin-width`), an order the script checks against rupture sizes (on the corrected inputs the median number of subsections per rupture rises from 3 in the lowest bin to 113 in the highest, rank correlation +1.00). `plot_slip_rates.py`, run by the notebook after it, plots each slip-rate section's target against the mean model's prediction (G times the rate vectors' `mean` column, which is also the posterior mean of the prediction), on a linear and on a log scale (`fig_slip_rates.png`, with a section table and a row table in `fig_slip_rates.txt`). Each section's mean prediction over its subsections is a dot, in one of the reference palette's eight colours cycled along the targets so that neighbouring sections never share one (`cru_common.neighbour_colours`; the paleo figure colours its rows the same way, and sections sharing a target stay stacked at it), with its subsections in grey behind it and the posterior 16–84% range of the section mean as a bar, taken from the predictions the runs recorded (the section table adds the 16%, 50% and 84% points). The bar is the model's spread, not the measurement's: a section mean's posterior sd is typically well under one measurement uncertainty (a median 0.38 of it in quick runs at 2e4), so a target outside its bar is no misfit in itself, and the grey band remains the test; in the 211,000-rupture reference run the target lies inside the bar for 37% of the sections, and 289 sections have a 16% point of exactly 0 (the section without events in at least 16% of the draws). The rows are divided by their uncertainties in the inputs, which do not give the uncertainties themselves, so both axes are in units of each section's measurement uncertainty (any √k weighting is undone for display): a grey band marks predictions within one uncertainty of their targets, and the sections furthest from their targets in those units are labelled with their original rows. The subtitle gives the mean model's χ² over the slip-rate rows as the likelihood weights them (for the best fit 17.0 under the defaults, 14.1 with the MFD bins counted as two measurements and 38.3 counting each subsection, as cleaning reports) and the share of sections within one and two uncertainties; several rate vectors give one row of panels each. `plot_paleo_rates.py`, run by the notebook after it, does the same for the paleo rows (every row in neither the slip-rate nor the magnitude-bin group whose target is positive, unless `--paleo-block` names them; rows with a target of 0 are zero-forcing penalty rows, left out with a note) on log-log axes (`fig_paleo_rates.png`, with a per-row table in `fig_paleo_rates.txt`): each row's target against the mean model's prediction, with the posterior 16–84% range of the prediction as a vertical bar, taken from the predictions recorded by the runs next to the rate vectors (`main_rep0.npz`, ...). In units of each row's uncertainty a point only moves along the 1:1 line, so its distance from the line is the ratio of prediction to target, and since a target in these units is one over its relative uncertainty, the best-measured sites lie furthest right; the shaded bands mark predictions within one and two uncertainties, and the rows furthest from their targets are labelled. Several rate vectors give one panel each. `compare_runs.py --a <run> --b <run>` compares two runs rate by rate and draws a figure (`fig_compare.png`). `compare_priors.py <run> <run> ...` produces the tables comparing priors (spike-and-slab against θ, say): fit, bin totals against targets, sparsity, convergence, and changes in the robust products.

For large configurations the draws dominate disk use: at 211,000 ruptures and the very conservative preset, about 68 GB per spike-and-slab or θ run. Without draws (`--no-samples`, as the notebook runs), each run keeps only its running summaries: compressed on disk to a small fraction of the draws, though the finer histogram needs about 1.4 GB of memory during the run at that size.

## Before routine use

What to test and watch for before routine use is in a separate document, `TESTING.md`: whether a new setup reproduces these results, whether the runs converge (including the very conservative preset), whether the conclusions survive the modelling choices, whether the workflow will scale, a checklist for every run, and the known weak spots.

## Open items

- **Spike-and-slab's long run:** at 211,000 ruptures the very conservative run of the configuration adopted in late October 2026 is done, and passes with eight slowly mixing ruptures (R̂ at most 1.022, 1.9% of the total rate; "The reference run at 211,000 ruptures"). On the corrected 2e4 inputs the very conservative run under the defaults is still to do. With `'two-measurements'` it has run, in 1 h 20 min on the M2, and passed in all three replicates: 0.2% of its ruptures (35–42) had R̂ above the noise baseline, the worst 1.009–1.013, and the replicates agreed rupture by rupture as expected (sd 0.991–0.999). The very conservative θ run (counting each subsection) also met the convergence targets (χ²'s R̂ on the 1.005 line in one replicate), but 4.5–4.7% of its ruptures had R̂ above the noise baseline, the worst 1.35–1.38, with errors that hold across replicates: a minority of ruptures that switch on and off stays slow there. Moves that exchange the active role between alternative ruptures remain open; a lower swap threshold would give seven of the 211,000-rupture reference run's eight slow ruptures a partner.
- **The fit shortfall at 211,000 ruptures:** the paleo rows are fitted at 2.2 per measurement, and the mean model sits about 1 uncertainty below the paleo targets and 0.4–0.9 below the slip-rate targets, under every prior variant tried ("Fits and the prior's lower end"). It comes from the bins counting events while the posterior takes each bin's average magnitude, and from the paleo rows' tension with the rest of the data. Whether to weight the paleo rows more against the bins (their uncertainties, in the inputs) is a modelling decision.
- **Subsection event rates at 211,000 ruptures:** they depend on the slab's shape (the square-root slab's a median 0.62 of the default's, with the same fit), so they are partly the prior's. The square-root slab was dropped as the cross-check at the end of October 2026, so this stays a caveat on those rates rather than a check run for each result set.
- **Dispersed starts:** chains started from simulated-annealing solutions or perturbed best fits (`gibbs.py --init`, from the command line), still untested (see TESTING.md). The 211,000-rupture reference run's 48 chains all started from the best fit, so their agreement cannot rule out good fits that this start never reaches.
- **Representative draws:** in the first test (conservative preset, spike-and-slab, 2 × 1,000 kept draws, counting each subsection), 20 representatives matched the posterior mean of the total rate (to 0.0%) and stayed inside the posterior's χ² and total-rate ranges with no zero weight, but the worst bin total was 4.6% off its exact mean, 76% of subsection means were within 10%, and 68–79% of the subsections' 16%, 50% and 84% points were within 15%, short of the proposed 2%, 90% and 90%. Forty representatives did little better (2.4%, 79%, 74–81%), and the quick preset gave much the same, so run length is not the limit. About a third of the subsections have totals whose coefficient of variation exceeds 0.45 (median 0.30), more than 20 draws can average to within 10%. With one measurement per slip-rate section (quick preset), 20 representatives matched 61% of subsection means within 10% under spike-and-slab and 53% under θ (40: 66% and 63%). With the MFD bins counted as two measurements their posterior spread triples, to about 30% per bin, and 20 representatives matched 58% of subsection means within 10% and the worst bin to 16% (quick preset, spike-and-slab). Revisit these tolerances with the hazard team, or calibrate the weights on the subsection and bin totals directly. At 211,000 ruptures, from the very conservative reference run, 100 representatives still fall short on the subsections (68–75% of their 16%, 50% and 84% points within 15%), one weight calibrates to 0, and the check that every representative lies inside the posterior's 1–99% ranges fails by chance most of the time for that many ("The reference run at 211,000 ruptures").
- **Evidence:** done for single-box floors, where it keeps rising as the floor drops; not done for spike-and-slab or θ (if wanted, from a few tempered runs by hand).
- **Hope: Conway's slip-rate uncertainty** (left as given, October 2026): the fault model gives 15.8 ± 0.2 mm/yr, and the inputs 12.64 mm/yr (every section's rate scaled by 0.8, its standard deviation not) with twice the standard deviation as the uncertainty, as for every section: a target of 31.6 uncertainties (rows 151–157 at 2e4, 719–725 at 211k), where the next tightest section, Clarence: Southwest, has 6.7 and the median is 1.6 (0.8 at 211k). Every solution holds the section at its target (the best fit, the posterior and simulated annealing at 12.65–12.74 mm/yr). A best fit with the uncertainty widened tenfold puts it at about 15.7 mm/yr, raises the paleo site on it (row 860, and 8916 at 211k) from 0.45 to about 0.6 of its target, and moves nothing else noticeably (bin totals under 0.5%, the total rate 0.05%, no other section by more than 0.16 of its uncertainty). Worth checking upstream whether 0.2 mm/yr is intended.
- **Sparse sections:** check the sections whose totals collapse against the fault model; the slip-rate figure is a first look. With one measurement per section, in quick runs, spike-and-slab and θ predict 18 sections (72 subsections) at under a quarter of their targets, all with targets only 0.6–2.4 uncertainties above zero, so each section's mean prediction stays within 2.3 uncertainties of its target and the data do not rule out near-zero slip there (counting each subsection, 41–43 subsections, within 1.42). Their χ² now sits 1 sd above the fit check, so the earlier worry that it fell well below, pointing at generous uncertainties, is gone. Paleo site row 853 is the same story at a site: the best fit nearly matches it, but both priors predict about 0 there in most draws (`fig_paleo_rates.png`).
- **Slip-rate sections:** detected as runs of copies (68 on the corrected inputs). A mapping of subsections to sections from the fault model (`SLIP_SECTIONS = 'file.txt'`) would settle the 32 sections that share their target with another and the 6 neighbouring pairs with identical targets that share no rupture, and should be used for new inputs if the detection looks doubtful.
- **Geodetic slip rates:** rows that vary from subsection to subsection are left as given, each its own measurement. If a geodetic model's errors are correlated along a fault (they come from a smooth fit), each row counts as more independent information than it carries; that would need a covariance, or an effective number of measurements per fault.
- **Uniform slip along a section:** the fit check assumes each section's true slip rate is uniform along it; real variation along a section adds to the expected χ², and the weighting tolerates it at √k times the measurement's uncertainty per subsection. Whether that tolerance suits the fault model is worth a look.
- **Scaling to 5e5:** filtered swap moves, with timing tests at about 1e5 ruptures first. Cleaning already works on the sparse G throughout (53 s at 211,000 ruptures; TESTING.md, "Before 5e5"), running summaries already allow runs without stored draws (`--no-samples`, as the notebook runs), and the sampler's per-draw bookkeeping works on each chain's nonzero rates, so it no longer grows with the number of ruptures (TESTING.md, "Run lengths, timing and storage").
- **Extremal bounds:** prior-free bounds for chosen functionals.
- **MFD covariance:** the bins' uncertainty is set by one multiplier (`MFD_UNCERTAINTY`), which keeps them independent. A correlated covariance, the two measurements' within-branch uncertainty projected onto the bins plus a small model error per bin, would let the MFD shift and tilt as a whole while staying smooth. It needs whitened bin rows, dense across the bins, which the per-bin priors and bin targets would have to be adapted to ("MFD uncertainty").
- **Optional refinement:** a similarity prior between overlapping ruptures.
- **HMC and evidence scripts:** `rhmc.py` (log-space HMC, the independent cross-check described above) and `evidence.py` are set aside from the code set, in `cru_retired_scripts.zip`. An HMC cross-check of the current priors would bring `rhmc.py` back; evidence comparisons can be done by hand with a few tempered runs.
- **NUTS:** the cross-check from the design notes was not run, since there is no JAX in the sandbox. Log-space HMC already uses NUTS's transform and agrees with Gibbs.
